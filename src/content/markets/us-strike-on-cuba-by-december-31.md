## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Cuba's accelerating military cooperation with Russia and China, particularly reports of Chinese intelligence facilities on the island and Russian naval visits to Havana. The Díaz-Canel government's continued imprisonment of protesters from the July 2021 demonstrations, combined with potential mass migration events that could trigger a humanitarian crisis in Florida, creates domestic political pressure for decisive action. Any incident involving U.S. personnel at Guantanamo Bay or aggressive Cuban interference with U.S. maritime operations in the Straits of Florida could provide a immediate casus belli. The 2024 and 2026 election cycles amplify these pressures, with Florida's crucial electoral votes incentivizing strong responses to Cuban provocations.

The bear case recognizes that direct military strikes carry enormous diplomatic and political costs with minimal strategic benefit. Cuba poses no immediate military threat to U.S. territory, and strikes would alienate Latin American allies while providing propaganda victories to Russia and China. The Pentagon's focus remains on Pacific deterrence and European security, making a Caribbean military operation a diversion from stated strategic priorities. Historical precedent strongly favors containment over intervention—no U.S. administration has launched strikes on Cuba since the Cold War despite numerous provocations. Economic pressure and diplomatic isolation remain the preferred policy tools across both parties.

Key catalysts include the 2025 legislative calendar when the Republican-controlled Congress may push for更aggressive Cuba policy, Cuba's May Day demonstrations that could turn violent and prompt refugee flows, and the June 2026 midterm primary season when candidates may advocate harder lines. Watch for any agreements between Havana and Beijing regarding permanent military installations, incidents involving the U.S. Coast Guard intercepting Cuban or Russian vessels, and State Department travel warnings for Guantanamo Bay personnel. The market will likely remain volatile around anniversary dates of historical U.S.-Cuba conflicts and during hurricane season when infrastructure collapse could create intervention scenarios.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Would a limited strike on Cuban military facilities trigger the full resolution criteria, or does this require a broader military campaign?

Any intentional U.S. military attack on Cuban territory, including drone strikes on specific installations or cruise missile attacks on intelligence facilities, would likely resolve this market as YES. The market language doesn't specify scale or duration.

### How do potential changes in Cuban leadership before 2026 affect the probability of U.S. military action?

A succession crisis or coup attempt could either decrease strike probability if a more U.S.-friendly government emerges, or increase it dramatically if instability leads to loose control of weapons systems, refugee surges, or Russian/Chinese intervention to protect their ally.

### What role does the status of Guantanamo Bay play in this market's resolution?

Any Cuban military action against Guantanamo Bay would almost certainly trigger U.S. strikes in response, making the base a critical flashpoint to monitor. However, U.S. defensive actions solely within the base perimeter would not constitute a strike "on Cuba" for market purposes.
