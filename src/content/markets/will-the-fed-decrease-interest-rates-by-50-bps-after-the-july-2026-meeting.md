## Market Analysis

**Bull Case for a Large Cut:** A financial crisis, major geopolitical shock, or sudden economic collapse could force the Fed into aggressive emergency easing. Historical precedents include March 2020 (pandemic) and 2008 (financial crisis) when the Fed slashed rates by 50+ bps between scheduled meetings or implemented outsized cuts. If inflation returns to the 2% target by late 2025 but unemployment spikes to 6-7% by mid-2026, the Fed would have room to cut aggressively. A credit crunch, commercial real estate collapse, or sovereign debt crisis in a major economy could trigger contagion requiring immediate Fed action. The July 2026 FOMC meeting would need to follow a deterioration visible in Q2 2026 employment reports (April, May, June NFP releases) and Q1 2026 GDP data (released late April 2026).

**Bear Case Against Large Cuts:** The current macroeconomic trajectory suggests a soft landing with inflation moderating gradually. The Fed has signaled a preference for measured 25 bps adjustments rather than shock moves, and Chair Powell's recent communications emphasize data dependency over dramatic pivots. For this market to pay out, conditions would need to deteriorate so severely that a 50+ bps cut becomes necessary—a scenario that typically only occurs during recessions or financial panics. With the Fed likely completing its current cutting cycle by early 2025 and maintaining rates in a neutral range, the baseline expectation is stability through 2026 absent black swan events.

**Key Catalysts to Monitor:** Watch the FOMC meetings in January, March, May, and June 2026 for language shifts toward urgency. Monthly CPI and PCE inflation readings through 2025-2026 will determine if the Fed has policy space to cut aggressively—if inflation remains above 3% in 2026, emergency cuts become nearly impossible. The employment situation reports for April, May, and June 2026 (released first Friday of each subsequent month) will be critical; consecutive prints showing 300K+ job losses would dramatically increase odds. Financial stability indicators including credit spreads, bank stress test results (June 2026), and the Q1 2026 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey will signal brewing crises before they force Fed action.

### Does this market resolve YES if the Fed cuts rates by exactly 50 bps at the July 2026 meeting, or does it need to be more than 50 bps?

The market requires "50+ bps" which means 50 basis points or greater would trigger a YES resolution. A cut of exactly 50 bps or, say, 75 bps would both qualify.

### Would an emergency inter-meeting rate cut before July 2026 affect this market's resolution?

No, this market specifically resolves based on rate changes announced after the July 2026 FOMC meeting. Any emergency cuts before that date, even if larger than 50 bps, would not cause a YES resolution.

### What's the difference between the Fed's typical cutting pace and the emergency scenario this market is pricing?

The Fed's standard practice since the 1990s has been 25 bps adjustments at scheduled meetings, allowing time for data assessment. A 50+ bps cut signals acute economic distress requiring immediate aggressive action, historically reserved for financial crises or severe recessions.
