## Market Analysis

**Bull Case for a Large Cut:** A significant economic deterioration between now and mid-2026 could force the Fed's hand. This would require either a major financial crisis, a sharp recession with unemployment spiking above 6%, or a rapid disinflation where core PCE falls well below the 2% target while growth collapses. Historical precedent shows the Fed has deployed 50bp cuts during acute crises like 2001, 2007-2008, and March 2020. If inflation successfully normalizes to target by late 2024 or early 2025, but then economic activity contracts sharply in 2026, the FOMC could justify emergency-style easing. Leading indicators such as inverted yield curves, deteriorating PMI readings below 45, or consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth would signal this scenario emerging.

**Bear Case Against Large Cuts:** The current economic baseline makes a 50bp cut extremely improbable. The Fed has signaled its preference for measured 25bp adjustments during normal policy recalibration. Even if the June 2026 meeting follows a cutting cycle that began earlier, the pace would likely be gradual unless confronting acute crisis conditions. Unemployment would need to surge dramatically and unexpectedly, which seems unlikely given current labor market resilience. The November 2024, January 2025, and March 2025 FOMC meetings will establish the rate trajectory—if cuts begin in that window and proceed at 25bp increments, the June 2026 decision would represent just another step in normalization rather than emergency action.

**Key Catalysts to Monitor:** Monthly CPI and PCE inflation reports through 2025 will determine whether the Fed achieves its inflation mandate and can focus purely on employment concerns. The December 2024 and March 2025 Summary of Economic Projections will show how the Fed views the neutral rate and appropriate policy path. Non-farm payroll reports starting in early 2025 become critical—sequential prints below 100k with rising unemployment claims would shift recession probabilities. The FOMC's May 2026 meeting (likely May 6-7) immediately preceding the June decision will provide crucial guidance on whether conditions warrant aggressive action. Credit spreads, financial conditions indices, and regional Fed surveys through Q1 2026 would need to show severe stress for this market to reprice meaningfully higher.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What historical precedent exists for 50+ basis point cuts outside of major crises?

The Fed has rarely cut by 50bp or more except during severe recessions or financial emergencies like 2001, 2008, and 2020. Routine policy normalization typically involves 25bp moves, making such large cuts signal material economic distress.

### Could the Fed cut by 25bp at multiple consecutive meetings leading up to June 2026 and still make a 50bp cut then?

Yes, but it would require conditions deteriorating significantly between the May and June 2026 meetings despite prior easing—essentially an unexpected shock requiring emergency response rather than planned policy adjustment.

### How would this market resolve if the Fed cuts by exactly 50 basis points at the June meeting?

The market specifically requires a decrease of 50+ basis points, so a cut of exactly 50bp would resolve YES, while 49bp or less would resolve NO regardless of how close it came to the threshold.
