The market is essentially a coin flip at 48% YES, reflecting genuine uncertainty about whether Elon Musk will take SpaceX public within the next 18 months despite mounting pressure from investors and market conditions. This matters because SpaceX is valued at approximately $180 billion in private markets (as of late 2024), making it one of the most valuable private companies globally—an IPO would be one of the largest debuts in tech history and a major signal about Musk's strategic priorities amid his Tesla and X commitments.

Additionally, SpaceX's primary customer remains the U.S. government, which creates regulatory and reputational constraints that may make Musk prefer remaining private.

Key catalysts to watch: any major Starshield contract awards in early 2025, Starlink's satellite internet subscriber growth numbers (targeting 20+ million by 2026), potential Fed rate cuts or rallies in high-growth equities, any statements from Musk on capital allocation at Tesla shareholder meetings, and geopolitical developments that could accelerate government spending on domestic launch capabilities. ### Why hasn't SpaceX IPO'd already given its massive valuation?
