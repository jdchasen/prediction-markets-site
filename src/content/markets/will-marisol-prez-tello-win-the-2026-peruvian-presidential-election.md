## Market Analysis

Pérez Tello, who served as Minister of Justice under Pedro Pablo Kuczynski (2016-2017), represents the moderate center-right but faces significant structural challenges. The bull case rests on Peru's extreme voter volatility—the country has elected political outsiders in recent cycles, including Pedro Castillo in 2021 who rose from single digits to victory within months. If established politicians falter amid ongoing corruption scandals and Pérez Tello successfully positions herself as a clean technocrat with ministerial experience, she could benefit from late-stage consolidation of anti-populist voters. Her legal background and previous government service provide credibility that other candidates lack in a country where institutional trust has collapsed.

The bear case is considerably stronger. Recent polling shows no significant support for Pérez Tello, and she lacks the party infrastructure, name recognition, or funding that Peru's top-tier candidates command. The 2026 field is already crowded with more prominent figures, including potential candidates from Popular Force, Popular Action, and various left-wing movements. Peru's electoral system requires either a first-round majority or a runoff between the top two candidates, making it extremely difficult for candidates polling below 5% to remain viable. Presidential campaigns typically consolidate around 6-8 serious contenders by late 2025, and Pérez Tello has shown no evidence of building the grassroots movement or media presence necessary to break through.

Key catalysts include party primary selections expected between November 2025 and January 2026, which will clarify the field. The first credible national polls typically emerge in October 2025, six months before the election. Traders should monitor whether Pérez Tello secures a major party nomination, attracts significant campaign funding, or registers above 3% in any reputable poll—all prerequisites for viability. Congressional elections occur simultaneously with the presidential vote, so candidate performance will also depend on their ability to build competitive legislative slates across Peru's electoral districts.
