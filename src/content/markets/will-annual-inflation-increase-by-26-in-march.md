## Market Analysis

The bear case for inflation staying at or below 2.6% requires an economic scenario that seems increasingly unlikely: a significant recession that crushes demand, rapid productivity gains from AI adoption that lower costs faster than anticipated, or a persistent strengthening of the dollar that makes imports dramatically cheaper. Housing costs, which make up roughly one-third of CPI and have shown stickiness even as rent growth moderates, would need to decline substantially. Additionally, tight labor markets would need to loosen considerably, with the unemployment rate rising well above current levels to put downward pressure on wage growth. The bull case—that inflation remains elevated above 2.6%—rests on structural factors including deglobalization trends increasing supply chain costs, continued government spending fueling demand, sticky service sector inflation, and potential tariff implementations that could add 0.5-1.0 percentage points to headline CPI according to various economic models.

Key catalysts to watch include the February 2026 CPI release (typically mid-March, about three weeks before market expiry), which will provide the clearest signal of the trend heading into March. The January and February FOMC meetings will indicate whether the Fed sees progress stalling on its 2% target. Monthly jobs reports through early 2026, particularly the February NFP data released in early March, will show whether wage pressures are accelerating or moderating. Any geopolitical developments affecting oil prices could swing headline inflation significantly, as energy comprises about 7% of the CPI basket.

The current 2.1% probability appears rationally priced given that annual CPI hasn't touched 2.6% or lower since early 2021, and the path from current levels around 3-4% to 2.6% in roughly 14 months would require sustained disinflation. Traders should monitor core PCE (the Fed's preferred measure), shelter inflation components in monthly CPI releases, and any shifts in FOMC dot plot projections. The December 2025 CPI release in mid-January 2026 will be particularly crucial as it sets the baseline for the final two-month stretch before market resolution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What exactly does this market resolve on—headline CPI or core CPI?

This market resolves on the headline year-over-year CPI inflation rate for March 2026, which includes volatile food and energy prices, not the core measure that excludes them.

### How much would oil prices need to drop to potentially push inflation under 2.6%?

Since energy is roughly 7% of CPI, a sustained 40-50% decline in oil prices from current levels combined with cooling in other categories could contribute meaningfully, though it would require simultaneous disinflation across housing and services to reach the threshold.

### Why is the market expiry date April 10, 2026, if it's measuring March inflation?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics typically releases monthly CPI data around the middle of the following month, so March 2026 CPI data would be published in mid-April, allowing the market to resolve after the official data release.
