## Market Analysis

The market is pricing an extremely low probability that Trump attempts to remove Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell before the end of March 2026, reflecting both the structural constraints on presidential power and current political dynamics. This matters because it captures market expectations about whether Trump will escalate his public criticism of the Fed into formal removal action—a question that has dominated financial and political discourse since his 2024 election victory. The razor-thin odds suggest traders believe either institutional safeguards will hold or Trump will pursue alternative strategies for influencing monetary policy rather than direct confrontation.

The bull case for removal hinges on Trump's demonstrated willingness to confront institutional norms and his deep frustration with Powell's rate-hiking cycle, which Trump has blamed for slowing growth. Trump has repeatedly criticized Powell publicly and called for his removal in previous statements. If the economy enters recession in late 2025 or early 2026—particularly if unemployment spikes before the March 31 deadline—Trump could view firing Powell as both politically advantageous and a solution to stimulate growth ahead of the 2026 midterms. Additionally, a major market correction or banking stress could create political cover for such an action. The key catalyst to watch is Q4 2025 economic data, especially the employment reports from December 2025 and January 2026.

The bear case is substantially stronger: the Federal Reserve Chair serves a 14-year term with statutory removal protections that require "cause" as defined by law—corruption, incompetence, or neglect of duty. Courts have consistently upheld these protections, and removing Powell would likely trigger immediate litigation that Trump would probably lose. Powell still maintains support from key Republican senators and the broader financial establishment, making Senate confirmation of a replacement unlikely. Trump has alternative tools: he can appoint new Fed governors (with Senate confirmation) and leverage the bully pulpit, which he has already done extensively. The political cost of attempting an arguably unconstitutional removal—potentially destabilizing markets and the dollar—likely exceeds any benefit, especially with midterm elections in November 2026 where economic performance matters more than Powell's removal.

The market's 0.5% pricing reflects appropriate skepticism. Watch for three indicators: (1) deteriorating employment data in Q4 2025 that Trump could credibly blame on Powell, (2) any Trump statement explicitly threatening removal rather than criticism, and (3) market stress that creates political pressure for scapegoating. The March 31 deadline is quite tight—even if Trump initiates removal action, litigation and procedural requirements would likely extend well past this date. Barring a severe economic shock or unprecedented institutional breakdown, this market should remain heavily weighted toward "no."
