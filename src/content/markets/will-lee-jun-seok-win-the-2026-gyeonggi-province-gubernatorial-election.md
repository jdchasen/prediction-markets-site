## Market Analysis

The bear case is overwhelming: Lee was expelled from the People Power Party (PPP) in 2023 following sexual bribery allegations and subsequent legal troubles, fundamentally damaging his political brand. Gyeonggi Province, with over 13 million residents and comprising roughly a quarter of South Korea's population, typically demands major party backing and extensive organizational infrastructure that Lee currently lacks. His attempt to form the Reform Party has gained limited traction, and independent or third-party candidates face severe structural disadvantages in Korean elections. The province has alternated between conservative and progressive governors, but both winners had full backing of their respective major parties. Lee's reputation issues make him toxic to mainstream conservative voters who might otherwise support PPP candidates, while progressive voters have no reason to back him over a Democratic Party nominee.

The bull case requires multiple improbable scenarios aligning: both major parties would need to nominate deeply unpopular candidates, creating an opening for a third option. Lee could potentially rehabilitate his image if legal proceedings conclude favorably or if public opinion shifts to view him as unfairly persecuted by party establishment. A major corruption scandal involving both the PPP and Democratic Party Gyeonggi candidates in 2025-2026 could theoretically create space for an outsider narrative. Lee demonstrated political talent as PPP chairman before his downfall, and if he successfully builds the Reform Party into a credible force over the next two years, he might consolidate anti-establishment sentiment.

Key catalysts include the PPP and Democratic Party primary processes expected in early 2026 (likely March-April), which will determine whether mainstream candidates are vulnerable. Watch for any developments in Lee's legal cases throughout 2025, as full exoneration could shift dynamics. The April 2025 by-elections will test whether Lee's Reform Party can gain any electoral momentum. National approval ratings for President Yoon Suk-yeol and the broader political environment heading into 2026 will determine whether voters are open to alternative candidates outside traditional party structures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why was Lee Jun-seok expelled from the People Power Party and how does this affect his electoral viability?

Lee was expelled in 2023 over allegations involving sexual bribery scandals and subsequent perceived disloyalty to party leadership. This expulsion severed him from the conservative party infrastructure essential for winning Gyeonggi Province and severely damaged his credibility with mainstream voters.

### What would Lee Jun-seok need to accomplish with the Reform Party to become competitive in this race?

The Reform Party would need to demonstrate viability in the April 2025 by-elections, build a robust ground organization across Gyeonggi's 31 cities and counties, and secure significant funding—all while Lee rehabilitates his personal image sufficiently to poll above 30% against major party candidates.

### Has any third-party or independent candidate ever won a Korean provincial governorship in the modern era?

Third-party victories in major Korean provincial races are exceptionally rare, as the political system strongly favors the two main parties through organizational advantages, funding structures, and voter behavior patterns that typically consolidate around major party nominees.
