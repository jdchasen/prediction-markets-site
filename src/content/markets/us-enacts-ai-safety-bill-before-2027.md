The bear case emphasizes historical Congressional gridlock on technology regulation, massive lobbying expenditures from major AI companies opposing binding safety requirements, and definitional challenges around what constitutes "AI safety" versus broader tech policy. The tech industry successfully watered down or blocked previous attempts at algorithmic accountability legislation. Republicans may prioritize AI competitiveness over safety mandates, viewing regulation as hampering U.S.-China rivalry. The 2024 presidential election outcome significantly impacts regulatory philosophy—a administration focused on deregulation would likely discourage Congressional action. Additionally, the Supreme Court's recent curtailment of agency power in *Loper Bright* creates uncertainty about enforcement mechanisms, potentially deterring legislative efforts.

Traders should monitor the Senate Commerce and House Energy & Commerce committees' 2025 legislative calendars, track lobbying disclosure reports showing AI company spending patterns, and watch for bipartisan bill co-sponsorship emerging in Q1 2025. Conversely, if major bills stall in committee by mid-2025 without advancing to floor votes, the probability should decline significantly given the typical legislative timeline requirements.
