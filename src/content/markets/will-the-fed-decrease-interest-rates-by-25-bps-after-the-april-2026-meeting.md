## Market Analysis

The bear case for a rate cut centers on structural inflation remaining above the Fed's 2% target through 2025-2026. If core PCE inflation stays elevated in the 2.5-3% range, labor markets remain tight with unemployment below 4%, and wage growth continues above 4% annually, the Fed would lack justification for cuts. Geopolitical pressures, fiscal expansion, or renewed supply chain disruptions could keep inflation sticky. The March 2026 FOMC meeting (approximately March 17-18) and subsequent economic projections would telegraph the Committee's stance ahead of April. Strong Q1 2026 GDP growth above 2.5% combined with robust January and February 2026 NFP prints exceeding 200K would reinforce the case against easing.

The bull case requires a significant economic deterioration between now and early 2026. A recession beginning in late 2025, with unemployment rising above 5% and monthly job losses, would force the Fed's hand. Disinflation accelerating through 2025, with core CPI falling to 2% or below by Q4 2025 (December 2025 CPI release on January 14, 2026), would provide the necessary room. Financial market stress—credit spreads widening, corporate distress, or a banking sector crisis—could compel emergency easing. The Fed typically conducts eight meetings per year, meaning the committee would have multiple opportunities through 2025 to signal this policy shift, particularly at the December 2025 meeting where updated Summary of Economic Projections would preview 2026 rate paths.

Traders should monitor the 2025 FOMC meeting schedule closely, particularly the dot plot projections released quarterly (June, September, and December 2025). The February 2026 CPI release (mid-March 2026) and February NFP data (early March 2026) will be the final major indicators before any April decision. Watch for the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE, in monthly personal income reports. Any pivot in Fed rhetoric from "higher for longer" to acknowledging downside economic risks would significantly shift these odds upward from their current low baseline.

### Why is the market specifically asking about 25 basis points rather than any rate cut?

The Fed typically moves in 25 bps increments during normal policy adjustments, reserving 50+ bps moves for emergencies. This market isolates the standard cut scenario rather than crisis easing.

### How many rate decisions will the Fed make between now and April 2026, and why does that matter?

The Fed will hold approximately 14 meetings from now until April 2026, providing multiple opportunities to signal policy direction. Markets typically price in cuts months in advance through Fed communications and dot plots.

### What historical precedent exists for Fed rate cuts in April specifically?

April is not part of the Fed's typical quarterly meeting cycle that includes press conferences and economic projections (March, June, September, December), making it a less likely meeting for major policy shifts unless economic conditions clearly warrant action based on prior data.
