The bear case is straightforward: Brazilian presidential elections typically feature well-known figures from major parties, and second-place finishers almost always have established national brands, congressional blocs, or gubernatorial experience. Current polling for 2026 shows former presidents and sitting governors dominating voter preference, with combined support for the top-tier candidates exceeding 70%. Santos would need to overcome Brazil's party-centric media access rules, which heavily favor candidates from larger parliamentary coalitions. The country's political fragmentation means even minor candidates struggle to break through without significant institutional backing or celebrity status. Historical data shows that candidates polling below 10% eighteen months before election day rarely finish in the top two.

### How does Brazil's two-round electoral system affect the strategy for finishing second versus winning outright?
