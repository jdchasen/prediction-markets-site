## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on Santos potentially consolidating support from fragmented opposition groups seeking an alternative to both Lula's PT party and traditional right-wing figures. If he can position himself as a fresh centrist or center-right option while building name recognition through aggressive early campaigning, he could capitalize on voter fatigue with establishment politicians. Brazil's multi-party system often produces surprise second-place finishers who emerge from relative obscurity as coalitions shift—a phenomenon that benefited candidates like Ciro Gomes in past cycles. Santos would need to secure backing from major party machinery or wealthy donors by late 2025 to have credible shot at breaking through.

The bear case is considerably stronger given current political dynamics. Santos lacks the national profile, party infrastructure, and polling presence of likely contenders including Jair Bolsonaro (if eligible), São Paulo Governor Tarcísio de Freitas, or other established figures from PSDB, MDB, or União Brasil. Brazilian presidential elections typically feature well-known governors, former presidents, or politicians with decades of federal experience. First-round results historically favor candidates who poll above 15% by mid-election year, and there's no indication Santos approaches that threshold. The October 2025 party convention season will likely clarify which candidates have realistic paths to the runoff.

Key catalysts include April 2025 party affiliation deadlines, when serious candidates must declare intentions, and the TSE's final candidate registration in August 2026. Watch for Datafolha and Ipespe polling throughout 2025—candidates who haven't broken into double digits by January 2026 rarely place second. Bolsonaro's potential ineligibility ruling could reshape the race, but even in that scenario, established politicians like de Freitas or Ronaldo Caiado appear better positioned to inherit right-wing support.
