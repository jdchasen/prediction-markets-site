## Market Analysis

The bull case hinges on usage rate expansion. If either Doncic or Irving sustains a season-ending injury, Brunson would immediately become the primary offensive engine for a contender, potentially generating 25+ shot attempts per game—the threshold typically required for scoring titles. His career-high 24.3 PPG in 2023–24 came with a 29.1% usage rate; at 32–33% usage (achievable in an injury scenario), he could realistically approach 28–30 PPG. Additionally, if the Mavericks pivot to a smaller lineups strategy emphasizing pace-and-space basketball, Brunson's efficiency and volume could both increase significantly. His 48.8% true shooting last season demonstrates he can score efficiently at scale.

The bear case is structural. Brunson has never led the league in scoring and shares a backcourt with Irving, one of the league's most prolific scorers. Even accounting for injury, the Mavericks employ Doncic as a ball-dominant wing, creating a three-headed monster that naturally limits any single player's offensive load. Historically, scoring titles go to high-volume players on non-contenders or offensive focal points (think Luka, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, or LeBron). Brunson's assist-to-scoring ratio (1.8 AST per game last season) positions him as a facilitator-scorer hybrid rather than a pure volume scorer. The 2025–26 Western Conference also features rising scorers like Anthony Davis, Damian Lillard, and SGA, all with stronger claims to high-volume roles.

Watch for roster moves before the October 2025 training camp—specifically, whether the Mavericks trade Irving or add another scoring perimeter player. Monitor Brunson's usage rate and shot attempts during the 2024–25 season as a baseline; if he reaches 25+ PPG on a 30%+ usage rate while Doncic and Irving both stay healthy, the probability would justifiably tick upward. Injury reports in September 2025 will be the most immediate catalyst, as a Doncic absence could push odds to 5–8% overnight.
