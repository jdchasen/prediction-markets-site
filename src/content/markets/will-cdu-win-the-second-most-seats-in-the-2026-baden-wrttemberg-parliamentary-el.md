## Market Analysis

The bear case dominates here: Baden-Württemberg has been governed by the Greens since 2011, with Minister-President Winfried Kretschmann maintaining extraordinary personal popularity ratings above 60%. The Greens won 32.6% in the 2021 state election while CDU captured 24.1%, finishing second but far behind. More critically, the Social Democrats (SPD) took 11.0% and the far-right AfD secured 9.7% in 2021. For CDU to lose second place, they would need to fall behind either the SPD or AfD—a dramatic collapse given their traditional 20-25% baseline support. Recent federal polling shows CDU/CSU leading nationally, which typically lifts their state-level performance. The question isn't whether CDU wins; it's whether they crater to third or fourth place, requiring a 10+ point drop or surge from competitors.

The bull case centers on AfD's rising trajectory and potential CDU fragmentation. National polls show AfD consistently above 20%, and in neighboring Bavaria and eastern states, they've achieved second-place finishes. If this rightward shift penetrates Baden-Württemberg more deeply than 2021, AfD could leapfrog CDU. Additionally, if Kretschmann retires before 2026 (he'll be 78), the Greens might consolidate opposition votes, squeezing CDU further. The SPD could also benefit from a strong federal coalition performance, though they'd need to more than double their 2021 result. CDU internal divisions over migration policy or a weak chancellor candidate selection could depress turnout among their base.

Key catalysts include Kretschmann's retirement decision (likely announced in late 2025), federal election results in September 2025 that could reshape state-level dynamics, and regional polling throughout 2025 that will show whether AfD momentum translates to this western state. Watch for SPD's Friedrich Merz potentially becoming chancellor, which historically hurts CDU in state elections as governing party fatigue sets in. Any major scandal involving Baden-Württemberg CDU leadership would be critical given the thin margin separating second from third place.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What would need to happen for CDU to actually fall to third or fourth place in Baden-Württemberg?

CDU would need to drop roughly 10 percentage points from their 2021 result of 24.1% while AfD surges above 25% or SPD more than doubles to 24%+. This requires either massive CDU voter defection or extraordinary mobilization by competitors in a traditionally moderate, prosperous state.

### How does Winfried Kretschmann's potential retirement affect this market?

If Kretschmann (age 78 in 2026) retires, the Greens lose their strongest asset but might consolidate center-left voters fearful of CDU gains, potentially squeezing CDU's vote share. His decision, expected in late 2025, could reshape the entire race dynamics and polling trajectories.

### Why is AfD's performance particularly relevant to CDU finishing second?

AfD is CDU's most likely challenger for second place, having grown from 15.1% in 2016 to 9.7% in 2021 despite national trends suggesting higher potential. If AfD reaches their national polling levels of 20%+ in Baden-Württemberg, they would easily surpass CDU's typical 24% and claim second place.
