Historical baseline retirement rates for the majority party hover around 15-20 seats per cycle; hitting 24-27 requires extraordinary conditions. If Republicans maintain reasonable unity and the economy stabilizes, retirements likely flatten toward 15-20. ### What historical retirement rate would support the 24-27 range?

The majority party typically retires 15-20 House members per cycle; 24-27 would represent roughly 30-35% above normal, requiring either severe party distress or a specific crisis like redistricting upheaval (which isn't happening in 2026).

### Why doesn't economic weakness automatically drive this outcome?

Weak economies can paradoxically suppress retirements among majority-party members, as leaving office mid-cycle looks like abandonment; they more often trigger *challengers* within primaries or *flip* seats through general election losses
