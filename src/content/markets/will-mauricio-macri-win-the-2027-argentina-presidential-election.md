## Market Analysis

The market is pricing Macri's return to the presidency at roughly 1-in-26 odds, reflecting deep structural headwinds facing the former president as Argentina heads toward its 2027 general election in late October. This matters now because Argentina's political landscape is crystallizing around Javier Milei's consolidation of power and Peronist fragmentation, leaving limited space for Macri's center-right faction to gain ground over the next three years.

The bull case for Macri rests on three pillars: first, his ability to mobilize upper-class and business-sector voters who may grow weary of Milei's libertarian economics if inflation remains elevated or recession deepens; second, his existing institutional network within the Republican Proposal party (PRO) and proven capacity to govern; third, the historical precedent of comebacks in Argentine politics (Cristina Fernández ran again in 2019 after losing in 2015). If economic conditions deteriorate sharply, Macri could position himself as a "responsible" alternative to both Milei's austerity and Peronism's alleged mismanagement. The bear case is far more compelling: Macri's 2015-2019 presidency is unpopular, associated with currency crises and rising poverty; his 2023 primary performance (finishing third) revealed his diminished base; PRO is now junior partner to Milei's La Libertad Avanza movement, limiting his party's independence; and Argentine voters historically punish one-term former presidents seeking immediate return. Polling shows Macri trailing most potential challengers, and absent a dramatic political realignment, he lacks a clear path to a primary victory, let alone the general election.

Key catalysts to monitor include Argentina's primary elections (PASO) likely scheduled for August 2027, which will test whether Macri can even win his own coalition's nomination; quarterly inflation and employment data through 2026, which will determine whether Milei's austerity agenda gains or loses public support; any major legislative battles in Congress during 2025-2026 that could fracture the Milei coalition; and Peronist primary dynamics, which could either consolidate behind a single candidate or splinter, affecting anti-Milei vote-splitting scenarios. The structural challenge for Macri is timing: if Milei remains broadly popular or if a unified Peronist challenger emerges, Macri becomes a third-force spoiler rather than a viable frontrunner.
