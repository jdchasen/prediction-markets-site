## Market Analysis

The bull case for three cuts hinges on a significant economic deterioration throughout 2025-2026. If CPI readings consistently drop below the Fed's 2% target by mid-2025 and nonfarm payroll reports show sustained job losses exceeding 100,000 monthly, the Fed could shift to aggressive easing. A recessionary environment with unemployment rising above 5% would create conditions for quarterly cuts starting March 2026 and continuing through September and December FOMC meetings. Additionally, if core PCE inflation tracks below 1.8% for consecutive quarters, the Fed would have room to provide stimulus without risking reflation.

The bear case, which the current odds strongly favor, assumes the Fed maintains a restrictive stance as inflation proves persistent around 2.5-3%. Even if the Fed implements one or two cuts in 2026, three cuts would require 75 basis points of easing—an aggressive pace only justified by economic crisis. The current labor market resilience, with unemployment near 4%, gives the Fed little urgency to cut rates rapidly. If wage growth remains above 4% annually and services inflation stays elevated, the FOMC will likely pause after initial modest cuts.

Key catalysts include the January 2025 CPI report (February 12 release), which will set the tone for Fed policy expectations heading into 2026. Monthly jobs reports throughout 2025, particularly the Q4 2025 data released in early 2026, will determine whether labor market cracks are forming. Traders should monitor the Fed's preferred inflation gauge—core PCE—with special attention to the December 2025 reading (released January 2026) as it will directly inform the probability of multiple cuts. Any Q1 2026 GDP print showing negative growth would dramatically shift these odds higher.
