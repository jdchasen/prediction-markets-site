## Market Analysis

The bull case for three cuts centers on a delayed recession scenario where economic weakness materializes in late 2025 or early 2026. If monthly CPI prints consistently fall below 2% year-over-year by Q4 2025, and nonfarm payrolls begin averaging below 100,000 jobs monthly, the Fed could pivot aggressively. A financial stability event—such as regional bank stress or a credit crunch in commercial real estate—would accelerate this timeline. The Fed's September 2025 Summary of Economic Projections will be critical; if dot plots shift to show three or more cuts penciled in for 2026, these odds would surge. Additionally, if unemployment rises above 4.5% by early 2026, historical precedent suggests the Fed cuts rapidly once labor market deterioration begins.

The bear case, which current pricing heavily favors, assumes core PCE inflation remains above 2.5% throughout 2025 due to persistent services inflation and wage growth. If average hourly earnings stay elevated above 4% annually and shelter costs refuse to moderate, the Fed will likely hold rates steady or cut only once or twice for technical adjustments. Current market pricing in fed funds futures shows terminal rates for 2026 still elevated, suggesting traders expect a "higher for longer" regime. The January 2026 FOMC meeting will set the tone, but traders should monitor Q4 2025 GDP growth—if it exceeds 2.5% annualized, rate cuts become politically and economically difficult to justify.

Key catalysts include the December 2025 CPI report (released January 15, 2026), which would inform the January 28-29, 2026 FOMC decision. The November 2025 employment report (December 5, 2025) will be crucial for assessing labor market momentum heading into the target year. Watch for the Fed's March 2026 SEP update, as any significant downgrade to growth forecasts could trigger a reassessment. If the Cleveland Fed's nowcast shows inflation expectations re-anchoring below 2.2% by Q1 2026, this market would need repricing, but current odds reflect skepticism that such disinflationary conditions will materialize.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the market require three full 25 basis point cuts or could smaller increments count?

The market resolves based on three distinct rate reduction decisions regardless of magnitude—three 25bp cuts would qualify, as would any combination totaling at least 75bp across three separate FOMC meetings in 2026.

### If the Fed cuts twice in 2025 and continues cutting into 2026, does that make three 2026 cuts more or less likely?

Less likely in most scenarios, as earlier cuts would suggest the Fed is front-loading easing and may pause in 2026 to assess effects, unless a severe recession forces continuous accommodation throughout both years.

### What inflation level would historically justify three rate cuts in a single year outside of crisis conditions?

Core PCE would typically need to fall decisively below 2% with unemployment rising above 4.5-5%, conditions that haven't materialized in current Fed forecasts, explaining the low probability assigned to this outcome.
