## Market Analysis

The bull case for recession hinges on policy-induced shocks and monetary tightening lag effects. Trump's proposed tariffs—potentially 10-20% universal tariffs and 60% on Chinese goods—could trigger supply chain disruptions and retaliatory measures that contract global trade. The Fed's restrictive stance through most of 2023-2024, with rates held at 5.25-5.50%, typically shows 12-18 month lag effects on real economic activity. Labor market softening is already evident in the November NFP report showing 227,000 jobs added but unemployment ticking to 4.2%. If the Sahm Rule indicator (currently at 0.57) crosses the 0.5 threshold sustainably or unemployment reaches 4.5%+, recession probability spikes significantly. Corporate earnings compressions in cyclical sectors would confirm demand destruction.

The bear case against recession points to consumer resilience and fiscal stimulus momentum. Real wages have turned positive with CPI inflation declining to 2.7% year-over-year in November, while household balance sheets remain relatively healthy with debt service ratios near historic lows. The Atlanta Fed's GDPNow model consistently projects growth above 2.5% for Q4 2024. Tax cut extensions and deregulation from the new administration could boost business investment. The Fed's December 2024 dot plot projects only two 25bp cuts in 2025, suggesting confidence in continued expansion. Services PMI readings above 50 indicate ongoing expansion in the economy's largest sector.

Critical catalysts include the January 29, 2025 FOMC meeting where Powell's guidance on the cutting cycle will clarify recession risks, followed by the February 7 jobs report. March CPI data (released April 10) will test whether inflation has genuinely been tamed or if tariff-driven price pressures are building. The Treasury's refunding announcements in February and May will signal fiscal sustainability concerns. Q1 2025 GDP advance estimate (April 30) provides the first hard data on how the economy is absorbing policy transitions. Traders should monitor weekly initial jobless claims for sustained moves above 250,000 and the ISM Manufacturing PMI for readings below 48, both recessionary warning signals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is recession defined for this market's resolution criteria?

Most prediction markets use the NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee's official determination, which considers multiple indicators beyond the simplified "two consecutive quarters of negative GDP" rule. This means resolution could occur months after the actual recession period begins.

### Why are recession odds only 37.5% when some economists see tariffs as highly disruptive?

Markets are pricing the gap between threatened tariffs and actual implementation, expecting negotiated compromises that reduce economic impact. Additionally, the 2-year timeframe allows substantial room for policy adjustments and Fed responses to offset shocks.

### What would cause these odds to jump above 60% quickly?

A sharp inversion of leading indicators—specifically unemployment rising above 4.7%, ISM Manufacturing below 45 for two consecutive months, or the 10-year/2-year yield curve steepening rapidly past 50bp after inversion—would signal imminent contraction and dramatically shift probabilities.
