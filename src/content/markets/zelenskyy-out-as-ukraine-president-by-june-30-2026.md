## Market Analysis

The bear case for Zelenskyy's tenure centers on war exhaustion and political vulnerability. If territorial concessions become necessary for peace negotiations—particularly regarding Crimea or Donbas—domestic opposition could mobilize against him despite martial law. Ukraine's constitution allows presidential impeachment with a three-quarters Rada vote, and while currently implausible, a catastrophic military collapse or corruption scandal involving his inner circle could shift parliamentary arithmetic. Additionally, if martial law ends before June 2026, Ukraine would need to hold the presidential election already postponed from March 2024, opening electoral risk if public sentiment sours on his wartime leadership. The economic strain of reconstruction, combined with potential Western aid fatigue, creates conditions where political challengers like former Commander-in-Chief Valery Zaluzhny could emerge as alternatives.

The bull case rests on Zelenskyy's consolidated wartime authority and lack of credible succession mechanisms. Martial law remains in effect with no clear end date, and the Rada has shown no appetite for political transitions during active conflict. His approval ratings, while down from their 90%+ wartime peak, remain strong enough domestically that organized opposition faces accusations of undermining national unity. International partners—particularly the U.S. and EU—view him as indispensable for aid continuity and negotiations, making external pressure for his removal unlikely. Even if elections occur, his wartime profile and control over media narratives during martial law provide substantial incumbency advantages.

Key catalysts include any ceasefire or peace agreement announcement, which could trigger constitutional requirements for elections within months. Watch for the U.S. presidential transition's impact on Ukraine policy through early 2025, as shifting American support could alter domestic political calculations. The Rada's periodic martial law extension votes (typically every 90 days) serve as informal confidence measures. Military developments around Pokrovsk and Kursk through winter 2024-2025 will determine whether momentum shifts enough to crack elite consensus. Any serious health concerns or security incidents involving Zelenskyy would immediately move this market given the concentration of power in his office.
