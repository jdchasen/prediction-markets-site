## Market Analysis

The bear case for Zelenskyy's tenure centers on military and political exhaustion. If Russian forces achieve significant territorial gains in 2025 or Ukraine faces battlefield collapse, domestic pressure for leadership change could become overwhelming. Ukraine's presidential elections were originally scheduled for March 2024 but suspended under martial law—if the war reaches a frozen conflict state or ceasefire by late 2025, constitutional pressure to hold delayed elections could force a vote where Zelenskyy faces a war-weary electorate. Polling from late 2024 showed declining approval ratings as the counteroffensive stalled, and former Commander-in-Chief Valerii Zaluzhnyi has emerged as a potential political rival with strong public support. Additionally, Western aid fatigue—particularly if Trump or a skeptical administration reduces military support—could undermine Zelenskyy's position as the symbol of resistance.

The bull case rests on Zelenskyy's consolidation of power during wartime and the absence of viable succession mechanisms. Ukraine's constitution provides no clear path for removing a president during martial law, and Zelenskyy has systematically neutralized potential rivals, including reassigning Zaluzhnyi to ambassador to the UK. As long as active hostilities continue, postponing elections remains legally defensible and practically necessary. International backing remains strong among European allies, and Zelenskyy's personal relationship with Western leaders makes him nearly irreplaceable as Ukraine's diplomatic face. Historical precedent shows wartime leaders rarely lose power mid-conflict, and Ukraine's political system lacks the institutional mechanisms for a smooth transition during ongoing warfare.

Key catalysts to monitor include the spring 2025 offensive season outcomes, any serious ceasefire negotiations (which could trigger constitutional obligations for elections), U.S. Congressional aid votes scheduled for the 2025 budget cycle, and signs of organized political opposition within Ukraine's parliament. The appointment of Zaluzhniy or other potential successors to prominent domestic positions would signal increasing internal pressure. Watch for polling data on presidential approval and any legislative efforts to clarify election timing under martial law conditions.
