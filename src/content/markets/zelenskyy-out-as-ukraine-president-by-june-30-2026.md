## Market Analysis

The bear case for Zelenskyy's tenure centers on Ukraine's constitutional requirement to hold presidential elections, originally scheduled for March 2024 but suspended under martial law. If a ceasefire or peace agreement emerges in 2025-2026, martial law would likely be lifted, triggering elections where war fatigue, territorial concessions, or corruption scandals could produce an upset. Recent polling shows Zelenskyy's approval ratings have declined from wartime highs of 90%+ to the 60-70% range as of late 2024, creating vulnerability if the conflict drags on without decisive progress. Additionally, Western pressure for negotiations—particularly if the U.S. administration pushes for a settlement—could force political compromises that weaken his position domestically. A health crisis, assassination attempt, or internal power struggle within Ukrainian leadership could also force an unexpected departure.

The bull case rests on Ukraine's martial law framework, which legally suspends elections during wartime and faces no serious domestic challenge given Russia's ongoing occupation of Ukrainian territory. Zelenskyy maintains international legitimacy as Ukraine's wartime leader, with no viable political challenger commanding comparable support or Western confidence. His Servant of the People party controls the Rada, and opposition figures like Petro Poroshenko or Yulia Tymoshenko poll significantly lower. Historically, wartime leaders rarely face successful removal, and Ukraine's political culture has consolidated around Zelenskyy as the symbol of resistance. Even if peace negotiations begin, the timeline from ceasefire to martial law lifting to organizing elections would likely extend beyond June 2026.

Key catalysts to monitor include any ceasefire announcements in 2025, which would start the clock on electoral timelines; Ukraine's spring 2025 and 2026 military offensives that could shift domestic sentiment; the U.S. 2024 election outcome and subsequent policy shifts; and any Rada discussions about lifting martial law or electoral procedures. Watch for polling data from Rating Group and Kyiv International Institute of Sociology, typically released quarterly, and statements from Western partners about Ukraine's political transition requirements tied to reconstruction funding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What specific constitutional mechanism would remove Zelenskyy before June 2026 if elections remain suspended?

Ukraine's constitution allows martial law to continue indefinitely during active combat, suspending elections automatically. Removal would require either impeachment by the Rada (requiring 338 of 450 votes), voluntary resignation, or incapacitation—all highly unlikely scenarios given his party's control.

### When would elections need to be scheduled to conclude before the June 30, 2026 deadline?

Ukrainian law requires at least 90 days between announcing elections and voting day, meaning martial law would need to lift by late March 2026 at the latest, which would require a peace agreement or ceasefire signed no later than early 2026.

### How have previous Ukrainian presidents left office, and does this provide precedent?

Viktor Yanukovych fled during the 2014 Euromaidan revolution, and all other post-Soviet Ukrainian presidents left through scheduled elections, making forced wartime removal unprecedented in Ukrainian history and suggesting the low probability reflects genuine institutional stability rather than market inefficiency.
