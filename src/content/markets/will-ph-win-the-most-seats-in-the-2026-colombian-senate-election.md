## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on PH's structural advantages as the governing coalition under President Gustavo Petro. The party holds organizational momentum from its 2022 breakthrough when it secured 20 Senate seats, making it the largest single bloc. Petro's administration controls patronage networks and can leverage government resources for coalition-building ahead of the March 8, 2026 election. Colombia's fragmented opposition remains divided among traditional parties like the Conservative Party, Liberal Party, and Centro Democrático, making it difficult for any single rival to consolidate anti-government voters. Additionally, PH benefits from strong urban support in Bogotá and other major cities where population growth favors their electoral prospects.

The bear case centers on governing coalition fatigue and Petro's declining approval ratings, which have fluctuated between 35-45% throughout 2024 according to Invamer and Guarumo polls. Historical patterns show Colombian voters often punish the incumbent party during midterm legislative elections. Petro's ambitious reform agenda—including healthcare, pension, and labor reforms—has stalled in Congress, potentially disappointing the progressive base that propelled PH to victory. Economic headwinds including inflation concerns and fiscal constraints could further erode support. The opposition could also consolidate around a unity coalition by late 2025, particularly if they coordinate around Senate list strategies in Colombia's proportional representation system. Security concerns in rural areas where ELN ceasefire negotiations have faltered may benefit conservative alternatives.

Key catalysts include Colombia's October 2025 regional elections, which will serve as a critical bellwether for national sentiment ahead of the Senate vote. Coalition registration deadlines typically fall in December 2025, revealing whether opposition parties will fragment or unite. Traders should monitor quarterly polling data from Invamer, Yanhaas, and Centro Nacional de Consultoría throughout 2025, particularly tracking PH's vote intention in the Senate race specifically (distinct from presidential approval). The formal campaign period begins 90 days before the March 8, 2026 election date, when advertising spending and debate performance could shift perceptions. Any major corruption scandals involving PH legislators or cabinet members would represent significant downside risk given Colombian voters' sensitivity to governance issues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many Senate seats does Pacto Histórico currently hold and what margin do they need to maintain plurality?

PH currently holds approximately 20 of 108 Senate seats. They need to remain the largest single bloc, which could mean as few as 18-20 seats given Colombia's extreme party fragmentation across a dozen active coalitions.

### Does "winning the most seats" mean PH needs an absolute majority or just a plurality?

This market requires only a plurality—more seats than any other single party or coalition, not the 55-seat majority threshold. Colombia's Senate has never had a single party majority in recent democratic history.

### What happens if opposition parties merge into a unified coalition before the election?

If opposition parties formally register as a single electoral coalition by the December 2025 deadline and win more combined seats than PH, that unified list would win this market, making pre-election coalition agreements a critical factor to monitor.
