If the Fed maintains its restrictive stance through Q1 2026, cumulative cooling effects could push year-over-year CPI toward the 2.5% level, especially if core inflation continues moderating from current levels. The bear case, reflected in the 1.1% odds, argues that inflation remains sticky above 2.5% due to service-sector inflation persistence, healthcare cost growth, and shelter costs that typically lag CPI broadly. Even with Fed tightening, the lag between policy and inflation outcomes means Q1 2026 year-over-year comparisons still face relatively high prior-year bases from early 2025. A Fed pause or pivot toward rate cuts during 2025 (potentially signaled at FOMC meetings in January, March, and May 2025) could reignite demand and push inflation higher, not lower. Oil prices, geopolitical disruptions, or a weaker dollar could all push headline inflation above 2.5% regardless of underlying demand dynamics.

Traders should monitor the PCE deflator releases in January and February 2026 (released in early-to-mid February and March respectively), as PCE trends often precede headline CPI movements. FOMC communications throughout late 2025 will signal the Fed's inflation confidence; any hawkish repricing would suggest they expect inflation to remain elevated. The January 2026 employment report (released early February) is critical—if jobless claims spike or wage growth decelerates sharply, it could create the deflationary conditions needed to push toward 2.5%. Watch for oil prices approaching $50/barrel by March 2026, as energy deflation is one of the few mechanisms that could drive headline inflation to target with sticky core readings still elevated.
