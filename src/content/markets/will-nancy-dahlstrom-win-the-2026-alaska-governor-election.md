## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Dahlstrom's institutional advantages as the sitting Lieutenant Governor with likely endorsement from popular Governor Dunleavy, who cannot seek a third consecutive term. She has statewide name recognition, executive experience, and potential backing from the Alaska Republican establishment and national GOP donors. Her conservative credentials could unite the Republican base in a state that went 53% for Trump in 2024. If she can position herself as Dunleavy's natural successor while avoiding the vote-splitting that plagued Sarah Palin's recent congressional bids, she could consolidate the right-of-center vote effectively under Alaska's ranked-choice voting system implemented in 2020.

The bear case is substantial. Alaska's ranked-choice voting system has proven treacherous for Republicans, enabling Democrat Mary Peltola to win the congressional seat in 2022 and 2024 despite Alaska's conservative lean. Dahlstrom herself withdrew from the 2024 congressional race after placing third in the August primary, demonstrating vulnerability even among Republican voters. Independent candidates have historically performed well in Alaska gubernatorial races, and a credible independent could siphon crucial second-choice votes. Former Governor Bill Walker won in 2014 as an independent, and similar dynamics could emerge in 2026. The filing deadline for Alaska's gubernatorial primary typically falls in early June 2026, which will clarify the full field.

Key catalysts include the June 2026 primary filing deadline, which will reveal whether multiple Republicans enter and split the conservative vote, and whether a strong independent or moderate Democrat emerges.
