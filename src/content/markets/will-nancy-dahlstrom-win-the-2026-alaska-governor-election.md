## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on Dahlstrom's existing statewide name recognition as Lieutenant Governor and her ties to the Alaska Republican establishment. She withdrew from the 2024 special House election to consolidate support behind Nick Begich, demonstrating party loyalty that could translate into unified GOP backing for a gubernatorial run. If she can position herself as the consensus conservative candidate while avoiding a crowded primary field, Alaska's conservative lean in statewide races gives her a foundation. Governor Mike Dunleavy's second term ends in 2026, creating an open race that typically presents more opportunities for new candidates.

The bear case is formidable: Alaska's ranked-choice voting system, implemented in 2022, fundamentally changed electoral dynamics by rewarding broad appeal over base mobilization. Dahlstrom would need to win over not just Republicans but also independents who comprise a massive voting bloc in Alaska. Her withdrawal from the 2024 House race raises questions about her ability to withstand competitive campaigns. Mary Peltola's success in federal races demonstrates how Democrats and moderate independents can unite behind a single candidate. If former Governor Bill Walker or another independent runs, they could split the anti-establishment vote in ways that complicate Dahlstrom's path, particularly if she's positioned as the establishment Republican.

Key catalysts include Alaska's August 2026 primary, which will reveal the full candidate field and whether Republicans consolidate behind Dahlstrom or fragment. Watch for polling throughout 2025 showing name recognition and favorability ratings among independent voters, who will likely decide the race. Any policy positions Dahlstrom stakes out on Alaska Permanent Fund dividends—perennially the state's most sensitive political issue—will immediately impact her viability. Dunleavy's approval ratings throughout his final year will also matter, as they'll shape whether voters want continuity or change from Republican governance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does Alaska's ranked-choice voting system affect Dahlstrom's chances compared to traditional primaries?

Ranked-choice requires candidates to appeal beyond their party base, making it harder for conventional Republicans to win. Dahlstrom would need second and third-choice votes from independents and moderate Democrats, a significantly higher bar than simply winning a Republican primary.

### What role does the Alaska Permanent Fund dividend play in this race?

The PFD amount is Alaska's defining political issue, and candidates' positions on dividend calculations versus state spending directly determine their electoral success. Dahlstrom will need to navigate between fiscal conservatives and voters demanding maximum dividend payments.

### Could a strong independent candidate like Bill Walker entering the race effectively eliminate Dahlstrom's path to victory?

An independent candidacy would likely attract moderate Republicans and independents who might otherwise give Dahlstrom second-choice votes, making it nearly impossible for her to assemble a winning coalition under ranked-choice voting where she'd need broad cross-partisan appeal.
