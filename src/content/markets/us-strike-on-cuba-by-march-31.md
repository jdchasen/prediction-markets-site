## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on potential scenarios where Cuba becomes a flashpoint for great power competition. China's expanding security relationships in Latin America, particularly any attempt to establish military facilities in Cuba reminiscent of Cold War dynamics, could trigger a forceful U.S. response. Additionally, a major terrorist incident or assassination attempt linked to Cuban territory, discovery of advanced weapons systems from adversary nations, or a sudden refugee crisis involving violence against U.S. assets could rapidly shift political calculations. The 2024-2025 period has seen increased Republican rhetoric about confronting authoritarianism in the hemisphere, and a crisis manufactured or real could provide pretext for military strikes against specific targets like intelligence installations.

The bear case is substantially stronger given historical precedent and contemporary constraints. The U.S. has not conducted military strikes on Cuba since the Bay of Pigs era despite decades of antagonism, preferring economic pressure and diplomatic isolation. Current U.S. military commitments in Ukraine support and Middle East deployments stretch resources thin, making new military adventures politically costly. The Biden administration through January 2025 showed no appetite for Caribbean military intervention, and even a more hawkish subsequent administration would face congressional skepticism about unilateral action absent a direct attack on U.S. territory. Cuba's limited strategic threat capability and the diplomatic fallout across Latin America make strikes extremely unlikely without an extraordinary triggering event.

Key catalysts to monitor include the January 2025 presidential transition and any policy signals from a new administration regarding Cuba, Chinese Foreign Minister visits to Havana (which occurred in 2023 and sparked surveillance balloon tensions), and the April-June 2025 period when the State Department typically reviews countries on the state sponsor of terrorism list. Any Cuban involvement in migration crises or the bi-annual Organization of American States summits could also shift dynamics, though none of these are likely sufficient alone to justify military action.
