## Market Analysis

The bull case for closure centers on Iran's historical willingness to threaten this critical waterway during peak confrontations, particularly if Western sanctions intensify or if Israel conducts strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities. Approximately 21% of global petroleum passes through this narrow chokepoint, making it Iran's most powerful leverage point against the West. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has repeatedly conducted exercises simulating strait closure, and hardliners within Iran's government have publicly stated they would close it if oil exports are completely blocked. Key catalysts include the March 2026 IAEA Board of Governors meeting where censure resolutions or snapback sanctions could be triggered, and any Israeli military action against Iranian nuclear sites at Natanz or Fordow. The June 2025 expiration of remaining JCPOA waivers creates another pressure point where Iran might escalate to force negotiations.

The bear case questions whether Iran would actually execute such a drastic measure given the guaranteed military response from the U.S. Fifth Fleet stationed in Bahrain and the economic devastation it would bring to Iranian allies relying on the strait, including China and India who purchase Iranian oil. A complete closure differs significantly from harassment operations or temporary blockades—sustaining it would require Iran to maintain military control against overwhelming U.S. naval and air superiority, something analysts consider militarily unfeasible for more than days. Additionally, partially restricting traffic or conducting temporary closures wouldn't meet most market definitions of "closing" the strait. The definition matters significantly here: if the market requires sustained total closure versus any temporary disruption, the probability should be considerably lower.

Traders should monitor IAEA quarterly reports (next major one expected September 2025), any movement toward reimposing UN sanctions through the snapback mechanism, and particularly Israeli cabinet discussions about military action following Prime Minister Netanyahu's statements about preventing Iranian nuclear capability. The U.S. election outcome in November 2024 and subsequent Iran policy announcements in early 2025 will substantially shift probabilities. Watch for Iranian threats specifically mentioning Hormuz closure—these have historically preceded major escalations—and any unusual IRGC naval deployments near the Strait's narrowest 21-mile passage point between February and May 2026.

### What specific actions would constitute "closing" the Strait of Hormuz for this market's resolution criteria?
