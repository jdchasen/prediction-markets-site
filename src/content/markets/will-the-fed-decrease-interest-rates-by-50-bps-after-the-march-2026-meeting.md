## Market Analysis

The bull case for a large cut hinges on a significant economic deterioration between now and March 2026. This would require multiple negative catalysts converging: a sharp recession with unemployment spiking above 5-6%, core PCE inflation dropping sustainably below 2%, or a financial system shock comparable to 2008 or March 2020. Historical precedent shows the Fed only cuts by 50+ bps in crisis scenarios—the last occurrence was March 2020's emergency cuts during COVID-19. For this market to pay out, we'd need to see consecutive months of NFP prints showing job losses exceeding 100,000, credit markets seizing up, or a major geopolitical event triggering immediate economic contraction.

The bear case, which current pricing reflects, assumes the Fed maintains a measured approach to monetary policy through 2025-2026. Even if the economy softens moderately, the Fed's demonstrated preference is for 25 bps increments unless confronting an acute crisis. The December 2024 FOMC minutes emphasized data-dependency and gradual adjustments. For large cuts to remain unlikely, we need inflation metrics like CPI and PCE to stay sticky above 2.5%, labor markets to remain resilient with monthly NFP prints above 100,000, and GDP growth maintaining positive territory even if slowing to 1-2% annually.

Key catalysts to monitor include the January 2025 CPI release (February 12) and subsequent monthly inflation data through 2025, which will determine the Fed's cutting cycle pace. The FOMC meetings scheduled for January 29, March 19, May 7, and June 18, 2025 will establish the trajectory—if the Fed is still cutting 25 bps per meeting by mid-2025, they're unlikely to need emergency cuts by March 2026. Monthly NFP reports remain critical; any three-month average below zero jobs created would dramatically shift crisis probabilities. Watch Q4 2024 and Q1 2025 GDP prints (released late January and April) for recession signals, and corporate earnings seasons for guidance on economic health heading into 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does this market resolve YES if the Fed cuts 50 bps cumulatively across multiple meetings, or must it be a single 50+ bps cut?

The market requires a single cut of 50 basis points or more at one meeting by the March 2026 deadline. Multiple smaller cuts totaling 50+ bps would not trigger a YES resolution.

### What historical scenarios have caused the Fed to cut by 50+ bps, and how often does this happen?

The Fed has only implemented 50+ bps cuts during severe crises: the 2001 recession, the 2007-2008 financial crisis, and the March 2020 COVID-19 emergency. These events occur roughly once per decade and typically involve systemic financial stress or sudden economic collapse.

### If the Fed cuts rates steadily through 2025, does that make a large March 2026 cut more or less likely?

Steady 25 bps cuts through 2025 would make a large March 2026 cut less likely, as it suggests the Fed is successfully managing a soft landing rather than facing an emergency requiring aggressive action. Large cuts only happen when the Fed needs to respond to unexpected shocks.
