## Market Analysis

The bull case for a rate cut hinges on successful inflation control through 2025-2026, potentially driven by moderated commodity prices, fiscal consolidation under Finance Minister Haddad's framework, and global monetary easing creating room for Brazil to lower rates from currently elevated levels. If inflation converges toward the 3% target by mid-2026 and economic growth remains sluggish, the Banco Central do Brasil would have clear justification to ease. The appointment of Gabriel Galípolo as BCB president in January 2025 introduces a Lula-aligned voice who may prove more dovish than his predecessor, though his inflation-fighting credentials remain untested. Brazil's October 2026 presidential election could also factor in, as incumbent pressure typically favors looser policy in election years.

The bear case centers on persistent inflation pressures that keep the Selic elevated well into 2026. Brazil's fiscal situation remains precarious despite recent efforts, with public debt exceeding 75% of GDP and spending pressures from social programs. If commodity exports remain strong or the real depreciates significantly, imported inflation could force the BCB to maintain restrictive policy. Galípolo may need to prove his inflation-fighting independence early in his tenure, making him reluctant to cut rates prematurely. The BCB's forward guidance through 2025 will be critical—any signals that current rate levels need to persist longer would shift probabilities downward.

Key catalysts include the BCB's policy meetings throughout 2025 and early 2026, particularly the March and May 2026 meetings that will set expectations for June. Monthly IPCA inflation readings (released mid-month) and quarterly GDP figures will directly influence rate expectations. The fiscal framework's credibility tests come with budget submissions in August of each year. Most importantly, the October 2026 presidential election and pre-election polling through mid-2026 will clarify political dynamics that could pressure BCB decision-making, even as the bank maintains formal independence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does Gabriel Galípolo's appointment as BCB president affect the likelihood of rate cuts in 2026?

Galípolo takes over in January 2025 with closer ties to President Lula than his predecessor, potentially creating more dovish tendencies. However, he faces immediate credibility tests on inflation control that may force him to maintain hawkish positioning through much of his early tenure.

### What inflation level would the Banco Central need to see to justify cutting rates after June 2026?

The BCB targets 3% inflation with a tolerance band of 1.5-4.5%. Sustained readings near or below 3.5% with declining expectations would likely create space for cuts, while inflation above 4% would keep policy restrictive regardless of political pressure.

### Does the timing after the June 2026 meeting specifically matter given Brazil's October 2026 election?

Yes, critically. A post-June cut would occur just months before the October presidential election, raising questions about political interference despite BCB independence. Markets will scrutinize whether any easing is justified by data or influenced by electoral considerations.
