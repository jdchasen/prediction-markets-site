## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does the PVV's small seat count (17 of 150) make dissolution more likely?

With only 17 seats, Wilders must keep three separate coalition partners aligned simultaneously; if any one partner withdraws over a policy dispute, the government loses its majority and must either dissolve or risk losing parliamentary votes, both of which cascade toward new elections.

### How do Dutch budget negotiations specifically threaten this coalition's stability?

The annual budget vote is a mandatory parliamentary moment where ideological differences surface sharply—VVD's fiscal conservatism, NSC's healthcare demands, and BBB's agricultural subsidies create inherent conflicts that previous Dutch coalitions have struggled to resolve without collapsing.

### Would individual coalition parties actually prefer early dissolution risk over staying in government until 2026?

No—polling suggests all four parties would lose seats in early elections, so they have strong electoral incentives to avoid triggering dissolution, which explains why the market prices dissolution at only 21% despite the coalition's ideological tensions.
