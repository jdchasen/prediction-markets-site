## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Netanyahu's legal vulnerabilities and coalition instability. Bennett maintains strong name recognition and could capitalize on public fatigue with the current government, particularly if security situations deteriorate or economic conditions worsen. His previous tenure demonstrated an ability to unite disparate political factions, and polling consistently shows voters desire new leadership options beyond the Netanyahu-Gantz binary. The ongoing judicial reform controversy continues to destabilize Netanyahu's coalition, and any coalition collapse before 2026 could create an opening. Bennett's centrist positioning appeals to both right-wing security hawks and centrist voters exhausted by political chaos.

The bear case highlights Bennett's 2022 exit from politics and lack of current party infrastructure. He disbanded his Yamina party and would need to either create a new political vehicle or join an existing one to contest elections. Recent polls show new parties face significant barriers to building voter trust quickly in Israel's crowded political landscape. Bennett's previous coalition collapsed after just one year, raising questions about his ability to maintain stable governance. Netanyahu has proven remarkably resilient despite multiple corruption trials, and his Likud party maintains a robust organizational structure Bennett currently lacks.

Key catalysts include any Supreme Court rulings on Netanyahu's fitness to serve while under indictment, scheduled coalition budget votes that could trigger snap elections, and Bennett's public statements about potential political re-entry. Traders should monitor Bennett's media appearances and whether he begins rebuilding political infrastructure in 2025. The next scheduled election isn't until 2026, but Israel has held five elections since 2019, making early dissolution a realistic possibility. Watch for polling data showing Bennett's new party (if formed) crossing the electoral threshold and coalition stability indicators.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Bennett need to win the next election to become Prime Minister, or are there other paths?

Bennett could become PM through coalition negotiations even without winning the most seats, as Israeli prime ministers are determined by who can form a governing majority of 61+ Knesset members. He could also emerge as a compromise candidate if neither major bloc can form a government.

### What would Bennett need to do to actually contest the next election given he's not currently in the Knesset?

Bennett would need to either establish a new political party and register it with the Central Elections Committee, or join and lead an existing party, both of which would require significant organizational groundwork months before any election is called.

### How does this market resolve if there's a rotating prime minister agreement like previous Israeli coalitions?

The market resolution likely depends on who holds the position on December 31, 2026, meaning rotation agreements could significantly impact outcomes—traders should verify the specific resolution criteria regarding whether Bennett needs to be serving at the expiry date or simply have held the office at any point.
