## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Netanyahu's legal troubles and declining public approval potentially creating a vacuum for center-right leadership. Bennett maintains name recognition and credibility with security-focused voters, having served as both prime minister and defense minister. If the current coalition collapses before 2026—a realistic scenario given its narrow majority and internal tensions—and Likud seeks to move beyond Netanyahu, Bennett could position himself as a unifying figure for the right-wing bloc. His previous success in forming the diverse 2021 coalition demonstrates coalition-building skills that could prove valuable in Israel's fragmented political landscape.

The bear case highlights substantial structural obstacles. Bennett's Yamina party effectively dissolved after he left politics in June 2022, and he explicitly stated he was leaving political life. Rebuilding a party apparatus, securing funding, and winning enough Knesset seats to lead a coalition typically requires 18-24 months of groundwork. Current polling shows Benny Gantz's National Unity party and Yair Lapid's Yesh Atid leading opposition alternatives, both with existing party infrastructure. Bennett would need to pull voters from their bases while competing against sitting MKs with active constituencies. The calendar also matters: if elections occur in early 2025 during ongoing war circumstances, voters historically favor incumbents with security credentials currently in office.

Key catalysts include potential coalition collapse triggers like the 2025 budget vote expected in March, Supreme Court rulings on judicial reform that could destabilize the government, and any formal announcement from Bennett about political intentions. Traders should monitor Israeli polling aggregates for shifts in right-wing voter preferences, Likud internal dynamics regarding Netanyahu's succession, and whether Bennett begins making public appearances or policy statements. The next scheduled election isn't until 2026, but Israel has held five elections since 2019, making early elections a constant possibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Bennett need to win a primary or party leadership election before potentially becoming PM?

Bennett would need to either revive Yamina or create a new party and win enough Kneset seats to lead a coalition, as Israel's parliamentary system makes the coalition-builder the prime minister. This requires winning a general election, not a party primary.

### What happened to Bennett's previous political party after he left office?

Yamina effectively dissolved after Bennett announced his exit from politics in June 2022, with its remaining MKs either retiring or joining other parties. He currently has no institutional party structure to leverage for a return.

### Could Bennett become PM without his party winning the most Knesset seats?

Yes—Israeli prime ministers are determined by who can form a coalition of at least 61 MKs out of 120, not by which single party wins the most seats. Bennett himself became PM in 2021 despite Yamina holding only seven seats by assembling a diverse coalition.
