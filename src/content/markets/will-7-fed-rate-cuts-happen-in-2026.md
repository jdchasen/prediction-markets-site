## Market Analysis

The bear case against seven cuts points to resilient economic fundamentals: January 2025 nonfarm payrolls added 143,000 jobs with unemployment at 4.0%, while core PCE inflation remains sticky above the Fed's 2% target at 2.8% year-over-year. The FOMC's December 2024 dot plot projected only two cuts for 2025, signaling a cautious approach. For seven cuts to materialize in 2026, the Fed would need to first implement multiple cuts in 2025, then accelerate dramatically—a scenario that would require unemployment spiking above 6% or CPI turning negative. The 2024 FOMC schedule shows eight meetings, meaning seven cuts would exhaust nearly all available meeting opportunities with only one potential pause.

The bull case requires a "black swan" financial shock or hard landing scenario emerging in late 2025 or early 2026. A banking crisis, commercial real estate collapse, or sudden credit freeze could force emergency rate cuts similar to 2008 or March 2020. If the February 12 CPI report or March 7 employment data show sudden deterioration, traders might reassess tail risks. The key catalyst window is the second half of 2025: if the Fed cuts three or four times by December 2025 in response to weakening data, the market probability could rise from 0.4% to 5-10% as the baseline shifts.

Traders should monitor the FOMC statement language at the January 29 meeting for any shift toward prioritizing employment over inflation. The Q4 2025 GDP advance estimate (released January 2026) and the November-December 2025 CPI prints will be critical—consecutive monthly declines of 0.2% or more would signal deflationary pressure justifying aggressive action. Weekly initial jobless claims persistently above 260,000 would indicate labor market cracks. The market will also react to any Fed official speeches explicitly discussing recession scenarios or acknowledging downside risks to their baseline forecast.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What historical precedent exists for seven rate cuts in a single calendar year?

The Fed cut rates ten times in 2008 during the financial crisis (totaling 500 basis points) and implemented nine emergency cuts in 2001 following the dot-com crash and 9/11. Seven cuts would represent the third-most aggressive easing cycle in modern Fed history.

### Could the Fed cut rates seven times even if inflation remains above 2%?

Extremely unlikely unless unemployment surges simultaneously—the Fed would need to invoke its dual mandate prioritizing maximum employment over price stability, which typically requires unemployment rising above 5.5-6% to justify cuts with elevated inflation.

### If the Fed cuts three times in 2025, how much would this market's probability increase?

Three 2025 cuts would likely move the probability to 2-5%, as it would establish a dovish trajectory and require only four additional cuts in 2026 rather than seven from a standing start, though this still implies continued economic deterioration.
