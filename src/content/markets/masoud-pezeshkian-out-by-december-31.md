## Market Analysis

The bull case for Pezeshkian's exit centers on his reformist orientation creating insurmountable friction with Supreme Leader Ayatollah Khamenei and the Revolutionary Guard Corps. His moderate stance on hijab enforcement and potential openness to nuclear negotiations with Western powers could trigger a conservative backlash similar to what undermined previous reformist presidents. Iran's economic crisis, with inflation exceeding 40% and ongoing mass protests over social restrictions, creates a volatile environment where any perceived weakness could prompt the Guardian Council or security apparatus to pressure him toward resignation or force impeachment through the Majles. The constitutional structure allows the Supreme Leader to effectively sideline presidents who deviate from hardline positions, and Pezeshkian's narrow July 2024 victory suggests limited public mandate for dramatic reforms.

The bear case recognizes that Iranian presidents historically complete their four-year terms despite political turbulence, with no president forcibly removed since the 1979 revolution. Pezeshkian demonstrated political survival skills by maintaining his parliamentary seat for years under conservative pressure, suggesting he understands how to operate within acceptable boundaries. His campaign emphasized pragmatism over radical change, and his cabinet appointments have included establishment figures alongside reformists, indicating willingness to balance competing factions. The Supreme Leader typically prefers stability over upheaval, and removing a president would signal regime weakness domestically and internationally. Pezeshkian's emphasis on economic management rather than confrontational social reforms may allow him to avoid crossing redlines that would trigger removal.

Key catalysts to monitor include Iran's nuclear negotiations timeline with European powers and potential U.S. engagement in 2025, parliamentary elections scheduled for 2028 that could shift legislative dynamics, and Supreme Leader Khamenei's health developments given his advanced age. Any major protest movements comparable to the 2022 Mahsa Amini demonstrations would test Pezeshkian's response and could expose him to removal from either direction—too harsh a crackdown alienating his reformist base, or too lenient a response provoking conservative intervention. Watch for budget battles in early 2025 and 2026 spring sessions, as economic policy disagreements historically trigger Iranian presidential crises.

### What mechanisms could actually force Pezeshkian from office before 2026?

The Iranian parliament can impeach the president with a simple majority vote followed by Supreme Leader approval, or Khamenei could effectively force resignation through behind-the-scenes pressure from the Revolutionary Guard and judiciary. Constitutional removal requires Majles action, but political marginalization often precedes formal processes.

### How does Pezeshkian's election mandate compare to previous Iranian presidents who faced removal pressure?

Pezeshkian won with approximately 54% in the runoff against a low turnout of 49%, representing weaker legitimacy than Rouhani's 73% in 2013, making him more vulnerable to conservative challenges if he loses public support during economic or social crises.

### What role do nuclear negotiations play in determining Pezeshkian's political survival?

Successful sanctions relief through nuclear diplomacy could strengthen his position economically, while failed negotiations or increased Western pressure would empower hardliners arguing his moderate approach weakens Iran, potentially triggering security establishment intervention against his presidency.
