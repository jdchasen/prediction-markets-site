If the labor market softens meaningfully—watch January 10 NFP data and the February 7 employment report for signs of weakness—rate cuts could accelerate beyond current market pricing. The bear case hinges on inflation remaining sticky or re-accelerating, forcing the Fed to hold rates higher for longer. PCE and core PCE releases (January 31, February 28) are critical here; if inflation proves more durable, yields could stabilize or rise above 3.8%. A strong labor market through 2025 would also keep the Fed cautious—NFP beats would hurt the YES probability. The 10-year currently sits in the mid-4% range, so reaching 3.8% requires about 20-40 basis points of decline—achievable but not trivial. Watch the yield curve's shape; if long-end rates fall faster than short rates, it signals recession fears dominating. Treasury auction data (first Tuesday and Wednesday of each month) and Fed balance sheet moves can create technical pressure on yields independent of economic data.
