The bull case rests on Milei's ideological commitment and recent momentum: his December 2023 election victory gave him a mandate, his administration has already eliminated the central bank's board (a preparatory step), and dollarization polls consistently with 40-50% public support among his voter base. Regional neighbors (Brazil, Chile) have discouraged the move diplomatically. Critically, the 2025 midterm elections (likely held May-June) will occur just one month before the market expires, making legislative action in that window politically toxic for any party voting to cede monetary sovereignty during an election cycle. Historical precedent matters: Ecuador's 2000 dollarization took years of preparation and came after near-total economic collapse.

Watch for three concrete catalysts: First, the composition of Congress post-midterms in mid-2025 will signal whether any path to supermajority support exists—if Milei's coalition doesn't gain meaningful seats, dollarization becomes mathematically impossible. Third, any formal legislative proposal in 2025 would be a major signal, though timing before midterms appears strategically untenable. Track statements from Senate bloc leaders, particularly from the Peronist opposition, who control enough votes to block any constitutional reform dollarization would require.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Argentina need congressional approval to dollarize, and what's the threshold?

Full dollarization would require either a two-thirds congressional supermajority to amend the constitution or a binding national referendum; Milei's current ~30% coalition support makes both paths implausible without dramatic political realignment.

### Has Milei already taken concrete steps toward dollarization beyond rhetoric?

Yes—his administration eliminated the central bank's board in 2024 and accelerated peso depreciation policies, but these are preparatory measures far short of actual dollarization, which requires institutional and legal overhaul.

### How much would external financing Argentina would need to dollarize?

Estimates range from $30-50 billion to back the monetary base with dollar reserves; Argentina's current reserves (~$23 billion) and IMF program constraints make securing this without severe fiscal contraction extremely difficult within 18 months.
