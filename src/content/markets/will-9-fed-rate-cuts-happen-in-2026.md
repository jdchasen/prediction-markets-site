## Market Analysis

The bear case against this outcome is straightforward: nine cuts of 25 basis points each would total 225 basis points of easing, an extreme level historically associated with severe recessions or financial crises. The Fed would need to see sustained negative GDP growth, unemployment spiking well above 5-6%, and inflation falling significantly below the 2% target. Even during the 2007-2008 financial crisis, the Fed made 10 cuts but over a longer two-year period. Current labor market resilience, with monthly NFP reports consistently above 150K, and core PCE inflation still hovering near 2.5-3%, makes this scenario implausible unless something breaks dramatically in the financial system.

The bull case requires imagining a severe economic shock materializing in late 2025 or early 2026. This could stem from a major credit event, commercial real estate collapse triggering banking stress, or geopolitical disruptions causing demand destruction. Traders should monitor the January 2025 FOMC meeting and subsequent dot plot updates through 2025 for any shift toward a more dovish long-term stance. If the unemployment rate jumps above 4.5% in any NFP release or core CPI falls below 1.5% on monthly prints throughout 2025, the probability could inch higher.

Key catalysts include every 2025 FOMC meeting (eight scheduled meetings including January 28-29, March 18-19, and beyond), monthly CPI releases on the 12th-15th of each month, and NFP data on the first Friday monthly. The December 2025 FOMC summary of economic projections will be critical for understanding whether officials see any recession risk warranting such aggressive cuts. Credit market indicators like widening corporate bond spreads, rising SOFR-OIS spreads, or Treasury yield curve inversions deepening would signal growing stress that could justify reassessing this market.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What historical precedent exists for the Fed making nine cuts in a single calendar year?

The Fed has never cut rates nine times in a single calendar year in its modern history. Even during the 2008 financial crisis peak, cuts were spread across 2007-2008, making this an unprecedented scenario requiring a catastrophic economic event.

### At what unemployment rate would nine cuts in 2026 become plausible?

The unemployment rate would likely need to reach 7-8% or higher with rapid acceleration to justify nine cuts, similar to levels seen during the financial crisis. Current levels around 4% would need to deteriorate throughout 2025 for markets to price this seriously.

### If the Fed cuts four times in 2025, does that make nine cuts in 2026 more likely?

Not necessarily—four cuts in 2025 would likely bring rates closer to neutral, reducing the available room for further cuts and suggesting the Fed had already addressed the economic slowdown. Nine additional cuts would still require a new crisis emerging after that initial easing cycle.
