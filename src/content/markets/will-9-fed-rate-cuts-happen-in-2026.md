## Market Analysis

The bear case for nine cuts happening is straightforward: this would require 225 basis points of easing in a single year, a pace only seen during acute crises like 2008 or the 2020 pandemic onset. Current economic conditions show resilient employment (latest NFP at 256k in December) and inflation still above the Fed's 2% target, with core PCE at 2.8% as of November. The January 2025 FOMC held rates steady at 4.25-4.5%, signaling caution rather than urgency. For nine cuts to materialize, the U.S. would need to experience a severe recession or financial crisis starting in 2025, allowing the Fed to cut rates to near-zero and maintain that cutting cycle through 2026. The current dot plot from December showed only two projected cuts for 2025, making this scenario require a complete reversal in economic trajectory.

The bull case requires envisioning a black swan event: a major financial system shock, a deep recession with unemployment spiking above 7%, or sustained deflation threatening price stability. In such scenarios, the Fed could cut by 50-75 basis points per meeting across multiple consecutive meetings. Historical precedent exists—the Fed cut 11 times in 2007-2008 and made emergency cuts in March 2020. A systemic banking crisis, commercial real estate collapse, or geopolitical shock disrupting global trade could trigger this path. However, even in severe downturns, nine cuts would require starting from current elevated rates and having sufficient runway through 2026.

Key catalysts to monitor include the February 12 CPI report and February FOMC minutes on February 19, which will clarify the Fed's 2025 cutting timeline. The March 19 FOMC decision will be critical for establishing whether any cuts begin in Q1. Monthly NFP releases (next on February 7) showing labor market deterioration would be the earliest signal of conditions potentially warranting aggressive easing. For this market to gain probability, traders need to see Q1 and Q2 GDP turning negative, unemployment rising above 5%, and the Fed beginning a cutting cycle of at least 50 basis points per meeting by mid-2025. The FOMC's eight scheduled meetings in 2026 provide the maximum opportunities, meaning the Fed would need to cut at nearly every meeting except one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many FOMC meetings would need to include rate cuts for this market to resolve YES?

With eight scheduled FOMC meetings in 2026, nine cuts would require either multiple 50+ basis point cuts at certain meetings or an emergency intermeeting cut, as happened in March 2020. The Fed would need to cut at virtually every opportunity.

### What would the federal funds rate need to be at the start of 2026 for nine cuts to be possible?

The Fed would need rates at minimum 2.25% entering 2026 to have room for nine 25-basis-point cuts without going negative. This requires substantial cutting throughout 2025 from current 4.25-4.5% levels, which itself seems unlikely given current economic data.

### Has the Federal Reserve ever executed nine or more rate cuts in a single calendar year?

The Fed cut rates 10 times in 2001 during the dot-com recession and made aggressive cuts in 2007-2008 across the financial crisis, but such frequency is limited to severe economic emergencies, making this a tail-risk scenario rather than a baseline forecast.
