## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on potential Israeli or U.S.-Israeli military action against Iranian infrastructure following Iran's nuclear program advancement or regional proxy attacks. Kharg Island handles roughly 90% of Iran's crude oil exports, making it a strategic target if conflict escalates beyond current tit-for-tat strikes. Traders pointing to YES would cite the collapse of nuclear negotiations, increased Israeli rhetoric about striking economic targets rather than just nuclear sites, and potential U.S. policy shifts that could greenlight broader military operations. Any major Iranian attack on Israeli territory or U.S. assets in the region could serve as a catalyst for strikes on Kharg, particularly given the island's isolation 25 kilometers offshore and its concentration of oil terminals.

The bear case reflects the enormous strategic implications that make this scenario improbable. Seizing and holding Kharg would require sustained military presence, not just airstrikes—an amphibious or airborne operation well beyond typical Israeli capabilities without massive U.S. involvement. Iran would likely respond with attempts to close the Strait of Hormuz, triggering global oil price shocks that no major power currently wants. Russia and China, both dependent on Iranian oil flows, would likely intervene diplomatically or economically. Even maximum pressure campaigns have historically avoided outright seizure of sovereign Iranian territory, focusing instead on sanctions and targeted strikes.

Key catalysts to monitor include the IAEA Board of Governors meetings (quarterly, with the next major session in March 2025), any Iranian uranium enrichment announcements crossing weapons-grade thresholds, Israeli cabinet discussions on expanding target sets beyond current strikes on Syrian and Iraqi proxies, and U.S. congressional authorization debates around Iran military action. Watch for unusual naval positioning by U.S. or Israeli forces near Iranian waters, and any Iranian repositioning of oil export infrastructure as a defensive signal. The June 2025 Iranian presidential transition period could also present windows of vulnerability or escalation depending on hardliner versus reformist outcomes.

### How would oil markets react if Kharg Island came under attack, even without changing control?

Any successful Iranian opposition movement would more likely target Tehran before peripheral oil infrastructure.
