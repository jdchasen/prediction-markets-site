## Market Analysis

The bull case for a rate hike centers on persistent inflation or a reacceleration scenario. If core CPI remains stubbornly above 3% through 2025 despite rate cuts, or if fiscal stimulus drives demand beyond the economy's capacity, the Fed could be forced to reverse course and tighten again in 2026. A tight labor market with NFP consistently above 250K monthly and wage growth accelerating past 5% would support this scenario. Supply-side shocks—whether from geopolitical conflicts affecting energy markets or new pandemic variants disrupting supply chains—could also force the Fed's hand on inflation control over employment concerns.

The bear case, which the current odds reflect, assumes the Fed's restrictive policy eventually succeeds in bringing inflation to target, allowing a shift to rate cuts through 2024-2025. If core PCE inflation trends toward 2% and unemployment rises above 4.5%, the Fed would have room to ease significantly. The December 2024 and March 2025 FOMC meetings will be critical in establishing the cutting cycle's trajectory. Should the economy enter recession in 2025—signaled by negative GDP prints or unemployment surging past 5%—the likelihood of 2026 hikes essentially vanishes as the Fed would remain in easing mode.

Key catalysts include the January 15, 2025 CPI report and February 7, 2025 NFP data, which will shape expectations for how much the Fed cuts in the first half of 2025. The Fed's Summary of Economic Projections at the March 19, 2025 FOMC meeting will provide the first dot plot extending into 2026, offering direct insight into policymaker thinking. Traders should monitor whether core inflation remains range-bound between 2.5-3% or breaks decisively in either direction, and watch for any shift in the Fed's rhetoric from "data-dependent" to more explicit forward guidance about the 2026 policy stance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does this market resolve YES if the Fed raises rates even once during 2026, or does it require multiple hikes?

A single rate increase at any of the eight scheduled 2026 FOMC meetings would trigger a YES resolution. The market doesn't require multiple hikes or a sustained tightening campaign.

### How would a 2025 recession affect the probability of 2026 rate hikes?

A recession would likely drop the probability near zero, as the Fed would be cutting rates aggressively through 2025 and would need significant recovery and inflation reacceleration before considering hikes just months later in 2026.

### What inflation threshold would realistically force the Fed to hike in 2026 after previously cutting?

The Fed would likely need to see core PCE inflation back above 3.5-4% with clear upward momentum to justify reversing course so quickly, similar to the "stop-and-go" policy errors of the 1970s that policymakers explicitly want to avoid.
