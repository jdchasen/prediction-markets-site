## Market Analysis

The bear case for a 2026 hike centers on persistent inflation resurfacing after initial rate cuts. If core PCE inflation remains above the Fed's 2% target through 2024-2025, or if fiscal stimulus from a new administration reignites price pressures, the Fed could be forced to reverse course. Strong labor market resilience would support this scenario—sustained NFP prints above 200k and wage growth accelerating beyond 4% would give the FOMC cover to tighten again. Additionally, if the neutral rate proves higher than currently estimated (above 3%), the Fed might need to hike from what it initially considered a neutral stance.

The bull case for these low odds assumes the Fed successfully engineers a soft landing by mid-2025, with inflation settling durably near 2% and unemployment stabilizing around 4-4.5%. This path suggests the Fed will have cut rates significantly through 2024-2025, and by 2026 will be holding steady or making only minor adjustments. Recession risks in 2024-2025 would accelerate cuts and make 2026 hikes even less likely. Historical patterns show the Fed typically waits 2-3 years after completing a cutting cycle before hiking again.

Key catalysts include the January 29, 2025 FOMC decision and subsequent dot plot updates throughout 2025, which will signal the Fed's terminal rate expectations. The December 2024 and January 2025 CPI reports (released mid-January and mid-February) will be critical for assessing whether disinflation continues. Watch the February 2025 employment report (March 7 release) for signs of labor market cooling. The trajectory of the Fed funds rate through 2025—particularly whether cuts pause above 4% or continue to 3% or below—will heavily influence 2026 positioning. Any shift in FOMC member commentary about the neutral rate estimate would be significant for this market.

### How does this market resolve if the Fed raises rates in early 2026 but then cuts later in the same year?

### Which economic scenario makes a 2026 hike most probable despite the current low odds?
