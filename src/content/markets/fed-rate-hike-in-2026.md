## Market Analysis

The bull case for a 2026 rate hike centers on sticky inflation refusing to return to the Fed's 2% target despite near-term cuts. If core CPI readings throughout 2025 persistently print above 3%, and labor markets remain tight with NFP consistently above 200K, the Fed could be forced to reverse course and hike in 2026 after attempting premature easing. This scenario mirrors the stop-start monetary policy of the 1970s, where the Fed cut too early and had to resume hiking. Geopolitical shocks driving energy prices higher or renewed supply chain disruptions could also necessitate renewed tightening.

The bear case—which clearly dominates market pricing—assumes inflation continues its downward trajectory through 2025, allowing the Fed to cut rates to neutral or below without rekindling price pressures. Economic indicators would need to show CPI returning to the 2-2.5% range by mid-2025, with unemployment rising to 4.5% or higher, giving the Fed confidence that inflation is durably controlled. Most forecasters expect the federal funds rate to settle in the 3-3.5% range by late 2025, making a 2026 hike unnecessary unless severe stagflation emerges.

Key catalysts include the January 2025 CPI report (February 12), which will set the tone for whether disinflation is continuing or stalling. The March 2025 FOMC meeting (March 18-19) will provide updated dot plots showing where officials expect rates to be by end-2026. The December 2025 NFP report (January 2026) will be critical for assessing whether labor markets have achieved the soft landing necessary to keep rates on hold. Traders should monitor core PCE monthly readings—the Fed's preferred inflation gauge—with any three consecutive months above 0.3% dramatically increasing hike odds for 2026.
