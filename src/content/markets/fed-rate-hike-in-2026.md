## Market Analysis

The bull case for a rate hike hinges on persistent inflation resurgence or wage-price spiral dynamics. If core CPI remains above 3% through 2025 despite current Fed tightening, or if employment costs accelerate beyond the 4-5% range seen in recent Employment Cost Index reports, the FOMC could be forced to abandon its easing bias. A scenario where the Fed cuts rates in 2025 only to see inflation reignite—similar to the stop-start tightening of the 1970s—would make 2026 hikes necessary. Geopolitical shocks driving energy prices above $100/barrel or supply chain disruptions from trade policy changes could also force the Fed's hand.

The bear case, which the 19.5% odds reflect as dominant, assumes the Fed successfully engineers a soft landing with inflation converging toward the 2% target by mid-2025. December 2024's FOMC Summary of Economic Projections already showed median rate expectations trending downward for 2025-2026. If monthly CPI prints through Q1 2025 continue showing disinflation (next release February 12, 2025) and nonfarm payrolls moderate to 100-150k monthly additions without triggering recession, the Fed will likely cut 100-150 basis points through 2025 and hold steady in 2026. The CME FedWatch Tool currently shows markets pricing terminal rates in the 3.25-3.50% range by end-2025, well below current levels.

Critical catalysts include the January 29, 2025 FOMC meeting where updated dot plots will signal rate path expectations, and the February 12 CPI report which will set the tone for early-year inflation trends. The March and June 2025 employment reports (released first Friday of April and July) will be decisive for whether labor market cooling continues without cracking. Watch the Atlanta Fed's Wage Growth Tracker and the Cleveland Fed's inflation nowcasts for real-time signals. Any reading showing core PCE inflation—the Fed's preferred metric—accelerating above 2.5% in quarterly data through 2025 would dramatically shift these odds upward.
