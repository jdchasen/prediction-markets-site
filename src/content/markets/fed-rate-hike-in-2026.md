## Market Analysis

The bull case for a 2026 hike centers on sticky core services inflation, particularly shelter costs that lag by 12-18 months, and potential fiscal stimulus from deficit spending that could reignite price pressures in 2025-2026. If core PCE remains above 2.5% through Q4 2025 and labor markets stay tight with wage growth above 4%, the Fed could pause cuts and eventually resume hikes. The January 2026 FOMC meeting would be the earliest realistic inflection point if Q4 2025 CPI prints (released January 15, 2026) show reacceleration. A secondary catalyst would be unemployment falling back below 3.7% in 2025 jobs reports, forcing the Fed to reverse course on what markets currently expect to be 100+ basis points of cuts through 2025.

The bear case rests on the Fed's strong preference to avoid policy whiplash and the likelihood that any 2024-2025 rate cuts will only occur after convincing disinflation is established. If core CPI trends toward 2% by mid-2025 (monthly releases on the 12th-15th of each month will be critical), the Fed would need an extraordinary inflation shock to justify rate increases within months of ending cuts. Historical precedent shows the Fed typically pauses for extended periods before reversing direction—the 2004-2006 hiking cycle came after three years of steady rates. The December 2025 FOMC projections will be pivotal in signaling whether officials see any 2026 hike scenarios as credible.

Watch the May 2025 CPI report (released June 11, 2025) and subsequent Q2 GDP data as the clearest early warning system. If core inflation prints above 0.3% monthly for three consecutive months in spring 2025, this market should reprice significantly higher. The November 2025 NFP report (released December 5, 2025) will also be crucial as the final major data point before the market's resolution year begins. Traders should monitor not just the data but Fed speaker commentary following each FOMC meeting—any mention of "optionality" or "data dependence" regarding policy direction in 2026 would suggest higher probability than current pricing indicates.
