## Market Analysis

The bull case for a rate hike centers on persistent inflation resurging in 2025-2026, potentially driven by labor market tightness keeping core PCE elevated above the Fed's 2% target or commodity price shocks reigniting headline inflation. If monthly CPI and core PCE readings consistently exceed expectations through late 2025, and the unemployment rate remains below 4% while wage growth accelerates above 4% annually, the Fed could be forced to resume tightening even after an initial pause or cut cycle. Supply-side disruptions from geopolitical events or deglobalization trends could also necessitate higher rates despite Fed preferences.

The bear case, which current odds strongly favor, assumes the Fed successfully engineers a soft landing with inflation declining to target by mid-2025, allowing rate cuts to begin in the second half of 2025 or early 2026. This scenario sees core PCE inflation tracking steadily toward 2% throughout 2025, unemployment rising modestly to 4.5-5%, and weakening economic data that justifies easing rather than additional tightening. Historical patterns show the Fed rarely pauses rate cuts for long enough to restart hikes within such a compressed timeframe unless confronted with severe stagflation.

Key catalysts include the December 2024 and March 2025 FOMC meetings that will establish the Fed's terminal rate and initial guidance on the 2025-2026 path. Monthly CPI releases (next: January 14, 2025) and employment reports (next NFP: February 7, 2025) will be critical throughout 2025 for assessing whether inflation remains sticky. The February 2025 Monetary Policy Report to Congress will provide detailed Fed projections for the 2026 outlook. Traders should monitor the Fed's dot plot updates at each quarterly SEP release, particularly June and September 2025, which will show whether any FOMC members project rate increases for 2026. Core PCE consistently printing above 2.5% in Q2-Q3 2025 would significantly increase hike probability, while readings at or below 2.2% would reinforce the current low odds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What specific inflation threshold would make a 2026 rate hike likely?

Core PCE inflation persistently above 3% through the second half of 2025, combined with rising inflation expectations in consumer surveys and market-based measures like 5-year breakevens exceeding 2.5%, would likely force the Fed to consider resuming hikes in 2026.

### Could the Fed hike rates in 2026 even if they cut rates in 2025?

Yes, though historically rare—the Fed could cut in response to a mild 2025 slowdown, then resume hiking if inflation proves more persistent than expected or if the economy reaccelerates unexpectedly, similar to the stop-and-go patterns seen in the 1970s.

### How many rate cuts would the Fed need to implement before 2026 to make a 2026 hike realistic?

If the Fed cuts 100-150 basis points in 2025 and inflation subsequently rebounds to 3.5%+ by late 2025, a 2026 hike becomes plausible as the Fed would have room to reverse course, though this scenario contradicts the current low market probability.
