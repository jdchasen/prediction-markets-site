## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on accelerating signs of succession planning within Iran's power structure. Reports from late 2024 indicated that the Assembly of Experts—the 88-member clerical body responsible for selecting the Supreme Leader—held confidential discussions about Mojtaba as the successor. He has systematically accumulated influence within the Revolutionary Guard and reportedly played a key role in the violent suppression of the 2009 Green Movement protests, demonstrating his willingness to use force to maintain regime stability. His father's advanced age and reported health issues create urgency around succession planning, and insiders suggest the elder Khamenei prefers a trusted family member to maintain ideological continuity. The regime may view a quick, controlled transition as preferable to a prolonged power struggle that could expose internal divisions.

The bear case centers on the profound illegitimacy such a move would represent within Iran's political framework. The Islamic Republic explicitly rejected monarchical succession after the 1979 revolution, and installing the Supreme Leader's son would contradict foundational revolutionary principles. This could trigger factional resistance from rival clerical networks, pragmatic conservatives, and reformist elements within the Assembly of Experts. Previous speculation about Mojtaba's rise has generated internal backlash, and several senior clerics have privately expressed opposition to dynastic succession. Alternative candidates with stronger religious credentials and broader consensus support—including President Masoud Pezeshkian or hardline clerics like Ebrahim Raisi's allies—present viable alternatives. The 2026 timeline also requires Ali Khamenei's death or incapacitation within two years, which, while plausible given his age, remains uncertain.

Critical catalysts include any announcements regarding Ali Khamenei's health status and meetings of the Assembly of Experts, which typically convene twice annually in March and September. The next Assembly elections are scheduled for 2024, and any moves to pack this body with Mojtaba loyalists would signal intensifying succession preparations. Watch for Mojtaba's public profile—he traditionally operates behind the scenes, so increased visibility or formal title appointments would indicate an active succession campaign. Regional instability, including Israel-Iran tensions and domestic unrest over economic conditions, could either accelerate a managed transition or create conditions where a controversial hereditary succession becomes politically untenable.
