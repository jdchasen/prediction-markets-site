## Market Analysis

The bull case for four cuts materializing requires a significant economic deterioration starting in late 2025 or early 2026. If core PCE inflation falls below the Fed's 2% target while unemployment rises above 4.5-5%, the FOMC would have room to cut aggressively. A financial stability event—commercial real estate collapse, banking stress, or credit crunch—could force the Fed's hand regardless of inflation levels. The 2026 FOMC meetings scheduled for January 28-29, March 17-18, May 5-6, June 16-17, July 28-29, September 15-16, October 27-28, and December 15-16 provide eight opportunities for cuts, making four technically feasible if conditions warrant.

The bear case, which the market strongly favors, assumes the Fed successfully engineers a soft landing with inflation stabilizing near target and unemployment remaining historically low. If 2025 CPI and NFP reports through December continue showing resilient labor markets (monthly job gains above 150k) and sticky services inflation around 3-3.5%, the Fed will likely hold rates elevated. The December 2025 FOMC projections and Summary of Economic Projections will be critical—if the median dot plot shows only 1-2 cuts for 2026, this market's probability will compress further.

Key catalysts include the January 2026 CPI release (around February 12) and NFP data (February 6), which will set the tone for early-year policy. The March 2026 FOMC meeting will be the first decision point where traders can gauge if the cutting cycle accelerates. Watch for any Q4 2025 GDP weakness reported in late January 2026, as consecutive quarters below 1% growth would substantially increase odds. The Fed's semi-annual Monetary Policy Report to Congress in February and July 2026 will provide transparency on their reaction function to evolving data.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Would three rate cuts in 2026 still count as a "NO" outcome for this market?

Yes, the market specifically requires four rate cuts. Three cuts, even if they total 75 basis points, would resolve as NO.

### If the Fed cuts 50 basis points at one meeting, does that count as one cut or two toward the total?

Standard interpretation counts each FOMC meeting action as one cut regardless of magnitude, so a 50bp reduction would count as a single cut, requiring three more meetings with cuts to reach four total.

### What historical precedent exists for the Fed implementing exactly four cuts in a calendar year?

The Fed cut rates four times in 1995 (a soft landing year), three times in 1998 (LTCM crisis), and began aggressive cycles in 2001 and 2007 that exceeded four cuts, making four cuts unusual outside of either mild slowdowns or the beginning of recession responses.
