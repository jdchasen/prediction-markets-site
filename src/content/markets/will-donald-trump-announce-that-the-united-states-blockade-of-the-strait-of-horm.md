## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on escalatory dynamics that could plausibly unfold over the next two years. If Iran significantly accelerates its nuclear enrichment program beyond weapons-grade levels or attacks American forces or allies in the region, a Trump administration might respond with a blockade to economically strangle Iran without full-scale invasion. Trump's maximum pressure campaign during his first term and his recent rhetoric about preventing Iranian nuclear weapons suggest he could consider such measures. The market timeframe extends through April 2026, providing ample opportunity for crisis escalation, implementation of a blockade in late 2025 or early 2026, and a subsequent announcement of its lifting following negotiations or capitulation.

The bear case is straightforward: implementing a Strait of Hormuz blockade would be an extraordinary act tantamount to economic warfare, threatening roughly 20% of global oil supply and risking direct military confrontation with Iran. Such a move would spike oil prices globally, alienate allies, and potentially trigger broader Middle East conflict. Even a Trump administration would face massive pushback from defense and economic advisors, while China and Europe would likely oppose it diplomatically. The historical record shows the U.S. has avoided this option even during peak tensions, preferring sanctions and covert operations.

Key catalysts to monitor include IAEA reports on Iran's nuclear program (typically quarterly), any Iranian attacks on shipping or U.S. bases in the region, and Trump's cabinet appointments—particularly Secretary of Defense and National Security Advisor—which will signal his administration's appetite for confrontation. The first year of Trump's term through 2025 will be critical for establishing policy direction, while any actual blockade would likely require a precipitating crisis event visible weeks in advance through military positioning and diplomatic warnings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does this market require an actual blockade to occur, or just Trump's announcement that one has been lifted?

The market resolves YES only if Trump announces a blockade has been lifted, which necessarily implies one was implemented first. A false claim without an actual blockade wouldn't constitute a legitimate announcement of lifting one.

### What would constitute a "blockade" versus normal freedom of navigation operations in the Strait?

A blockade would involve the U.S. Navy actively preventing Iranian oil exports by stopping and inspecting vessels, representing an act of war under international law. This differs fundamentally from routine patrols or escorts that don't impede Iranian commerce.

### Why does the market extend to April 2026 specifically rather than the end of Trump's term?

The timeframe likely reflects a realistic scenario where a blockade could be imposed in response to a 2025 crisis and then lifted relatively quickly through negotiations, rather than extending for years. A blockade would be economically unsustainable for extended periods given global oil market impacts.
