## Market Analysis

The bear case for rate cuts centers on structurally elevated inflation driven by deglobalization, labor market tightness, and fiscal dominance. If core PCE inflation remains above 2.5% through 2025, the Fed will lack the justification to ease policy regardless of growth concerns. December 2024's CPI report (due January 15) and subsequent monthly releases will establish the inflation trajectory. Strong nonfarm payroll prints above 150,000—the next release comes February 7, 2025—would reinforce the "no landing" scenario where the economy runs hot without disinflationary progress. The Fed's March 2025 Summary of Economic Projections will provide critical dot plot guidance on the terminal rate path, potentially signaling a higher-for-longer regime that eliminates any 2025-2026 cutting cycle.

The bull case for cuts requires a sharp economic deterioration that forces the Fed's hand despite inflation concerns. A recession triggered by commercial real estate losses, geopolitical shocks, or consumer credit deterioration could push unemployment above 5%, compelling emergency rate relief. Watch the ISM Manufacturing PMI (released monthly, first business day) and services PMI—sustained readings below 45 would signal severe contraction. Weekly initial jobless claims breaking above 250,000 for consecutive weeks would be an early warning. The FOMC's January 2025 meeting on the 28-29th will clarify whether the pause announced in December represents a true policy pivot or merely a temporary hold.

Key catalysts include the January 29, 2025 FOMC decision and Powell's press conference rhetoric on the inflation-employment tradeoff, March 2025's SEP update, and quarterly GDP prints throughout 2025. The Fed's preferred metric, core PCE (released monthly, last Friday), must show consecutive prints near 2% to open the door for eventual cuts. Credit market stress indicators like widening BBB spreads or inverted term spreads reversing sharply would signal economic fragility that could accelerate the timeline, though current market pricing suggests traders see virtually no scenario bringing cuts by March 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What would need to happen for this market to move significantly toward YES?

Unemployment would need to spike above 5% with multiple consecutive months of job losses exceeding 100,000, combined with core PCE falling below 2.2% for at least two quarters, creating conditions where the Fed prioritizes employment over inflation concerns.

### How many rate cuts would the Fed need to execute to reach March 2026, and from what starting point?

The market implies cuts FROM the current federal funds rate target range; traders are betting the Fed either maintains current rates or potentially hikes further, making any cut by March 2026 extremely unlikely regardless of magnitude.

### Why is the probability so low when March 2026 is over a year away?

The extended timeline actually works against cuts—markets expect inflation to remain sticky throughout 2025, and the Fed has signaled a multi-year restrictive stance, meaning even 14+ months provides insufficient time for the economic deterioration that would necessitate emergency easing.
