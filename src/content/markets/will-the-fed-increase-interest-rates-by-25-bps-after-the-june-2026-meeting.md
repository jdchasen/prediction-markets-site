## Market Analysis

The bear case for a rate increase centers on a soft landing scenario where the Fed successfully brings inflation back to its 2% target throughout 2024-2025, allowing unemployment to stabilize in the 4-4.5% range without triggering recession. In this environment, the Fed would likely pause at a neutral rate somewhere in the 3-3.5% range by late 2025 or early 2026, making a June 2026 hike unnecessary. Markets currently expect multiple cuts through 2024-2025 based on futures pricing, which would need to reverse entirely for this outcome. The bull case requires either persistent inflation that proves stickier than anticipated—with core PCE remaining above 2.5% through 2025—or an unexpected economic reacceleration in late 2025 that forces the Fed to restart tightening after an initial easing phase. This would mirror the stop-start cycles of the 1970s and require a significant reassessment of the neutral rate.

Key catalysts include the FOMC meetings throughout 2024-2025, particularly the December 2024 Summary of Economic Projections (due December 18, 2024) and March 2025 SEP (March 19, 2025), which will reveal dot plot projections extending into 2026. Monthly CPI and PCE releases through 2025 will be critical—particularly January 2026 CPI (released mid-February 2026) and the subsequent months leading to the June decision. NFP reports showing sustained job growth above 200K combined with wage growth exceeding 4% would support the hike scenario, while labor market softening with unemployment rising above 4.5% would effectively eliminate it. The May 2026 FOMC meeting (likely May 5-6, 2026) will provide the final signal before the June decision.

Traders should monitor the Fed's reaction function to incoming data, particularly whether 2024-2025 rate cuts begin in Q1 or Q2 2024 and their pace. If the Fed cuts below 4% by mid-2025 and inflation rebounds above 3%, this market's probability could surge to 20-30%. Conversely, if core PCE settles durably at 2% by Q4 2024, the odds will likely compress further toward 2-3%. The March 2026 CPI reading (released April 10, 2026) represents the last major data point that could realistically shift June 2026 expectations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What would need to happen between now and June 2026 for a rate hike to become likely?

The Fed would need to cut rates substantially in 2024-2025, followed by an inflation resurgence with core PCE climbing back above 2.5-3% in late 2025 or early 2026, forcing a reversal of the easing cycle.

### How does this market relate to current Fed funds futures pricing for 2025-2026?

Fed funds futures currently price the policy rate around 3-3.5% by late 2025, implying cuts from current levels. For a June 2026 hike, rates would need to be low enough that hiking makes sense, yet inflation high enough to justify tightening—a narrow scenario.

### What's the difference between this outcome and the Fed simply holding rates steady in June 2026?

This market specifically requires an active 25 basis point increase, not a pause. The low probability reflects that even if the Fed hasn't cut as much as expected, maintaining existing rates is far more likely than actively hiking at that point in the cycle.
