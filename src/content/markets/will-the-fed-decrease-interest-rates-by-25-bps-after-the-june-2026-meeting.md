## Market Analysis

The bull case for a June 2026 cut requires an unusual scenario: either the Fed pauses its easing cycle in 2025 due to persistent inflation, leaving rates elevated into 2026, or economic conditions deteriorate in early 2026 requiring renewed cuts. If core PCE inflation remains above 3% through 2025, the Fed might maintain restrictive policy into 2026, keeping cuts on the table. A sharp recession beginning in Q1 2026 could also justify continued easing. The bear case—which the market strongly favors—assumes the Fed completes its cutting cycle by late 2025 or early 2026, reaching a neutral rate around 3-3.5%. Once at neutral, the Fed would hold steady barring significant economic shocks, making a June 2026 cut unnecessary.

Key catalysts include the March 2025 FOMC meeting (March 18-19) and subsequent dot plot, which will clarify the Fed's expected terminal rate and timing. Monthly CPI and employment reports through 2025 will determine whether disinflation continues on track—particularly the April 2026 CPI release (May 13, 2026) and May NFP data (June 6, 2026) immediately preceding the June FOMC. If unemployment spikes above 5% in early 2026 or core inflation unexpectedly reaccelerates above 3.5% in late 2025, probabilities could shift materially.

The market's near-zero pricing suggests traders view June 2026 as too late in any plausible easing cycle. For odds to reach even 20%, we'd need evidence by late 2025 that the Fed significantly miscalculated either the neutral rate or inflation persistence. Watch the September 2025 FOMC Summary of Economic Projections for revised longer-run rate estimates, and Q4 2025 GDP growth—sustained weakness below 1% would increase the probability of extended easing into mid-2026.
