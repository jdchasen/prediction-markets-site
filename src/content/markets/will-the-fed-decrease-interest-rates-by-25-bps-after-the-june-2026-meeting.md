## Market Analysis

The bear case for a rate cut centers on persistent structural inflation and a resilient labor market. If core PCE inflation remains above the Fed's 2% target through 2025-2026, the central bank will lack justification for easing. Strong nonfarm payroll reports averaging above 150,000 monthly jobs and wage growth sustaining above 4% would indicate an economy running too hot for accommodation. Additionally, if fiscal stimulus from government spending continues expanding the deficit, the Fed may need to maintain restrictive policy to counteract inflationary pressures. The FOMC's own dot plot projections from upcoming meetings in March 2025, May 2025, and September 2025 will signal their longer-term rate trajectory expectations.

The bull case requires a significant economic deterioration between now and mid-2026. A recession beginning in late 2025 or early 2026, marked by consecutive negative GDP quarters and unemployment rising above 5%, would create conditions for aggressive easing. Disinflation accelerating to the point where core CPI falls below 2% on an annualized basis would give the Fed room to cut. A financial stability event—such as regional banking stress or credit market dysfunction—could force the Fed's hand regardless of inflation data. Traders should monitor the May 2026 FOMC meeting scheduled for May 5-6, which would provide the final policy decision and forward guidance before the June meeting.

Key catalysts include the monthly CPI and employment reports throughout 2025 and early 2026, particularly the April 2026 jobs report (released early May) and May 2026 CPI data (released mid-June, potentially after the June 16-17 FOMC meeting). The Fed's Summary of Economic Projections released quarterly will show whether officials anticipate cuts in their 2026 forecasts. Treasury yield curve dynamics matter significantly—an inverted curve persisting through 2025 historically precedes recession and eventual rate cuts within 12-18 months.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is the probability so low for a cut at this specific June 2026 meeting rather than just "sometime in 2026"?

The market is pricing the probability of a 25 basis point cut at this particular meeting, not cumulative cuts throughout 2026. Even if the Fed cuts rates earlier in 2026, that wouldn't satisfy this market's conditions.

### How would cuts before June 2026 affect this market's probability?

Prior rate cuts would actually decrease the likelihood of an additional 25 bps cut in June, as the Fed typically cuts in measured cycles. If they've already eased significantly by June, they may pause to assess economic impacts.

### What's the significance of the 25 bps specification versus other cut sizes?

The Fed typically moves in 25 bps increments during normal policy adjustments, but could cut 50 bps in crisis scenarios. This market only resolves YES for exactly a 25 basis point reduction, not larger emergency cuts.
