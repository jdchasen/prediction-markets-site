## Market Analysis

The bear case is straightforward: Han resigned as PPP leader in December 2024 amid internal party warfare over his handling of Yoon's martial law crisis, losing his power base and facing accusations of betrayal from pro-Yoon factions that still dominate conservative politics. Gyeonggi Province, South Korea's most populous region surrounding Seoul, has trended Democratic Party in recent cycles, and Han would need to secure the PPP nomination while simultaneously rebuilding credibility with a conservative base that largely views him as disloyal. The Democratic Party currently holds strong positioning in the province, and any conservative candidate faces an uphill battle given the association with Yoon's constitutional crisis. Han's political resurrection would require navigating a hostile PPP primary process against better-positioned rivals, likely in early 2026.

The bull case depends on a dramatic political realignment where Han successfully rebrands as a reformist conservative who opposed Yoon's authoritarian overreach—potentially appealing to moderate swing voters in Gyeonggi if the Democratic Party nominates a polarizing candidate. If the PPP fragments or seeks distance from Yoon's legacy by late 2025, Han could position himself as the face of conservative renewal. He maintains name recognition and demonstrated some political courage during the impeachment crisis, which could resonate with voters seeking accountability. A unity candidacy or coalition framework might create an opening if traditional party structures break down.

Key catalysts include the Constitutional Court's impeachment ruling on Yoon (expected by mid-2025), which will determine the political environment for all conservatives, and the PPP's internal restructuring and primary schedule for Gyeonggi Province, typically held 3-4 months before the June election. Watch whether Han attempts to contest the PPP primary or explores an independent run, and monitor Democratic Party candidate selection in spring 2026. Polling data on conservative vote consolidation and Han's personal favorability ratings in Gyeonggi will be critical indicators of whether these odds should move at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why would Han Dong-hoon run for Gyeonggi governor given his recent resignation as party leader?

Gubernatorial races offer a path for sidelined national politicians to rebuild credibility and create independent power bases, though Han has not indicated interest in this specific race and may be pursuing other political opportunities or temporary retirement.

### What would need to happen for the PPP to nominate Han despite his conflict with pro-Yoon factions?

The party would need to decisively break from Yoon's legacy following the impeachment verdict and conclude that only a reformist candidate can compete in Gyeonggi—an unlikely scenario given current factional dynamics and the strength of Yoon loyalists in party structures.

### Could Han run as an independent or third-party candidate instead of through the PPP?

While theoretically possible, South Korea's political system heavily favors major party candidates, and an independent conservative run would likely split the right-wing vote, guaranteeing a Democratic Party victory and making such a campaign strategically pointless.
