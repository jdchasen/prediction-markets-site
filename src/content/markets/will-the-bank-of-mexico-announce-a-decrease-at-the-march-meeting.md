## Market Analysis

The bull case for a rate cut centers on Mexico's inflation trajectory and economic cooling. If headline inflation continues declining toward Banxico's 3% target through early 2026, and core inflation shows sustained moderation, the central bank will have room to cut rates further. Weaker economic growth indicators, particularly in manufacturing and consumption tied to U.S. demand, would strengthen the case for accommodative policy. Banxico has historically been willing to ease when inflation expectations remain anchored, and if the Federal Reserve maintains a dovish stance through 2025-2026, this removes pressure on Banxico to keep rates elevated to defend the peso.

The bear case rests on persistent inflation stickiness or renewed price pressures from wage growth, energy costs, or peso depreciation. Services inflation in Mexico has proven stubborn, and if core CPI remains above 4% heading into March 2026, Banxico may pause its easing cycle. Political uncertainty surrounding Mexico's fiscal policy under the Sheinbaum administration, particularly regarding Pemex's financial position and budget deficits, could trigger currency weakness that forces Banxico to maintain higher rates. External shocks from U.S. trade policy changes or commodity price spikes would also argue for holding rates steady.

Key catalysts include Banxico's February 2026 meeting decision (approximately February 13, 2026), which will signal the bank's near-term stance, and the late February/early March inflation reports that directly inform the March decision. Traders should monitor Mexico's January and February CPI releases, typically published mid-month, along with Banxico Governor Victoria Rodríguez Ceja's public statements. The Federal Reserve's March 2026 FOMC meeting (around March 17-18) could influence Banxico's calculus on the peso's stability. Mexico's Q4 2025 GDP figures, expected in late February 2026, will clarify whether economic weakness justifies continued easing.
