## Market Analysis

The bear case (supporting the current low odds) rests on Mojtaba's entrenchment in Iran's power structure. As a senior cleric reportedly wielding significant influence within the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and serving in his father's office, he has spent decades building domestic power networks. His father, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, is 85 years old, making the succession question increasingly urgent. Mojtaba's departure would mean forfeiting what many analysts view as the inside track to becoming Iran's next Supreme Leader. Iranian insiders who leave the country typically face severe consequences, including asset seizure and endangerment of family members. The regime's tight control over elite movement, particularly for figures of Mojtaba's stature, makes voluntary departure extraordinarily unlikely.

The bull case requires envisioning extreme scenarios: a factional power struggle where Mojtaba loses catastrophically and faces arrest or execution, forcing him into exile; a complete collapse of the Islamic Republic's governing structure amid widespread protests (similar to those following Mahsa Amini's death in 2022, but more successful); or credible assassination threats that make remaining in Iran untenable. Iran's economic deterioration, with inflation exceeding 40% in recent years and continued Western sanctions, could theoretically destabilize the regime enough to fracture elite consensus. If Ayatollah Khamenei dies before March 2026 and rival factions in the Assembly of Experts select a different successor, Mojtaba could face prosecution for alleged corruption or abuse of power.

Key catalysts to monitor include the Supreme Leader's health status and any emergency meetings of the Assembly of Experts, which would signal succession maneuvering. Escalation in regional conflicts—particularly between Israel and Iranian proxies—could trigger internal Iranian power struggles. Watch for unusual travel by Mojtaba's family members, changes in his public appearances or IRGC command structures, and any reporting from Iranian opposition sources about factional conflicts. The March 2026 Iranian parliamentary elections could reveal shifting power dynamics, though the Guardian Council's vetting process typically prevents genuine opposition participation.
