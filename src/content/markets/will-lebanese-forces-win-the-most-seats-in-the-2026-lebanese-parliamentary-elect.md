## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Lebanon's deepening economic collapse and shifting sectarian dynamics following the October 2023 Gaza conflict and subsequent Hezbollah-Israel clashes. The Lebanese Forces, led by Samir Geagea, could capitalize on growing frustration with Hezbollah's role in dragging Lebanon into regional conflicts, particularly if the current ceasefire collapses or reconstruction aid flows primarily through government channels that favor reformist parties. Christian voters may consolidate further behind the LF if they perceive the Free Patriotic Movement continues weakening, while Sunni voters lacking strong leadership could tactically support LF candidates in mixed districts. Recent municipal election results in Christian heartlands showed increasing LF dominance, suggesting momentum that could translate nationally.

The bear case acknowledges Lebanon's confessional electoral system fundamentally limits Christian party ceiling for total seats. Hezbollah and Amal retain ironclad control over most Shia seats (27 guaranteed), while the FPM still commands significant Christian support despite losses. The 2018 proportional representation law favors established blocs with cross-sectarian alliances, where the LF lacks strong Sunni or Druze partner parties. Economic desperation may actually benefit Hezbollah's patronage networks rather than harm them, as demonstrated by the party maintaining its seat count in 2022 despite the lira's collapse. Mass emigration has depleted the reform-minded voter base that might otherwise support the LF.

Key catalysts include the presidential election process (currently vacant since October 2022), formation of any new electoral alliances by March 2026, and whether international sanctions on Hezbollah tighten following reconstruction debates. Traders should monitor voter registration deadlines in early 2026, any amendments to the electoral law that Parliament might pass, and whether economic conditions stabilize enough to reduce protest voting against the establishment—paradoxically helping the anti-Hezbollah opposition consolidate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many seats does the Lebanese Forces need to win to resolve this market as YES?

They need to win more seats than any other single party in the 128-seat parliament. In 2022, no party won more than Hezbollah's estimated 13-15 seats as a single party, though blocs like the Shia Duo (Hezbollah-Amal) won far more collectively.

### Could the Free Patriotic Movement splitting the Christian vote prevent the Lebanese Forces from winning the most seats even in a favorable scenario?

Yes, this is a critical risk factor—the FPM won approximately 18 seats in 2022 versus LF's 19, and if Christian votes remain divided while Hezbollah and Amal maintain unity in Shia districts, the LF may win the most Christian seats but not the most seats overall.

### What role does the ongoing presidential vacuum play in this market's outcome?

The selection of a president before the election could significantly reshape alliances and momentum; an LF-aligned president would boost their credibility, while a Hezbollah-backed president would likely strengthen their bloc's coordination and potentially discourage LF-leaning swing voters in mixed districts.
