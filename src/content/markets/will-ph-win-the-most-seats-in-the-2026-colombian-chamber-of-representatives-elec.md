## Market Analysis

**Bull Case**: The Pacto Histórico maintains organizational momentum from President Gustavo Petro's 2022 victory and controls the government apparatus heading into the campaign. Colombia's proportional representation system with multiple regional districts favors established coalitions with national reach. The opposition remains divided among traditional parties (Conservative, Liberal), the right-wing Centro Democrático, and newer movements, making it mathematically difficult for any single faction to surpass PH's seat count even if the left loses overall vote share. Recent local and regional elections have shown PH's voter mobilization network remains functional, particularly in urban centers like Bogotá and among younger demographics who turned out strongly in 2022.

**Bear Case**: Petro's approval ratings have fluctuated significantly during his presidency, dipping below 40% in several 2024 polls amid concerns over security and economic management. Legislative elections typically punish governing parties in Latin America, especially when inflation and security concerns dominate voter priorities. The Colombian opposition could potentially coordinate around a unified electoral vehicle or alliance specifically to counter PH's dominance, similar to pre-electoral coalitions seen in Chile and Peru. If centro-right forces consolidate behind former Medellín mayor Federico Gutiérrez's movement or a renewed Liberal Party push, the fragmented opposition dynamic could shift dramatically in the 12 months before the March 2026 vote.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What exactly constitutes the Pacto Histórico coalition for this election, and could parties leave before 2026?

The PH core includes Colombia Humana, Unión Patriótica, and MAIS, but the coalition's exact composition for 2026 isn't finalized. Smaller allied parties could defect or rebrand if Petro's popularity declines further, potentially affecting whether their seats count toward PH's total.

### How does Colombia's Chamber electoral system affect the likelihood of PH winning a plurality versus a majority?

Colombia uses proportional representation across 33 electoral districts with varying seat allocations, making it nearly impossible for any party to win an outright majority but favorable for the largest organized coalition to claim plurality. PH would need only to win more seats than any other single party or coalition, not necessarily 50%+.

### What happened in the 2022 Chamber elections that informs these odds?

PH won just 27 of 188 seats in 2022 despite Petro's presidential victory, with the Liberal Party taking 32 seats, meaning the market is betting on either PH growth or opposition fragmentation preventing Liberals or Centro Democrático from surpassing them in 2026.
