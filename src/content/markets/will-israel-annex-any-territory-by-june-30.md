## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Israel's current government coalition, which includes far-right parties explicitly committed to annexing parts of the West Bank. Finance Minister Bezalel Smolitch and National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir have repeatedly called for sovereignty over Judea and Samaria, and the ongoing security situation in Gaza could provide political cover for unilateral moves in the West Bank. A second Trump administration, if elected in November 2024, might offer tacit approval or at least minimal opposition, similar to the 2020 recognition of Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights. Regional normalization efforts with Saudi Arabia could theoretically proceed with annexation as a fait accompli if the Saudis prioritize their strategic alignment with Washington and Tel Aviv over Palestinian statehood.

The bear case is considerably stronger given multiple hard constraints. The Biden administration has explicitly opposed annexation and conditioned aspects of security cooperation on territorial restraint, while even a Republican successor would likely face Congressional pushback on supporting overt annexation. The EU has indicated it would impose economic consequences, and Israel's Supreme Court could challenge the legality of annexation under existing framework laws. More critically, Israel's security establishment has consistently warned that annexation would collapse security coordination with the Palestinian Authority, potentially trigger a third intifada, and jeopardize normalization with additional Arab states. Netanyahu himself shelved annexation plans in 2020 when faced with Emirati opposition, demonstrating how regional dynamics constrain action.

Key catalysts include Israel's next scheduled elections (none currently mandated before 2026, but coalition instability could trigger early voting), any formal Saudi-Israeli normalization agreement negotiations in 2024-2025, and the November 2024 U.S. presidential election. Traders should monitor Knesset legislative calendars for sovereignty bills, statements from the UAE and Bahrain regarding their treaty commitments, and International Criminal Court proceedings that could either embolden or restrain Israeli actions. The timeline extending to mid-2026 means multiple government turnovers in Israel remain possible, with polls currently showing opposition leader Yair Lapid and Benny Gantz significantly ahead of Netanyahu's coalition, and both oppose unilateral annexation.
