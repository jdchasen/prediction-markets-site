## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on the current Israeli government composition and shifting geopolitical dynamics. Netanyahu's coalition depends on far-right parties like Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism and Itamar Ben-Gvir's Jewish Power, both of which explicitly advocate for annexing parts of the West Bank. The Trump administration's previous recognition of Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights and potential return of Trump-aligned officials creates a window where international pushback might be muted. Finance Minister Smotrich has already announced plans to prepare infrastructure for annexation in Area C, and the ongoing war in Gaza has shifted Israeli public opinion rightward on territorial issues. A normalization deal with Saudi Arabia could theoretically provide cover for limited annexation as part of a broader regional agreement.

The bear case emphasizes the substantial international and domestic obstacles to formal annexation. Even with a potentially sympathetic U.S. administration, European allies would likely impose economic sanctions, and key Arab states including Egypt and Jordan have indicated such a move would rupture diplomatic relations. The Israeli military establishment and security services have consistently opposed annexation due to operational complications and the risk of sparking a third intifada. Economically, Israel faces pressure to maintain its credit rating and tech sector investments, both vulnerable to international backlash. Legal challenges would tie up any annexation attempt in Israeli courts for months, and the definition of "annex" in this market matters—continued de facto control and settlement expansion differs from formal legal annexation requiring Knesset legislation.

Key catalysts include the formation of any new Israeli government following potential coalition collapse (Netanyahu's approval ratings remain below 40%), U.S. presidential election results in November 2024 and the subsequent policy direction through 2025-2026, and progress on Saudi-Israel normalization talks which appear stalled. Traders should monitor Knesset committee votes on sovereignty bills, particularly any movement from Smotrich's office on applying Israeli civil law to settlements. The International Criminal Court proceedings against Israeli officials may paradoxically reduce annexation likelihood by increasing legal exposure, or alternatively push the government toward more aggressive territorial moves if isolation appears inevitable regardless. Settlement construction data released quarterly by Peace Now provides leading indicators of government intentions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Would applying Israeli civil law to existing settlements count as annexation for this market?

This depends on the market's specific resolution criteria, but typically yes—extending Israeli civilian law and administration to West Bank settlements would constitute de facto annexation even without explicitly declaring sovereignty. The 2020 proposed annexation plan focused on precisely this mechanism.

### How does the Gaza conflict affect annexation probability in the West Bank?

The Gaza war simultaneously creates political cover for bold moves while consuming military and diplomatic resources that annexation would require. Extended conflict reduces bandwidth for West Bank annexation, but a decisive Israeli victory could embolden the government to act before international attention refocuses.

### What specific territory is most likely to be annexed if this resolves YES?

Major settlement blocs like Maale Adumim, Ariel, and Gush Etzion are most probable, as they have broad Israeli consensus including center-left support, contain over 400,000 settlers, and were included in previous annexation proposals. The Jordan Valley is also possible due to its strategic value to security hawks.
