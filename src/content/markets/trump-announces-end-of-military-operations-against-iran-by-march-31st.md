## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Trump's historical pattern of military restraint despite aggressive rhetoric, as seen in his first term when he authorized the Soleimani strike in January 2020 but avoided broader conflict. Trump has consistently campaigned on ending foreign wars rather than starting new ones, and any military action initiated in early 2025 could face domestic political pressure as the 2026 midterms approach in November. Additionally, if operations begin as limited strikes or drone campaigns, Trump could declare mission accomplished and announce their conclusion within this timeframe to claim a foreign policy victory. The resolution wording only requires an announcement of ending operations, not proof that no military activities continue.

The bear case rests on the ambiguity of what constitutes "military operations" and whether they'll formally begin at all. If tensions with Iran remain at the level of proxy conflicts, sanctions, or isolated incidents rather than a declared military campaign, there would be nothing to officially end by March 2026. Furthermore, if substantive operations do commence—particularly following an Iranian attack on U.S. forces or allies—the military and political establishment may resist a rapid withdrawal that could be framed as weakness. The timeline extends beyond the critical first 14 months of Trump's term when he'll have maximum political capital, but ends before the midterms create maximum pressure to wind down conflicts.

Key catalysts include Trump's January 20, 2025 inauguration and any immediate Iran policy announcements, the potential reimposition of "maximum pressure" sanctions in Q1 2025, and any Iranian responses to U.S. actions in February-April 2025. Israel's ongoing regional conflicts and potential strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities could force Trump's hand on military engagement. Traders should monitor defense authorization bills in Congress (typically finalized by December each year), any Authorization for Use of Military Force (AUMF) proposals specific to Iran, and Trump's rhetoric shifts around the August 2025-January 2026 period when midterm campaign dynamics begin intensifying. The State of the Union addresses in 2025 and 2026 will provide crucial indicators of how Trump frames any Iran military posture.
