A catalyzing event could be a repeat of the January-style drone attacks that Jordan intercepted, but with successful strikes on civilian infrastructure or US military facilities at Al-Tanf or Prince Hassan Air Base. The bear case emphasizes Jordan's consistent policy of strategic restraint and dependence on Iranian economic corridors for trade despite security concerns. The kingdom's domestic stability depends on maintaining its role as a mediator rather than combatant, with Palestinian-majority populations unlikely to support military adventurism. No Jordanian mobilization indicators have appeared, and the March 31 deadline leaves minimal time for the diplomatic breakdown and military preparation that would precede such action.

Key monitoring points include any incidents along the Jordan-Syria or Jordan-Iraq borders, particularly involving US forces, and readouts from King Abdullah's Washington meetings. The February 20 Arab League ministerial meeting in Cairo may reveal whether Jordan seeks regional backing for a harder Iran stance. Satellite imagery of Jordanian air bases and munitions movements would provide concrete deployment signals, while any upgrade to the US-Jordan Status of Forces Agreement could indicate expanded mission parameters.

### What specific Iranian targets would be within Jordan's operational reach?
