## Market Analysis

The bear case is straightforward and explains the current pricing. Sa'ar leads the New Hope party, which holds only four Knesset seats and polls consistently below the electoral threshold. He rejoined Netanyahu's government in September 2024 after previously breaking away from Likud, a move that damaged his credibility as an independent alternative. Netanyahu remains firmly in control of the governing coalition, and even if the government falls, Sa'ar lacks the parliamentary support or public backing to form a government. Recent polling shows him struggling for relevance, with his party often registering under 2% support. The opposition is led by figures like Yair Lapid and Benny Gantz who command far larger political movements.

The bull case requires a cascade of unlikely events but isn't impossible. If Netanyahu's government collapses before late 2026 and new elections produce a fragmented Knesset where neither left nor right can form a majority, Sa'ar could emerge as a compromise candidate acceptable to both sides. His experience as a former Likud interior minister and his current role in Netanyahu's cabinet give him credentials with the right, while his past opposition to Netanyahu could make him palatable to centrists. A security crisis or legal development forcing Netanyahu's exit could create chaotic conditions where political norms break down. Sa'ar would need both major blocs deadlocked and specifically seeking a unity figure with his profile.

Key catalysts to monitor include any potential collapse of Netanyahu's coalition, which remains stable but faces ongoing tensions over judicial reform and ultra-Orthodox conscription issues. The next scheduled election isn't until October 2026 unless the government falls earlier. Watch for polling that shows Sa'ar's party crossing the 3.25% electoral threshold or coalition negotiations following any early election call. Netanyahu's ongoing legal situation and potential Supreme Court rulings on coalition agreements could trigger political upheaval, though current indicators suggest government stability through 2025.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why did Sa'ar's decision to rejoin Netanyahu's government hurt his chances of becoming Prime Minister?

Rejoining the coalition after positioning himself as an anti-Netanyahu alternative undermined his brand as an independent leader and eliminated his potential role as a compromise candidate from outside the current government. It also consolidated his image as a minor political player willing to accept cabinet positions rather than compete for top leadership.

### Could Sa'ar become Prime Minister without winning an election outright?

Yes, Israel's parliamentary system allows anyone who can command a Knesset majority to form a government, so Sa'ar could theoretically be selected as a unity candidate in a deadlocked situation even with minimal seats, similar to how Naftali Bennett became PM in 2021 despite leading a small party.

### What would need to happen in Israeli politics for Sa'ar's odds to rise above 5%?

A combination of Netanyahu's exit from politics, an election producing a hung Knesset with neither bloc able to form a government, and Sa'ar positioning himself as the only acceptable compromise between right-wing and centrist parties—essentially recreating the chaotic conditions that previously elevated unlikely candidates to the premiership.
