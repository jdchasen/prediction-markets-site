## Market Analysis

The bull case for rate cuts hinges on a significant economic slowdown materializing over the next year. If monthly NFP reports begin consistently printing below 100,000 jobs or unemployment rises above 4.5%, the Fed would face mounting pressure to pivot. A sustained decline in core CPI below 2.5% for multiple consecutive months—particularly the shelter component which has remained sticky—would provide the clearest signal that inflation is truly conquered. The December 2024 and January 2025 CPI releases (scheduled for mid-January and mid-February) will be critical early indicators. Additionally, if Q4 2024 or Q1 2025 GDP growth comes in below 1%, recession fears could force the Fed's hand earlier than currently anticipated.

The bear case maintaining high rates centers on the "higher for longer" scenario where inflation proves structurally embedded above the Fed's 2% target. Even with headline CPI moderating, services inflation excluding energy has remained elevated around 4-5%, suggesting wage pressures continue transmitting through the economy. The January 2025 FOMC meeting on January 28-29 will provide crucial forward guidance, and Chair Powell's language around inflation persistence will shape expectations. Strong retail sales data and continued wage growth above 4% year-over-year would reinforce the no-cut scenario. The Fed has repeatedly emphasized they would rather hold rates too high too long than cut prematurely and reignite inflation.

Key catalysts include the eight FOMC meetings between now and April 2026, with the March 2025 meeting and updated Summary of Economic Projections being particularly significant for dot plot revisions. Monthly CPI releases will remain the highest-impact data points, while the quarterly Employment Cost Index provides the cleanest read on wage pressures without composition effects. Any significant financial stability events—credit market stress, regional banking issues, or commercial real estate deterioration—could rapidly shift probabilities toward cuts regardless of inflation data.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is the market so confident rates won't be cut by April 2026 despite that being over a year away?

The timeline reflects the Fed's clear messaging that they view inflation risks as asymmetric and would rather risk mild recession than repeat the 1970s policy mistake of premature easing. Current forward guidance and dot plots show committee members projecting rates remaining elevated well into 2026.

### What would need to happen for these odds to move significantly higher toward 50% or above?

A sharp deterioration in employment data with unemployment jumping above 5%, combined with core CPI falling decisively below 2% for at least three consecutive months, would dramatically increase cut probabilities. Financial contagion events similar to March 2023's banking crisis could also force emergency cuts.

### Does this market resolve YES if the Fed cuts rates at any meeting before April 2026, or only at the April meeting specifically?

The market resolves YES if rate cuts occur by or at the April 2026 FOMC meeting, meaning any cuts implemented at prior meetings (including all of 2025 and early 2026) would trigger a YES resolution.
