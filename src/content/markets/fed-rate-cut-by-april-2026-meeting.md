## Market Analysis

The bear case for rate cuts centers on persistent inflationary pressures, particularly in services and shelter components of CPI, combined with a resilient labor market that keeps the Fed in restrictive territory. If core PCE inflation remains above 3% through 2025 and nonfarm payrolls continue adding 150,000+ jobs monthly, the Fed will likely hold rates elevated well into 2026. The January 2025 CPI report (due February 12) and the March FOMC meeting (March 18-19) will provide early signals on whether inflation is reaccelerating after recent progress stalled. Strong Q4 2024 GDP growth above 3% would reinforce the higher-for-longer narrative and further diminish cut probabilities.

The bull case requires either a significant economic slowdown or inflation decisively breaking below 2.5%. A recession triggered by lagged monetary policy effects, commercial real estate stress, or consumer exhaustion could force emergency cuts. Key leading indicators to watch include the unemployment rate crossing 4.5% (currently 4.1%), ISM Manufacturing PMI dropping below 45, or consecutive negative NFP prints. The Fed's Summary of Economic Projections at the June 2025 FOMC meeting (June 17-18) will reveal whether officials see any 2025-2026 cuts in their dot plot. Weekly jobless claims trending above 250,000 and the yield curve steepening sharply would signal deteriorating conditions that make cuts more likely.

Critical catalysts include the February 7 jobs report, quarterly GDP releases (January 30 for Q4 2024, April 30 for Q1 2025), and each FOMC meeting through 2025. The May CPI report (released June 11, 2025) will be particularly important as it arrives just before the summer FOMC meeting. If core CPI runs at or above 0.3% monthly through mid-2025, the 18.5% probability could drop into single digits. Conversely, three consecutive months of sub-0.2% core CPI readings combined with unemployment rising above 4.5% could push odds above 40%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does this market resolve YES if the Fed cuts rates at any meeting before April 2026, or only if they cut at the April 2026 meeting specifically?

The market resolves YES if the Fed implements a rate cut at any FOMC meeting through and including the April 29-30, 2026 meeting. A single 25 basis point cut at any of the eight scheduled meetings between now and then would trigger a YES resolution.

### How much would the Fed funds rate need to decrease from current levels to resolve this market as YES?

Any reduction in the target federal funds rate qualifies, even a single 25 basis point cut. With the current range at 4.25-4.50% as of January 2025, a move to 4.00-4.25% at any meeting through April 2026 would resolve YES.

### What happens if the Fed pauses rate changes but shifts to more dovish forward guidance suggesting future cuts?

Forward guidance and policy signals don't affect resolution—only actual implemented rate cuts matter. The market only resolves YES if the FOMC votes to lower the target federal funds rate, not based on meeting statements, press conference rhetoric, or dot plot projections.
