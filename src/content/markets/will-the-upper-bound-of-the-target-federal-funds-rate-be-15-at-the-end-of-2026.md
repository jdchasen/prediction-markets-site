## Market Analysis

The bull case for sub-1.5% rates rests on recession dynamics potentially forcing the Fed into aggressive easing through 2025-2026. If labor markets crack meaningfully (unemployment rising sharply from current ~4% levels) or inflation genuinely collapses to undershooting the 2% target, the Fed would be forced to cut substantially. The FOMC meets eight times annually through 2026, with the December 2024 and January 2025 meetings likely establishing the tone for the cycle—if the Fed signals panic cuts in response to labor data deterioration, the probability window widens. Additionally, if a recession begins in 2025 (the WSJ survey shows growing recession probability), the Fed typically cuts aggressively, potentially pushing rates into the 1-1.5% range by December 2026.

The bear case, which dominates current pricing, argues that the Fed will maintain a higher neutral rate than markets expect. With core inflation still above target and financial conditions having eased substantially, there's limited economic rationale for rates to fall that low absent a genuine crisis. The Fed has signaled it won't reach 1.5% unless conditions deteriorate severely—Chairman Powell's recent rhetoric emphasizes data-dependence but caution against cutting too quickly. Additionally, 2026 contains no major elections, reducing political pressure for stimulus, and Congress is unlikely to pursue expansionary fiscal policy given deficit concerns, meaning the easing burden falls entirely on monetary policy.

Key catalysts include the Q1 2025 PCE inflation prints (released early spring), which will signal whether sticky inflation persists, and the monthly employment reports through 2025, particularly if nonfarm payrolls show sustained weakness below 100k. The Fed's June 2025 Summary of Economic Projections will provide critical guidance on the long-run neutral rate assumptions. Traders should monitor the 2-year Treasury yield as a market-implied proxy for expected Fed policy; as of now it trades well above the 1.5% level, reflecting skepticism about the bull case. Watch for any recession declaration from the NBER or sustained unemployment rate increases above 5%, which would be the primary mechanism pushing this market higher.
