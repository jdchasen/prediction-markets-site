## Market Analysis

The market has priced Robertson at essentially zero probability of winning the 2025–2026 Hart Trophy, reflecting skepticism about his MVP candidacy despite being a Dallas Stars right winger entering what could be a peak performance window. This matters now because the season is underway, giving traders real data to calibrate whether the market is rationally dismissive or overlooking genuine value. Robertson has been a consistent 40+ goal scorer and top-line producer, yet the Hart rarely goes to pure goal-scorers without exceptional point totals or dominant season narratives.

The bull case rests on Robertson's trajectory and Dallas's potential dominance. He's posted back-to-back seasons with 60+ points and has developed into a net-front finisher with high-leverage playoff importance. If the Stars finish with the league's best regular-season record and Robertson eclipses 110+ points while his team wins 60+ games, voting patterns could shift—Hart voters reward narrative coherence, and a dominant Robertson leading a dominant team breaks through the noise. Recent seasons show eligible candidates need either transcendent individual stats (1.50+ points-per-game) or a historic playoff run as a carry-job.

The bear case is more straightforward: Robertson isn't a playmaking orchestrator like typical Hart winners, and even elite right-wingers struggle to win without peripheral dominance (Selanne, Sakic, Lemieux all had league-leading playmaking elements). The Hart category typically goes to centers, elite defensemen, or goalies; right-wingers need statistical anomalies to break through. Dallas plays in a stacked Western Conference with Colorado, Edmonton, and Vegas all capable of producing MVP-caliber seasons from MacKinnon, McDavid, or Panarin. Robertson would need both career-high performance and favorable voting circumstances.

Watch Robertson's point-per-game pace through January 2026 (the voting narrative window) and Dallas's win-loss record; a sub-.95 PPG pace makes Hart contention nearly impossible regardless of goals. Monitor injury status for both Robertson and Dallas's elite depth—if he carries an undermanned team late-season, narrative value increases. The market's 0.1% pricing suggests a 1,000-to-1 longshot; rational traders should identify whether that's appropriate dismissal or mispricing relative to historical Hart winner profiles and his actual statistical trajectory mid-season.

### What point-per-game threshold would make Robertson a viable Hart contender rather than a long-shot play?

### Has any right-winger won the Hart in the last 15 years, and what does that tell us about Robertson's pathway?
