## Market Analysis

The market is currently pricing in only a 3.5% probability of a rate hike at the June 2026 FOMC meeting, reflecting widespread trader conviction that the Fed will either be holding rates steady or cutting further by that point. This extraordinarily low odds reflects the market's expectation of persistently low inflation and economic softness stretching nearly two years into the future, making it a contrarian bet against the consensus disinflation narrative.

The bull case for a hike rests on the possibility of rekindled inflation pressures or unexpectedly strong labor market resilience into 2026. If PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred gauge) remains persistently above the 2% target through 2025, or if the unemployment rate stays sub-4% with continued wage acceleration, the Fed could be forced to keep rates higher for longer than markets currently expect. The CME FedWatch tool currently shows near-zero probability for June 2026 hikes, but historical precedent shows the Fed has shifted course rapidly—if we see CPI or core CPI trending upward in late 2025 or early 2026, long-dated rate expectations could reprice sharply. Key inflation releases to watch: the December 2025 CPI report (January 14, 2026) and the January 2026 PCE data (late February 2026) will be crucial for assessing whether disinflation has stalled.

The bear case—the dominant position—assumes the Fed successfully engineers a soft landing with inflation cooling toward 2% by mid-2025, allowing the central bank to cut rates materially through 2025 and into 2026. The current Fed funds futures curve is pricing in cumulative cuts totaling 75-100 basis points through mid-2026. Non-farm payroll data will be critical: if the January 2026 NFP report shows sustained job creation above 150K with stable wage growth, it would support the baseline scenario, but a sharper slowdown (below 100K) would reinforce deflation concerns and push hike odds even lower. Additionally, Fed communications and guidance at the December 2024 and January 2025 FOMC meetings will heavily influence market expectations; any dovish pivot strengthens the bear case.

The specific catalyst dates to monitor are the June 18, 2026 FOMC meeting itself and the quarterly Summary of Economic Projections that will accompany it. In the six months leading up, traders should track the trajectory of core PCE inflation (monthly data releases on the 10th of each month), unemployment data from the first Friday of each month, and any major Fed speaker signals about 2026 policy intentions. The spread between current odds (3.5%) and what similar markets were pricing one year prior would signal if recent inflation surprises have shifted the needle; currently, any surprising hot CPI print in late 2025 would be the primary catalyst to move this from contrarian long shot to legitimate consideration.
