## Market Analysis

The market is pricing a 45% probability that the BoE holds rates steady at its June 2026 meeting, with significant uncertainty reflecting genuine debate about UK monetary policy direction. This matters because it reveals trader expectations about inflation persistence, sterling strength, and the BoE's policy stance more than a year out—a timeframe where predictability typically erodes. The near-even split suggests the market sees legitimate scenarios for both continued rate cuts (if inflation cools) and a pause (if it proves sticky).

The bull case for a rate hike rests on persistent services inflation and wage growth remaining above target. UK core inflation has proven stickier than headline CPI, particularly in domestically-driven sectors. If inflation remains above the BoE's 2% target heading into mid-2026, and wage growth doesn't decelerate as expected, the committee could opt to hold or even raise rates to prevent expectations from becoming unanchored. Sterling weakness would amplify this scenario by pushing import prices higher. Key inflation readings in early 2026—particularly January and February CPI releases—will be crucial signals for this outcome.

The bear case argues that by June 2026, the BoE will have already cut rates substantially from current levels as growth slows and inflation gravitates toward target. The yield curve currently inverts at longer maturities, signaling recession concerns that typically precede rate cuts. If UK unemployment rises materially through 2025 and 2026, or if global disinflation accelerates, the BoE faces pressure to ease rather than tighten. The March 2026 MPC meeting two months prior could telegraph the June decision; watch that communication closely for forward guidance shifts.

Traders should monitor three specific catalysts: Q1 2026 GDP and employment data (January-March releases), which establish growth trajectory; February and March 2026 CPI prints showing whether inflation is genuinely on track to target; and any FOMC signals about Federal Reserve cuts, since BoE policy typically correlates with Fed moves. Watch for wage growth data in early 2026—a reading below 4% would bolster the cuts case. The spread between sterling forwards and real rates will also matter; if real yields compress significantly, it reduces the incentive to hike.
