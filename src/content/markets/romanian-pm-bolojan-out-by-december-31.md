The 57.5% YES probability suggests traders see meaningful downside risk to his tenure despite his reputation as a competent administrator.

The bull case for early departure centers on Romania's fragile coalition dynamics. Key triggers include parliamentary votes on judicial overhauls tied to EU Rule of Law monitoring (ongoing throughout 2025-2026), regional elections scheduled for autumn 2024 that could reshape political leverage, and potential disputes over fiscal discipline demanded by Brussels. The bear case emphasizes Bolojan's relative strength and the absence of obvious challengers. He took office in December 2024 with a mandate to stabilize institutions and improve EU relations, areas where his technocratic background is an asset. The PNL (National Liberal Party) retains legislative seats that give him some stability, and early coalition partners appear invested in continuity through EU funds absorption cycles. Major early elections would be required to destabilize him, and while the judicial reform votes are contentious, they're unlikely to force an outright government collapse in the next 24 months.

Traders should monitor coalition health signals closely: watch for public conflicts between coalition partners over spending priorities or judicial appointments, track EU compliance feedback letters (typically quarterly), and note any significant shifts in polling that might embolden junior coalition members to demand leadership rotation. The autumn 2024 regional elections and any subsequent power-broker repositioning are immediate tells on whether the broader political class sees Bolojan as durable or expendable.
