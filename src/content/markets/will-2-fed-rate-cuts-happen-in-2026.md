## Market Analysis

The bull case for two cuts hinges on a material economic slowdown materializing in late 2025 or early 2026. If the unemployment rate rises from its current 4.1% to above 4.5-5.0% and CPI falls convincingly below 2.5% on a sustained basis, the Fed would have room to ease. A recession scenario—even a mild one—would almost certainly trigger more than two cuts, making this market underpriced. Watch the Q4 2025 GDP advance estimate (late January 2026) and the December 2025 NFP report (January 10, 2026) as critical inflection points that would influence the early 2026 FOMC meetings scheduled for January 28-29 and March 17-18.

The bear case is that inflation remains above the Fed's 2% target throughout 2025, particularly in housing and wage-driven services categories. Core PCE running at 2.5-3.0% would keep the Fed on hold for most of 2026. The more likely scenario may be zero or one cut rather than two, especially if the labor market remains resilient with NFP prints consistently above 150K and wage growth holding above 4%. The Fed has also signaled higher tolerance for keeping rates restrictive longer, with dot plot projections from the December 2024 FOMC meeting showing only modest easing through 2026.

Key catalysts include the January 29, 2025 FOMC decision and updated dot plots, which will reveal how many 2025 cuts remain in the baseline forecast. Each monthly CPI release (next: February 12, 2025 for January data) and NFP report (first Friday of each month) will recalibrate both 2025 and 2026 expectations. The critical window is Q4 2025 when the Fed will provide its first formal 2026 guidance. Traders should monitor the December 2025 FOMC meeting (December 16-17) closely, as the updated Summary of Economic Projections will show whether two 2026 cuts are even in the median forecast, which would be necessary but not sufficient for this market to resolve YES.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if the Fed cuts once in 2026 but not twice?

The market resolves NO, as it requires exactly two or more rate cuts (25 basis point reductions) during the 2026 calendar year. One cut of 50 basis points would count as two separate cuts if executed as a double reduction at a single meeting.

### How does the 2025 rate path affect the probability of 2026 cuts?

If the Fed cuts aggressively in 2025 (three or more cuts), it reduces the likelihood of further cuts in 2026 unless economic conditions deteriorate significantly. Conversely, if the Fed holds steady through most of 2025, it creates more potential cutting room in 2026 but also suggests inflation remains problematic.

### Could the Fed hike rates in 2026 instead of cutting them?

While unlikely based on current projections, a reacceleration of inflation or significant fiscal stimulus could force hikes in 2026, which would make this market resolve NO and represents tail risk not fully captured in the 18.5% odds.
