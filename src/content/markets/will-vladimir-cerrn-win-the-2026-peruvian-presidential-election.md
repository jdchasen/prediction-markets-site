## Market Analysis

The bear case dominates this market for concrete reasons. Cerrón has been in hiding since October 2023 after Peru's Supreme Court upheld his corruption conviction and 3.5-year prison sentence related to his time as Junín regional governor. He remains on Peru's most-wanted list with a fugitive warrant, making any legal campaign participation extraordinarily difficult. Even if he somehow evaded capture through the election cycle, Peruvian electoral law bars convicted individuals from running for office. His Peru Libre party has also seen its political capital collapse since backing President Pedro Castillo's failed 2022 self-coup attempt, which resulted in Castillo's imprisonment. Recent polling shows Peru Libre support in single digits nationally, and the party faces potential disqualification proceedings for anti-democratic conduct. The April 2026 first-round vote would require him to somehow clear these legal obstacles, emerge from hiding, pass electoral registration scrutiny, and rebuild a devastated political brand.

The bull case requires an extraordinary sequence of events: a political amnesty deal, judicial reversal of his conviction, or a complete breakdown of Peru's institutional order that allows extra-legal candidacies. Peru's political instability—six presidents since 2016—creates some theoretical opening for radical constitutional changes or emergency measures that could theoretically benefit Cerrón. If leftist forces consolidate and sweep Peru's 2026 congressional elections (held simultaneously with the presidency), they could potentially legislate amnesty provisions, though this remains highly speculative. Cerrón maintains some loyal following among Peru's rural poor and mining regions who supported Castillo's 2021 victory.

Key catalysts include Peru's electoral calendar: candidate registration typically closes in January 2026, three months before the April vote. Traders should monitor any Supreme Court decisions on Cerrón's legal appeals, Peru Libre's standing with the National Jury of Elections (which can disqualify parties), and whether Cerrón surfaces from hiding. Congressional sessions in late 2025 could reveal whether any amnesty legislation gains traction. Polling from firms like Ipsos Peru and Instituto de Estudios Peruanos will clarify whether Peru Libre can even field a viable substitute candidate if Cerrón remains unavailable.
