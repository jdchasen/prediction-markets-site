## Market Analysis

The current 16.5% odds price in Oman joining the Abraham Accords reflects deep skepticism about Sultanate participation before year-end 2026, despite regional normalization momentum that has already drawn in UAE, Bahrain, Morocco, and Sudan. This matters now because Oman's geopolitical positioning—its historic role as a neutral mediator in the region and its close ties to Iran—makes it structurally different from previous signatories, creating genuine uncertainty about whether economic incentives alone can overcome strategic constraints.

The bull case centers on two drivers: first, economic pressure from lower oil revenues and regional economic integration benefits that could incentivize leadership to abandon costly neutrality; second, potential normalization with Iran following nuclear deal developments or leadership changes in Tehran, which would reduce Oman's need to balance between Iran and Gulf allies. If the incoming administration in Iran signals diplomatic openness in 2025, Oman's calculation shifts materially. Additionally, if Saudi Arabia—which has negotiated its own separate normalization track rather than joining the Accords—formally adheres to the framework, it dramatically increases regional legitimacy and reduces Oman's isolation risk. Current Sultan Haitham bin Tariq, who took power in 2020, has shown selective pragmatism on foreign policy; a formal invitation backed by Saudi consensus could prove difficult to refuse.

The bear case is substantially stronger. Oman's entire foreign policy foundation rests on maintaining equidistant relationships with Iran and the GCC, a positioning that predates the current conflict and survives across administrations. Joining the Accords would explicitly rupture ties with Tehran, risking economic retaliation (Iran-Oman trade is meaningful) and undercutting Oman's mediator role in future regional crises. Historical precedent matters: Oman notably abstained from the 2011 Arab Spring interventions and the Qatar blockade, signaling institutional commitment to non-alignment. No credible reporting suggests leadership appetite for this shift, and domestic Omani politics offer no reform pressure from parliament or civil society pushing for Israeli normalization. Iran's strategic importance to Oman—as a counterweight to Saudi hegemony and as a civilizational anchor—appears durable regardless of near-term nuclear negotiations.

Key dates to monitor: any formal Saudi Arabia accession announcement (which would reset regional expectations), Iranian leadership transitions or major policy shifts (late 2025 or beyond), and any Oman-specific economic crisis triggering diversification pressure. Watch for Israeli-Oman trade or security cooperation announcements, which would be early signal of normalization momentum. Current market pricing at 16.5% appears reasonable given the structural barriers; movement upward should require concrete diplomatic repositioning signals from Muscat, not just regional trends.
