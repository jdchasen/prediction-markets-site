## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Oman's pragmatic foreign policy track record and existing diplomatic infrastructure. Sultan Haitham bin Tariq has maintained the country's tradition of quiet mediation, and Oman never severed ties with Israel like other Arab states did historically. The sultanate hosted Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu in 2018 and has facilitated backchannel communications between regional rivals. With Bahrain and the UAE already in the Accords, Oman faces less regional isolation risk than in previous years. Economic incentives are substantial: potential technology partnerships, investment from both Israeli and American sources, and enhanced trade relationships could help diversify Oman's oil-dependent economy. The sultanate's 2024-2025 fiscal pressures and $47 billion debt load create motivation for arrangements that could unlock economic benefits.

The bear case highlights Oman's strategic positioning as a neutral regional mediator, which normalization could compromise. Unlike the UAE and Bahrain, Oman maintains functional relationships with Iran across the Strait of Hormuz and has served as a crucial intermediary in conflicts from Yemen to U.S.-Iran negotiations. Joining the Accords risks alienating Iran and undermining this valuable diplomatic role. Domestically, Omani public opinion remains sympathetic to Palestinian causes, and the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack and subsequent Gaza war have hardened regional sentiment against normalization. The sultanate lacks the authoritarian insulation of Gulf neighbors to ignore public pressure entirely. Saudi Arabia's conspicuous absence from the Accords also matters—Muscat typically avoids leading on controversial Arab-Israeli issues when Riyadh holds back.

Key catalysts to monitor include any Saudi movement toward normalization, which would provide political cover for Oman. The outcome of the 2024 U.S. presidential election and subsequent Middle East policy direction will matter significantly, as American diplomatic pressure and security guarantees have driven previous Abraham Accords signings. Watch for high-level state visits between Omani and Israeli officials, particularly any invitation for Israeli leadership to visit Muscat officially. The evolution of the Gaza situation and any progress on Palestinian statehood discussions would shift regional dynamics. Oman's annual budget announcements in late December provide insight into fiscal pressures that might motivate seeking new economic partnerships. Any breakthrough in Yemen peace talks—where Oman plays a mediating role—could either free up diplomatic bandwidth for normalization or reinforce Oman's preference for maintaining its neutral mediator status.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What distinguishes Oman's relationship with Israel from other Gulf states that joined the Abraham Accords?

Oman never formally broke diplomatic relations with Israel and has maintained quiet contacts for decades, but it prioritizes its role as a neutral regional mediator—particularly with Iran—which full normalization could jeopardize. This makes Oman both more familiar with Israel and more cautious about public formalization.

### How would Saudi Arabia's decision affect Oman's likelihood of joining?

Saudi normalization would provide essential political cover for Oman, as the sultanate historically avoids leading on controversial Arab-Israeli issues when larger Gulf powers remain uncommitted. Riyadh joining would effectively eliminate regional political risk for Muscat's accession.

### What economic pressures might push Oman toward the Abraham Accords despite political hesitations?

Oman faces a $47 billion debt burden and oil-dependency challenges requiring economic diversification, making Israeli technology partnerships, American investment incentives, and enhanced trade access potentially attractive despite the diplomatic costs.
