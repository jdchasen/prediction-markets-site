## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on Oman's long track record as a regional mediator and its existing quiet cooperation with Israel on security matters. Sultan Haitham bin Tariq, who took power in January 2020, has shown willingness to continue his predecessor's pragmatic foreign policy while diversifying Oman's economy away from oil dependence—a goal that Israeli technology partnerships could accelerate. Oman hosted Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu in 2018 and has maintained covert intelligence-sharing relationships. With Saudi Arabia reportedly edging closer to normalization talks with Israel throughout 2024-2025, Omani officials may calculate that joining alongside or shortly after Riyadh would provide political cover. The Trump administration's return in 2025 could intensify diplomatic pressure and sweeteners for holdout Gulf states, particularly if linked to security guarantees or economic packages. Key monitoring points include any announced visits by Israeli officials to Muscat or bilateral trade delegations in 2025-2026.

The bear case recognizes Oman's deeply embedded policy of strategic neutrality and its crucial relationship with Iran, with whom it shares control of the Strait of Hormuz. Oman has maintained diplomatic and economic ties with Tehran even as other Gulf states have confronted Iran, positioning itself as an essential backchannel for regional de-escalation. Public normalization with Israel would jeopardize this mediator role and potentially expose Oman to Iranian retaliation or proxy threats. Domestically, while Oman lacks the same level of public opposition to Israel normalization seen in some Arab states, Sultan Haitham governs through consensus-building rather than bold unilateral moves. The Palestinian issue remains symbolically important to Omani public opinion, and without meaningful progress toward a two-state solution, formal normalization carries domestic political risks. Additionally, if Saudi normalization stalls—whether due to Israeli political constraints, Palestinian violence escalation, or U.S. congressional opposition to security pact provisions—Oman would have even less incentive to move first.

Critical catalysts include Saudi Arabia's normalization timeline, any announcements from the expected Trump administration Middle East team in early 2025, and Oman's economic diversification plans outlined in Vision 2040. Traders should monitor bilateral visit announcements, particularly around major diplomatic events like the UN General Assembly in September 2025 and 2026, and watch for reporting on Israeli-Omani economic agreements that might precede formal diplomatic recognition. The prosecution of the Gaza conflict and any breakthrough in Israeli-Palestinian negotiations would significantly shift probabilities, as would Iranian actions that might push Oman closer to the anti-Iran coalition.

### What existing ties does Oman already have with Israel that might lead to formal Accords membership?

### How does Oman's relationship with Iran affect the likelihood of joining the Abraham Accords?

### Would Oman join the Abraham Accords independently or wait for Saudi Arabia to move first?

Historical precedent suggests Oman would likely wait for Saudi cover, as the sultanate rarely takes bold unilateral foreign policy positions. A Saudi normalization breakthrough would dramatically increase Oman's odds of following suit within months.
