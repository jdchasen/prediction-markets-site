## Market Analysis

The market prices an 8% chance that South Korea's Democratic Party leader faces impeachment before year-end 2026, reflecting substantial skepticism that formal removal proceedings will succeed despite his legal vulnerabilities and political opponents' control of legislative mechanisms. This matters now because Lee currently faces multiple criminal trials, the conservative People Power Party controls the National Assembly, and Korea's political dynamics shift sharply between presidential administrations—making impeachment a plausible but uncertain tool for political rivals.

The bull case rests on three concrete vulnerabilities. First, Lee faces active criminal prosecutions including bribery and fraud charges; conviction in any case could trigger bipartisan impeachment momentum, particularly if sentences approach imprisonment. Second, the ruling PPP holds 108 of 254 National Assembly seats and needs only 180 votes (two-thirds) to impeach, meaning they require roughly 72 opposition votes—achievable if conviction shames Democratic Party defectors or if public sentiment shifts dramatically. Third, South Korea has a recent precedent: President Park Geun-hye was impeached in 2016 and convicted, demonstrating the process works under sufficient political pressure. Watch for trial verdict dates in 2025-2026, especially any guilty verdict in his bribery trial scheduled for mid-2025.

The bear case, supporting the low odds, emphasizes Lee's institutional insulation. The Democratic Party controls the party's direction and can block coordination with the ruling coalition on such a dramatic step. Impeachment requires sustained political will across party lines, and Korean politics historically compartmentalizes legal troubles from legislative proceedings—Lee could face criminal conviction without triggering impeachment removal. The PPP may prefer winning 2027's presidential election to expending political capital on impeachment, which carries reputational costs. Additionally, Lee's supporters maintain he faces politically motivated prosecution, a narrative that hardens party loyalty.

Concrete catalysts include his trials' verdict dates (bribery trial verdict expected late 2025), any guilty verdicts in fraud cases, and shifts in public approval polling tracked by Gallup Korea and Realmeter. The 2026-12-31 deadline means traders should monitor Q3-Q4 2026 for any sudden legislative pushes; if no momentum builds by October 2026, the 8% odds likely compress further toward expiration. Watch legislative session schedules and any statements from PPP leadership signaling willingness to collaborate with Democrats on removal—currently minimal—as the strongest signal the probability should spike.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What specific crimes could trigger impeachment, and which trial verdict is most likely to be the catalyst?

Lee faces bribery charges (potentially carrying prison time), fraud charges, and embezzlement allegations. His bribery trial, with a verdict expected in late 2025, carries the highest political weight because bribery convictions historically generate the strongest bipartisan removal sentiment in Korean politics.

### Could Lee be impeached for crimes committed before his leadership role, or must offenses relate to current office?

South Korean constitutional law permits impeachment based on crimes committed both in and out of office if they breach the public trust; Lee's pre-leadership alleged offenses (mostly as Seongnam mayor and Gyeonggi governor) remain valid grounds, though in-office misconduct generates stronger political pressure.

### If Lee is convicted criminally, do impeachment odds automatically spike, or are there structural reasons removal might still fail?

Criminal conviction would likely double or triple the odds, but impeachment still requires the PPP to prioritize removal over winning the 2027 presidential election—a political calculation that isn't automatic
