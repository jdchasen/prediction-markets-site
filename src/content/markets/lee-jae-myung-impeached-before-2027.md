## Market Analysis

The current 7.1% odds reflect relatively low near-term impeachment risk for the Democratic Party's Lee Jae-myung, South Korea's most prominent opposition figure, despite persistent legal vulnerabilities and an opposition-controlled parliament. This market matters now because Lee is positioning himself as a 2027 presidential frontrunner while facing multiple ongoing trials, and the political dynamics around presidential succession could shift dramatically depending on outcomes in the National Assembly and prosecution cases.

The bull case for impeachment centers on concrete legal exposure: Lee faces convictions in multiple trials including charges related to bribery, campaign finance violations, and abuse of power, with verdicts potentially arriving through 2025-2026. The ruling People Power Party controls parliament and has recently shown willingness to use impeachment as a political weapon (as demonstrated with judicial impeachments in 2024). If Lee's trial convictions finalize before year-end 2026, the PPP could move to impeach him on grounds that a convicted felon cannot represent a major party or run for president. The bull thesis assumes intensifying political confrontation and PPP determination to eliminate a leading rival before the 2027 election. Additionally, if Lee's Democratic Party loses ground in the April 2024 or expected 2026 local elections, diminished political leverage could make him more vulnerable to impeachment proceedings.

The bear case argues that impeaching an opposition leader carries significant political risk and sets precedent for tit-for-tat retaliation, which constrains even a hostile ruling party. Lee's trials moving slowly through appellate courts—South Korean judicial processes are notoriously lengthy—means final convictions may extend past the December 2026 deadline. Constitutional impeachment requires demonstrating he violated laws while serving as a legislator or party leader (not private citizen crimes), a technical bar that complicates prosecution politically. The Democratic Party's 170+ parliamentary seats provides blocking power if impeachment requires supermajority support. Most crucially, Lee remains the Democratic Party's likely presidential nominee and strongest challenger to the PPP's 2027 candidate; impeaching him could backfire by rallying opposition voters and making the PPP appear authoritarian ahead of the election.

Watch specifically for: the Seoul Supreme Court's verdict on Lee's bribery case (expected mid-2025), any PPP supermajority power shift following regional elections, and whether prosecution successfully frames charges as crimes committed while Lee served as Gyeonggi Province governor (impeachable office) rather than as party leader. Parliamentary dynamics matter enormously—if the PPP gains additional seats or the Democratic Party fractures, odds shift materially. The December 2026 deadline means the market essentially prices impeachment as a political decision, not a legal inevitability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between conviction in Lee's criminal trials and impeachment through parliament?

Criminal conviction determines guilt for specific crimes and may trigger legal sanctions; impeachment is a separate political decision by parliament that removes him from office (if he holds one) and can precede, follow, or occur independently of convictions.

### Could Lee be impeached before conviction finalized if parliament decides he's unfit?

Technically yes—parliament could initiate impeachment on political grounds without waiting for final court verdicts, but it would require framing specific violations under constitutional law rather than criminal charges alone, making it legally riskier for the ruling party.

### How does the April 2024 general election outcome affect this market's trajectory?

If the ruling PPP wins decisively and gains parliamentary supermajority (200+ seats), impeachment becomes procedurally easier
