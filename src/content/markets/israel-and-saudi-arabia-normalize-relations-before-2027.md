## Market Analysis

The bull case centers on strategic alignment against Iran and economic incentives that remain compelling for both parties. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has consistently signaled openness to normalization, viewing it as crucial for Vision 2030 economic reforms and accessing advanced Israeli technology. The Trump administration's return in January 2025 creates a potential catalyst, given Trump's team brokered the Abraham Accords and Jared Kushner maintains close ties with MBS. A substantive de-escalation in Gaza or a credible pathway to Palestinian statehood—even symbolic concessions—could provide Saudi Arabia political cover domestically and across the Arab world. Reports suggest framework discussions continue through back channels, and a breakthrough announcement at a Gulf Cooperation Council summit or during a state visit could rapidly shift probabilities.

The bear case is anchored in the Gaza war's ongoing devastation and hardened Arab public opinion. Saudi Arabia has repeatedly stated normalization requires meaningful progress on Palestinian statehood, a condition Israel's current governing coalition fundamentally opposes. Prime Minister Netanyahu depends on far-right coalition partners who reject any Palestinian state, making the necessary concessions politically suicidal for his government. Even with Trump's return, his administration would need to pressure Israel significantly—something his previous term and campaign rhetoric suggest is unlikely. Iranian escalation risks, including potential nuclear developments requiring attention by late 2025 or 2026 according to IAEA assessments, could paradoxically freeze normalization talks rather than accelerate them. Saudi domestic considerations also matter: MBS faces constraints from religious establishment and public sentiment that remains strongly pro-Palestinian after October 7th.

Key developments to monitor include Trump administration appointments to Middle East positions expected by January 2025, any Israeli coalition reshuffling that could enable policy shifts, and the Saudi-hosted Arab League summits in 2025 and 2026 where Palestinian issues dominate. The International Court of Justice proceedings on Israeli actions in Gaza, with potential rulings throughout 2025, could further complicate Saudi calculations. Traders should watch for signs of renewed U.S.-brokered negotiations, particularly any Israeli commitments on West Bank settlement expansion freezes or Palestinian governance authority—the minimum threshold Saudi officials have indicated privately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Would normalization require a full Israeli-Palestinian peace deal or just symbolic gestures toward Palestinian statehood?

Saudi Arabia has indicated it needs something more substantial than Abraham Accords terms but less than full resolution—likely a credible political horizon including settlement freezes, expanded Palestinian Authority control in West Bank areas, or formal recognition of a future state. Token gestures would be insufficient given current Arab public opinion.

### How does the timing of Israeli elections affect this market's probability?

Israel's next scheduled elections aren't until October 2026, but coalition instability could trigger earlier votes. A centrist government replacing Netanyahu's far-right coalition would dramatically increase normalization odds, while current coalition stability keeps the door essentially closed regardless of external pressure.

### Does Trump's presidency make normalization more or less likely than it would be under continued Democratic administration?

Trump increases the probability due to his transactional approach with Gulf states and proven willingness to pursue dramatic Middle East deals, but his unconditional support for Netanyahu may paradoxically reduce pressure on Israel to make the Palestinian concessions Saudi Arabia requires for normalization.
