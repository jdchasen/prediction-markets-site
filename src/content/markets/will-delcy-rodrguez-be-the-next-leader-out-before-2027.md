## Market Analysis

The market is pricing an extremely low probability that Venezuela's vice president will be forced from power before year-end 2026, reflecting confidence in her political survival despite intense international pressure and domestic instability. This market matters because Rodríguez is a key figure in the Maduro regime's inner circle and a primary target of U.S. sanctions, making her removal a potential inflection point for Venezuelan politics and a test of whether external pressure can destabilize the government.

The bull case for early exit rests on accelerating regime fracture. The July 2024 election disputes have already triggered military defections and prompted Maduro to purge security officials, suggesting internal cohesion is weakening. If the opposition maintains momentum through 2025—particularly if protests escalate or the National Assembly revokes immunity—Rodríguez could become a scapegoat for economic collapse or a negotiated sacrifice in a power-sharing transition. International sanctions targeting her directly create constant pressure for regime elements to distance themselves. The 2026 midpoint also coincides with potential humanitarian crisis escalation that could force internal reshuffling.

The bear case, reflected in the 0.1% odds, is substantially stronger. Rodríguez has survived multiple coup attempts and sanctions waves since 2016 because she controls critical intelligence apparatus and maintains Maduro's personal trust. Without external military intervention—which remains off the table—removing entrenched regime figures requires internal consensus that hasn't materialized. The opposition remains fragmented, and Maduro has consolidated security forces around loyalists. Most critically, there's no clear successor arrangement that would benefit enough power brokers simultaneously to trigger a palace coup.

Traders should monitor three specific catalysts: opposition-led protests intensity in early 2025, any military leadership changes announced by the regime, and U.S. policy shifts following the administration transition. The Inter-American Court's rulings on Venezuelan cases may also create legal pressure. However, absent a dramatic security force mutiny or international intervention framework, the low odds appear justified by historical patterns of regime resilience.
