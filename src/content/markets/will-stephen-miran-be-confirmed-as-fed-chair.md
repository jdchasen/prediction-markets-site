## Market Analysis

The bear case, which clearly dominates current pricing, rests on several concrete obstacles. Jerome Powell's term as Chair doesn't expire until May 2026, and historically Fed Chairs serve their full terms without early removal. The Federal Reserve Act provides significant job protections, requiring "for cause" termination rather than at-will removal. Even if a president attempted removal, the resulting legal battle would likely extend beyond Powell's term. Additionally, Miran lacks the traditional credentials typical of Fed Chair appointments—he has never served on the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, which would require separate Senate confirmation before elevation to Chair. The Senate confirmation process for Fed positions typically takes months, and any controversial nominee faces significant scrutiny from both parties given the Fed's importance to financial stability.

The bull case requires a chain of low-probability events. Miran, who served in Trump's Treasury Department and advised the 2024 campaign, could theoretically be appointed if Powell were to resign voluntarily (perhaps under political pressure), face a successful removal effort, or if this market resolves on him being *nominated* rather than confirmed. A pathway would involve Trump winning in 2024, applying maximum pressure on Powell through public criticism and potential legal challenges, Powell stepping down to avoid institutional damage, and Miran receiving both a Board nomination and subsequent Chair nomination. Some Trump allies have floated aggressive theories about presidential authority over the Fed.

Key catalysts include the 2024 presidential election outcome, any announcements about Powell's intentions regarding his full term, and potential legal opinions on presidential removal power over Fed leadership. Watch for Trump's statements on Fed leadership if he wins, Senate Banking Committee composition after the 2024 elections, and any signs of Powell signaling early departure plans. The May 2026 expiration of Powell's Chair term represents the natural transition point, though this market extends through October 2026, allowing for confirmation of a successor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Could Trump remove Jerome Powell before his term ends in May 2026?

Legal consensus suggests the Federal Reserve Act requires "for cause" removal, making it extremely difficult. Any removal attempt would trigger immediate legal challenges that could outlast Powell's remaining term.

### Has Stephen Miran ever served on the Federal Reserve Board?

No, Miran has no prior Federal Reserve experience. He would need Senate confirmation to join the Board before potentially becoming Chair, adding another institutional hurdle to this scenario.

### What happens if Powell resigns voluntarily before May 2026?

The President would nominate a new Chair from either current Board governors or nominate someone new to both the Board and Chair position simultaneously, though the latter requires two separate confirmation processes.
