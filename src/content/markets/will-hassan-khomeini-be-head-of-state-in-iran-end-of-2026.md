## Market Analysis

The market is pricing Hassan Khomeini—grandson of Ayatollah Khomeini and a reformist cleric—as an extreme longshot to become Iran's head of state by year-end 2026, reflecting the structural barriers he faces within Iran's theocratic system. This matters now because Iran's 2025-2026 political cycle includes presidential elections (expected June 2025) and potential constitutional shifts that could theoretically elevate non-Supreme Leader figures, making this an inflection point for understanding regime succession dynamics.

The bull case rests on three pivotal factors: Khomeini has explicitly signaled reform ambitions and maintains genuine legitimacy as a Khomeini family descendant, which carries symbolic weight in Iran's clerical hierarchy. Second, Supreme Leader Khamenei is 86 and succession uncertainty creates institutional fluidity—if he dies or steps down before 2026, a reformist realignment becomes possible. Third, the 2024-2025 period has seen unprecedented public discontent following the Mahsa Amini protests and economic deterioration, potentially forcing regime recalibration toward a softer face. The bear case is more compelling: Iran's Supreme Leader remains constitutionally supreme and controls the military and judiciary—the presidency is subordinate regardless of who wins the June 2025 election. Khomeini is not a presidential candidate in 2025 (the Guardian Council's vetting process, which disqualifies most reformists, almost certainly excludes him), and he has no institutional power base. Even if Khamenei died, the Assembly of Experts would elect his successor—a 88-member body dominated by hardliners unlikely to choose a reformist grandson. Historical precedent shows Iran's system protects theocratic control; reformists who reach high office (like Rouhani) operate within tight constraints.

Key catalysts to monitor: the June 2025 presidential election outcome (watch whether hardliners or pragmatists gain ground), any public health developments regarding Khamenei's status (he underwent prostate surgery in 2014), and Assembly of Experts activity in late 2025/early 2026. The constitutional amendment process, if pursued, could theoretically change executive structure, but this remains speculative. Traders should track reformist performance in 2025 midterms and whether Khomeini's public statements escalate—silence suggests acquiescence to constraints, while activism suggests regime-permitted positioning.
