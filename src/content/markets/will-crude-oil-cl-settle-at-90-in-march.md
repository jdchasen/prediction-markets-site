## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on constrained supply from OPEC+ production cuts, geopolitical escalation in the Middle East or Russia-Ukraine conflict, and potential U.S. sanctions tightening. If current tensions persist and the Biden administration (or successor) maintains or deepens export restrictions on Iran or Russian crude, $90+ becomes the floor rather than a ceiling. The spring 2026 timeframe also coincides with peak driving season demand in North America, historically supportive. Conversely, the bear case hinges on demand destruction from a potential recession by late 2025/early 2026, renewable energy substitution accelerating faster than consensus expects, and OPEC+ fracturing to open the spigots. A U.S. recession would gut oil demand; additionally, if geopolitical tensions cool markedly—particularly between Israel-Iran or in Ukraine—risk premiums compress fast, pushing prices toward the $70-80 range.

Critical catalysts include OPEC+ production meetings (typically June and December, with interim reviews), 2026 U.S. midterm elections in November (which will shape energy policy direction), and any major Middle East escalation or de-escalation moves. Crude's path also correlates tightly to Fed rate decisions through mid-2026; if inflation remains sticky and rates stay higher longer, that inverts demand curves, pressuring oil lower. Watch for any major pipeline disruptions, sanctions announcements on Iran or Venezuela, or U.S. shale production surprises—each can swing the contract 5-10 points in either direction.
