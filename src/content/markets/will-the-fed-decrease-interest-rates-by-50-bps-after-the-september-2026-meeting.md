## Market Analysis

The bull case rests on a significant recession or deflationary shock materializing by mid-2026. If unemployment spikes sharply (a surprise jump in the monthly NFP reports between now and September 2026), or if core CPI (released monthly on the 10-12th of each month) rolls over into sustained sub-2% territory, the Fed could face pressure to cut aggressively. A financial crisis, sharp equity market decline, or external shock (geopolitical escalation, credit event) could also force the Fed's hand into emergency-style cuts. Historical precedent shows the Fed can move 50+ bps when conditions deteriorate rapidly—the 2020 COVID response being the most recent example.

The bear case, which the market heavily favors, assumes the Fed successfully navigates a soft landing or modest growth slowdown with inflation anchored near 2%. If PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation metric, released monthly) remains sticky above target through 2026, and labor markets stay resilient (NFP averaging 150k+ monthly job additions), the Fed will likely cut in 25 bp increments at most. The FOMC's published guidance and forward rate expectations from the June and December 2025 meetings will be critical signals—if the Fed signals confidence, the probability of 50+ bp cuts drops further. Traders should monitor the 10-year Treasury yield and market breakeven inflation rates starting in late 2025, as these will telegraph whether markets expect a crisis-level scenario by September 2026.

Key catalysts include the monthly CPI and PCE releases (every third week), quarterly jobs reports, and FOMC meetings scheduled roughly every 6 weeks through 2026. The December 2025 FOMC Summary of Economic Projections will be especially important, as it will show whether Fed officials themselves are pricing in a rate-cut scenario aggressive enough to justify 50+ bp moves. A material miss on inflation data (CPI printing above 3%) or a rapid deterioration in labor market metrics (unemployment jumping 0.5%+ in a single month) would be the type of shock that could swing this market meaningfully higher.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between a 25 bp cut and a 50 bp cut, and why does the market treat them so differently?

A 25 bp cut is the Fed's standard move and reflects normal policy adjustment; a 50 bp cut signals emergency conditions, crisis response, or severe economic deterioration. The market implies only a ~4% chance of the latter, suggesting traders see no meaningful tail-risk scenario for September 2026.

### How would a shallow recession in 2025 affect this market versus a deep recession in 2026?

A shallow recession in 2025 would likely result in Fed cuts starting earlier and at a gradual pace, making a 50 bp cut in September 2026 less necessary; a sharp recession *in* 2026 closer to the meeting would be more likely to trigger aggressive cutting, moving this market higher.
