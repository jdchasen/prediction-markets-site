## Market Analysis

The bear case is overwhelming: Won, a former Jeju Governor and current People Power Party member, faces demographic and structural headwinds in Gyeonggi Province, which delivered decisive victories for Democratic Party candidates in recent elections. The province's 13.5 million residents skew younger and more liberal than national averages, particularly in cities like Suwon and Seongnam. The Democratic Party has controlled the governorship since 2018, and current polling infrastructure suggests the PPP would need a dramatic nationwide shift to be competitive. Won's national profile remains limited despite his previous cabinet position under President Yoon Suk-yeol, and internal PPP primary competition for the nomination could favor candidates with stronger regional ties.

The bull case requires a perfect storm of events: a complete collapse of the Democratic Party's standing in the Seoul Capital Area due to scandals or economic deterioration under their governance, Won securing the PPP nomination early and consolidating conservative support, or President Yoon's approval ratings reversing dramatically upward by late 2025. If housing affordability in Gyeonggi deteriorates significantly—a key voter concern—and Won positions himself as the solution candidate, pathways narrow but emerge. The PPP primaries in early 2026 (typically February-March) will determine if Won can even secure his party's nomination against rivals with stronger provincial connections.

Key catalysts include the National Assembly elections aftermath in April 2024's dynamics carrying forward, PPP nomination decisions expected in Q1 2026, and any major political realignments following local issues like the GTX railway expansion controversies or Seoul metropolitan housing policies. Traders should monitor Won's ability to establish a provincial campaign presence throughout 2025, polling data from Gyeonggi-specific surveys released by Realmeter and Gallup Korea quarterly, and whether he differentiates from Yoon's administration or embraces it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is Won Hee-ryong specifically positioned so poorly for Gyeonggi Province compared to other conservative candidates?

Won's political base is in Jeju Island, not Gyeonggi, giving him minimal name recognition and organizational infrastructure in the province. His association with President Yoon's administration, which polls poorly in the Seoul Capital Area, further handicaps him in this swing-progressive region.

### What would need to happen for Won to secure the PPP nomination against other potential candidates?

Won would need either strong backing from President Yoon's inner circle or a fractured field of local PPP politicians splitting the vote in primaries scheduled for early 2026. His national government experience could appeal to party elites seeking a competent administrator over pure regional appeal.

### Has any conservative candidate won Gyeonggi Province recently, and what were the conditions?

The PPP's Nam Kyung-pil won in 2014 during a conservative national wave, but lost his re-election bid in 2018 as the political pendulum swung against then-President Park Geun-hye's party. Such victories require exceptional national tailwinds that don't currently exist for 2026.
