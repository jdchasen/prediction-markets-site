## Market Analysis

The bear case is overwhelming: Won served as Jeju Governor from a conservative People Power Party (PPP) background but lacks the established base in Gyeonggi that typically drives successful gubernatorial campaigns. Gyeonggi has alternated between progressive and conservative governors, but the province's demographics have trended toward younger, more progressive voters concentrated in cities like Suwon and Goyang. Won would need to secure the PPP nomination first—likely determined in spring 2026—against candidates with deeper Gyeonggi roots. His 2022 presidential primary loss and relatively low national profile make him a long-shot even within his own party. The Democratic Party has maintained organizational strength in Gyeonggi despite recent national setbacks, and they're expected to field heavyweights like former Prime Minister Kim Boo-kyum or current National Assembly members with Gyeonggi constituencies.

The bull case requires multiple unlikely breaks: Won could position himself as a reform-minded conservative who appeals to moderates frustrated with both major parties' polarization. If President Yoon Suk-yeol's PPP faces a severe crisis or if the Democratic Party splinters before their primaries (typically held 2-3 months before the June election), Won might emerge as a consensus alternative candidate. His gubernatorial experience, though from Jeju, gives him executive credentials that legislative candidates lack. A major corruption scandal involving the likely Democratic frontrunner between now and spring 2026 could completely reshape the race.

Key catalysts include PPP primary scheduling (expected March-April 2026), Democratic Party candidate selection, and any polling data from early 2026 showing voter sentiment shifts in Gyeonggi's key districts. The National Assembly elections context and any constitutional or electoral reforms passed in 2025 could alter campaign dynamics. Traders should monitor Won's visibility in Gyeonggi throughout 2025, endorsements from local PPP officials, and whether he builds campaign infrastructure in the province's northern cities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is Won Hee-ryong specifically running in Gyeonggi rather than seeking another Jeju term or a different office?

Won is term-limited after serving as Jeju Governor and likely views Gyeonggi—with 13 million residents and major political significance—as a stepping stone to higher office. However, his lack of local connections in the province severely undermines his viability.

### What would need to happen for Won to secure the PPP nomination given his outsider status in Gyeonggi?

Won would need established Gyeonggi conservatives to either decline to run or fracture the vote, while he consolidates support from party leadership seeking a candidate with executive experience and moderate appeal. This scenario appears highly unlikely given the province's importance.

### How has Gyeonggi Province historically voted in gubernatorial races, and what does that suggest for a conservative candidate in 2026?

Gyeonggi elected conservative Kim Moon-soo (2006-2014) but swung to progressive Nam Kyung-pil (2014-2018) and Lee Jae-myung (2018-2021) before returning to conservative Kim Eun-hye in 2022 by just 0.16%. The razor-thin margins suggest a strong Democratic candidate would be favored in 2026.
