## Market Analysis

The bear case, which traders clearly favor, rests on the UK's historical reluctance to make such designations without strong allied coordination and the Labour government's emphasis on diplomatic engagement with Iran. Foreign Secretary David Lammy has consistently signaled preference for maintaining channels of communication rather than expansive proscription policies. The UK already banned the IRGC's Quds Force in 2019, and government officials have repeatedly argued that a full designation would complicate hostage negotiations, intelligence gathering, and back-channel diplomacy without meaningfully enhancing security capabilities. The 18-month timeline gives Labour ample room to resist pressure while pointing to existing sanctions regimes.

The bull case centers on potential forcing events that could make inaction politically untenable. A major IRGC-linked terrorist attack on UK soil or against British nationals abroad would dramatically shift the calculus, as would discovery of active IRGC operations within Britain. Escalating regional conflict involving direct Iranian military action against Western interests could create momentum for coordinated designations across allied nations. Conservative opposition continues filing motions calling for proscription, and if polling shows overwhelming public support following an Iran-related incident, Labour might calculate that the diplomatic costs are manageable. A US administration pushing hard for allied unity on Iran policy could also tip the balance.

Key catalysts include parliamentary debates on UK-Iran relations, any security incidents attributed to IRGC proxies, and coordination opportunities at NATO or G7 summits throughout 2025. Watch for Home Office terrorism threat assessments and whether MI5 publicly attributes any domestic plots to IRGC networks. The UK's next Strategic Defence Review, expected in mid-2025, could reframe Iran threat perceptions. Traders should monitor UK-Iran diplomatic incidents, hostage situations involving British nationals, and whether other major European allies move first on designation.

### Does the UK's existing designation of the IRGC Quds Force make a full IRGC designation more or less likely?

Less likely in practice, as UK officials consistently argue the 2019 Quds Force proscription already covers the most operationally dangerous elements, reducing political pressure for expansion while maintaining the option for future escalation.

### What would need to happen for this probability to reach 30% or higher?

A direct IRGC-orchestrated attack on UK territory, coordinated Five Eyes intelligence revealing imminent threats from IRGC networks in Britain, or the US and major EU partners simultaneously moving toward designation would fundamentally reshape the odds.

### How does Labour's foreign policy approach differ from the Conservatives on Iran proscription?

Labour emphasizes pragmatic engagement and views broad terrorist designations as limiting diplomatic flexibility, whereas Conservative backbenchers have repeatedly pushed for full IRGC proscription as a values-based stance, making cross-party consensus unlikely without a major security incident.
