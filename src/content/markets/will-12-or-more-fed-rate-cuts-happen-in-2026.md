## Market Analysis

The bear case against twelve cuts is straightforward: the U.S. economy would need to experience a catastrophic recession or financial crisis comparable to 2008 or the pandemic shock. Even during aggressive easing cycles, the Fed rarely cuts at every meeting. The 2019 cycle saw just three cuts total. For context, reaching twelve cuts implies either 50-75 basis point cuts at regular meetings or multiple emergency cuts between scheduled sessions. Current inflation dynamics, with core PCE still above the Fed's 2% target as of recent readings, suggest limited room for such aggressive easing unless accompanied by dramatic economic deterioration. The January 29, 2025 FOMC meeting and subsequent CPI reports (released monthly, typically mid-month) will establish the baseline trajectory for 2025 that conditions 2026 expectations.

The bull case requires envisioning a severe downturn materializing in late 2025 or early 2026. If unemployment spikes above 6-7% (currently near 4%), accompanied by consecutive negative GDP prints and financial system stress, the Fed could implement emergency rate cuts. Historical precedent exists: the Fed cut rates eleven times in 2001 and ten times in 2007-2008. Traders should monitor the initial jobless claims (released weekly every Thursday) and monthly NFP reports for signs of labor market cracking. A sharp reversal in inflation—core CPI dropping below 2% amid deflationary pressure—would remove the key constraint preventing aggressive cuts.

Key catalysts include the FOMC's Summary of Economic Projections at the March 19, June 18, September 17, and December 17, 2025 meetings, which will reveal the committee's anticipated rate path. The quarterly GDP advance estimates (released late January, April, July, and October) will signal whether recession risks are building. Credit market indicators like widening corporate bond spreads or banking sector stress would accelerate cut expectations. Any geopolitical or financial shock in 2025—similar to the SVB collapse in March 2023—could fundamentally reshape the probability if it triggers systemic concerns requiring Fed intervention.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Could the Fed physically implement twelve rate cuts in 2026 given their meeting schedule?

Yes, with eight scheduled FOMC meetings allowing for eight cuts, plus the Fed can call emergency meetings for additional cuts as they did during the 2008 crisis and March 2020 pandemic response.

### What would the federal funds rate need to be at the start of 2026 for twelve cuts to be possible?

The rate would need to be at least 3% (twelve 25bp cuts) or higher at the beginning of 2026, meaning the Fed would need to hold rates elevated through 2025 or cut minimally before then.

### Has the Fed ever cut rates twelve or more times in a single calendar year?

No modern precedent exists for twelve cuts in one year; the most aggressive recent easing saw eleven cuts spread across 2001, making this market's scenario historically extraordinary.
