## Market Analysis

The bull case for a jumbo rate increase requires envisioning a severe inflation resurgence beginning in late 2025 or early 2026. This scenario would likely involve multiple consecutive CPI prints exceeding 5-6% annually, possibly driven by supply shocks (energy crisis, geopolitical disruptions), wage-price spirals, or fiscal stimulus overheating the economy. The Fed hasn't implemented a 50+ basis point hike since 2022-2023's rapid tightening cycle, and returning to such measures would signal that inflation expectations have become unanchored despite prior policy actions. Key catalysts include the CPI releases from March, April, and May 2026 (typically released mid-month), the May 2026 employment report (early June), and any emergency FOMC communications ahead of the June 16-17, 2026 meeting. If core PCE inflation—the Fed's preferred measure—consistently prints above 4% in Q1-Q2 2026, this probability would surge.

The bear case, which the market overwhelmingly prices in, assumes the Fed maintains its gradualist approach with standard 25 basis point adjustments or holds rates steady. Historical precedent supports this: 50+ basis point moves are reserved for genuine crises, and the Fed under Chair Powell has telegraphed moves well in advance to avoid market disruption. By mid-2026, inflation is expected to be within striking distance of the 2% target based on current forward curves, while unemployment likely remains below 5%. The January 2026 FOMC meeting (January 28-29) and subsequent March meeting (March 17-18) will establish the tightening trajectory well before June. If these meetings show 25bp increments or pauses with dot plot projections showing measured policy, the case for an emergency 50bp move dissolves entirely.

Traders should monitor the December 2025 FOMC projections (December 17-18) for any hawkish surprises in the Summary of Economic Projections, particularly upward revisions to the 2026 inflation outlook above 2.5%. The February 2026 employment report (released early March) and subsequent wage growth data will be critical—average hourly earnings consistently above 4.5% year-over-year would raise alarm bells. Additionally, watch Treasury market pricing of terminal rates through 2026; if two-year yields spike above 5.5% in early 2026, it signals bond markets are pricing in emergency tightening that could validate this low-probability outcome.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What historical precedent exists for 50+ basis point rate hikes outside of the 2022-2023 cycle?

The Fed implemented 75 basis point hikes three times in 2022, but before that, such large single moves were rare and typically occurred during the Volcker era fighting double-digit inflation in the early 1980s. Modern Fed communication strategies make surprise jumbo hikes unlikely.

### Could the Fed skip the June 2026 meeting entirely and hike between meetings if inflation spirals?

While technically possible through an emergency intermeeting action, this would constitute an even more dramatic signal than a 50bp hike at a scheduled meeting and would likely crash this market's probability to near-zero since it wouldn't count as happening "after" the June meeting.

### How would Fed forward guidance in early 2026 impact this market's probability?

If the March or May 2026 FOMC statements include language about "considering larger adjustments" or "expedited policy normalization," this probability could jump to 10-20% as traders price in increased likelihood of aggressive action, even though the base case would remain 25bp moves.
