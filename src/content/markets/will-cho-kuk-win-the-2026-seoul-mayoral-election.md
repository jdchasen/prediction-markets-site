## Market Analysis

The bull case hinges on several factors working in Cho's favor simultaneously. His Rebuilding Korea Party demonstrated surprising electoral strength in the 2024 National Assembly elections, capturing progressive voters disillusioned with the mainstream Democratic Party. If his legal appeals succeed—his September 2023 conviction for academic fraud and obstruction of justice is still working through higher courts—he could regain eligibility. Seoul's progressive electorate might rally around him as a martyr figure if the current conservative Yoon administration becomes deeply unpopular by 2026. The Democratic Party establishment could also fracture, leaving space for an outsider candidacy.

The bear case is overwhelming. South Korean election law typically bars individuals with criminal convictions from running for office, and Cho received a two-year prison sentence suspended for three years. His legal troubles extend beyond one case—he faces multiple investigations and has become politically toxic even among some progressives who view his legal defense as undermining the party's anti-corruption credentials. Seoul mayoral races have historically favored candidates with executive experience and broad coalition support, neither of which Cho can easily assemble. The mainstream Democratic Party will almost certainly field a strong candidate, and Seoul's centrist swing voters who determine elections are unlikely to embrace such a polarizing figure.

Key catalysts include any Supreme Court rulings on Cho's appeals (timeline unclear but potentially in 2025), the Democratic Party's candidate selection process likely occurring in spring 2026, and polling data that emerges throughout 2025 showing whether Cho maintains any constituency beyond his core supporters. Traders should monitor whether Cho's party maintains its momentum in local by-elections and whether political prosecution narratives gain traction if the administration changes. Presidential election dynamics in 2027 could also affect the 2026 Seoul race, as parties position their candidates.
