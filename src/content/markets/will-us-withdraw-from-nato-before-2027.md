## Market Analysis

The bull case for withdrawal centers on Trump's return to office following the 2024 election, with his repeated statements that NATO allies are "delinquent" and his reported private comments about leaving the alliance during his first term. Congressional Republicans have shown increasing isolationist tendencies, and Trump could potentially attempt executive action to reduce US commitment or withdraw entirely, though this would face significant legal and institutional resistance. The House Freedom Caucus has already demonstrated willingness to block Ukraine aid, signaling a broader skepticism toward European security commitments that could embolden more dramatic moves. Any escalation in the Russia-Ukraine conflict that drains US resources or a major intra-NATO dispute over defense spending could accelerate this scenario.

The bear case recognizes that actual withdrawal requires overcoming substantial barriers: the 2023 National Defense Authorization Act included provisions requiring congressional approval for NATO withdrawal, making unilateral presidential action legally dubious. The Senate remains institutionally pro-NATO with strong bipartisan support from leadership, and even during Trump's first term, the Senate passed a non-binding resolution 97-2 supporting NATO. Defense contractors, military brass, and the foreign policy establishment would mobilize aggressively against withdrawal. European allies are rapidly increasing defense spending toward the 2% GDP target, with Poland already exceeding 4% and Germany committing to major military expansion, potentially addressing Trump's core complaint.

Key catalysts include the November 2024 presidential election results and subsequent cabinet appointments, particularly the Secretary of Defense and State positions. Watch for the July 2025 NATO summit where defense spending figures will be scrutinized, and any attempts to introduce withdrawal legislation in early 2025 if Republicans control Congress. The 2025 National Defense Authorization Act debate (typically September-December 2024) could see attempts to modify the congressional approval requirement. European defense spending data releases throughout 2025 will matter, as will any major Article 5 scenarios or transatlantic trade disputes that could strain alliance cohesion.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the president have unilateral authority to withdraw the US from NATO, or would Congress need to approve it?

The 2023 NDAA includes a provision requiring Senate approval for NATO withdrawal, though the constitutional question of whether Congress can constrain presidential treaty powers remains untested. A determined president could attempt withdrawal anyway and force a legal battle, creating significant uncertainty.

### What specific threshold of European defense spending would likely satisfy US concerns and reduce withdrawal probability?

Trump and NATO skeptics have focused on the 2% GDP defense spending target, with 23 of 32 allies expected to meet it in 2024 compared to just 6 in 2021. However, Trump has suggested higher thresholds and criticized past underspending, so even achieving 2% compliance may not eliminate the risk.

### Could the US functionally withdraw from NATO without formally leaving the treaty?

Yes, through strategic ambiguity about Article 5 commitment, removing troops from Europe, or refusing to participate in NATO command structures—actions a president could pursue without congressional approval. This "hollow alliance" scenario wouldn't trigger this market but could achieve similar practical effects.
