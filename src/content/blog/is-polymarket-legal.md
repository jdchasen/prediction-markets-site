Polymarket is the biggest prediction market in the world by trading volume. Billions of dollars flow through its markets on everything from elections to interest rates to whether Elon Musk will tweet 200 times in a week. But there is a question that comes up constantly: is it actually legal?

The short answer is complicated. Polymarket is not illegal in the way that running an unlicensed casino is illegal. But it is also not a regulated exchange, and it has a real enforcement history with US regulators. Here is exactly where things stand.

## The CFTC Settlement: What Actually Happened

In January 2022, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) [charged Polymarket](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8478-22) with operating an unregistered derivatives exchange. Polymarket settled for $1.4 million and agreed to wind down its US-facing operations.

The key points from the settlement:

- Polymarket was offering binary options (event contracts) without CFTC registration
- The CFTC classified these contracts as swaps, which require exchange registration
- Polymarket did not admit or deny the charges
- As part of the settlement, Polymarket agreed to block US users from its platform

After the settlement, Polymarket geo-blocked US IP addresses and required non-US identity verification for larger accounts. The platform continued operating internationally and grew dramatically, particularly during the 2024 US presidential election.

## Can US Traders Still Access Polymarket?

Officially, no. Polymarket's terms of service prohibit US persons from using the platform. The website blocks US IP addresses.

In practice, the situation is murkier. Polymarket runs on public smart contracts deployed on the Polygon blockchain. These contracts are permissionless — anyone with a crypto wallet can interact with them directly, regardless of location. Some US users access the platform through VPNs or by interacting with the contracts without using Polymarket's frontend.

This is a gray area. Using a VPN to circumvent geographic restrictions violates Polymarket's terms of service. Whether it violates US law is less clear. The CFTC's enforcement action targeted Polymarket as a company, not individual users. No US trader has been prosecuted for using the platform.

That said, there are real risks:

- **Account seizure**: Polymarket can freeze accounts that it identifies as US-based
- **Withdrawal issues**: If flagged, you may have difficulty withdrawing funds
- **No legal recourse**: If something goes wrong, you have no regulatory protections
- **Evolving enforcement**: The CFTC could expand enforcement to users in the future

## The 2024 Election Changed Everything

Polymarket became a household name during the 2024 US presidential election. The platform [famously predicted Trump's victory](/blog/polymarket-2024-election-prediction) more accurately than virtually every major poll, processing hundreds of millions in election-related volume.

This visibility created a paradox. Polymarket proved that prediction markets work — that crowds putting real money on outcomes can outperform traditional forecasting. But it did so on a platform that US residents are not supposed to use.

The election performance also intensified the regulatory debate. Some in Washington pointed to Polymarket's accuracy as evidence that prediction markets should be more broadly legalized. Others argued that the massive unregulated volume demonstrated exactly why oversight is necessary.

## Is Polymarket Gambling?

This is one of the most debated questions in prediction market law. The CFTC's position is that Polymarket's contracts are swaps (financial derivatives), not gambling. This is actually important — it means they fall under financial regulation rather than state gambling laws.

If prediction market contracts were classified as gambling, each state's gambling commission would regulate them separately, creating a patchwork of rules. The CFTC's classification as financial instruments means federal regulation applies uniformly.

For [Kalshi](/blog/is-kalshi-legal), this classification is a feature — they applied for and received CFTC registration as a Designated Contract Market (DCM). For Polymarket, the same classification is what triggered enforcement, because they were offering financial derivatives without registration.

The practical difference for you as a trader: Kalshi's CFTC registration means your activity is legal financial trading. Polymarket's lack of registration means your activity exists in a regulatory gray zone.

## What About Other Countries?

Polymarket's legal status varies by jurisdiction:

- **United States**: Not available (CFTC settlement, geo-blocked)
- **United Kingdom**: Accessible, though the FCA has not issued specific guidance on crypto prediction markets
- **European Union**: Accessible in most countries, subject to MiCA crypto regulation
- **Canada**: Accessible, no specific restrictions
- **Australia**: Accessible, ASIC has not targeted prediction markets

In most countries outside the US, Polymarket operates in a largely unregulated space. This is neither explicitly legal nor illegal — there simply are not specific laws addressing crypto prediction markets in most jurisdictions.

## The Future: Will Polymarket Become Legal in the US?

There are several scenarios that could change Polymarket's US status:

**Scenario 1: Polymarket gets CFTC registration.** Polymarket could apply to become a registered DCM like Kalshi. This would require significant compliance infrastructure, KYC for all users, and ongoing CFTC oversight. The platform would need to fundamentally change its crypto-native, pseudonymous model.

**Scenario 2: Congress passes prediction market legislation.** Several bills have been introduced that would create a clearer legal framework for prediction markets. Broader legalization could create a path for platforms like Polymarket to operate in the US.

**Scenario 3: The CFTC changes its approach.** Under different leadership, the CFTC could take a lighter touch with crypto prediction markets, particularly if they are seen as providing valuable price discovery.

**Scenario 4: Nothing changes.** Polymarket continues operating internationally while US users either use Kalshi or find ways to access Polymarket through unofficial channels.
