## The Fee Difference Is Massive

This is the single most important difference, and most DraftKings users do not fully understand it.

When DraftKings offers you -110 odds on both sides of an NFL spread, they are charging you roughly 4.5% per bet. That -110/-110 line implies each side has a 52.4% chance of happening, which totals 104.8%. That extra 4.8% is the vig — what you are paying DraftKings to take your bet.

On proposition bets, same-game parlays, and exotic markets, the vig is much higher — often 10-20% or more. DraftKings makes billions in revenue from this margin.

Polymarket charges zero trading fees. When you buy a contract at $0.60, the full $0.60 goes into the market. There is no hidden spread, no vig, no platform fee. The only cost is the bid-ask spread between buyers and sellers, which on popular markets is typically 1-2%.

Over time, this difference compounds significantly. A bettor wagering $100 per week on DraftKings at an average 7% vig pays roughly $364 per year in hidden fees. The same activity on Polymarket costs essentially nothing in platform fees.

## The Exchange Model vs. The Sportsbook Model

Understanding this difference is key to understanding why the economics are so different.

**DraftKings (sportsbook model)**: You bet against the house. DraftKings sets the lines, manages risk, and profits from the vig. They are the counterparty to every bet. If everyone bets the same side, DraftKings is exposed. They manage this by moving lines to balance action. The house always has an edge built into the price.

**Polymarket (exchange model)**: You trade against other users. Polymarket is the marketplace, not the counterparty. Someone selling YES at $0.60 is matched with someone buying YES at $0.60. Polymarket takes zero cut of this transaction. It is structurally similar to a stock exchange.

This matters because it changes your expected value:

| | DraftKings | Polymarket |
|---|---|---|
| Your counterparty | The house | Other traders |
| Built-in house edge | Yes (5-10%) | No |
| Can you have +EV bets? | Possible but rare | Yes, regularly |
| Does the platform profit from your losses? | Yes | No |

On DraftKings, finding positive expected value requires beating the vig. On Polymarket, you just need to be more right than the other traders — there is no vig to overcome. This is why sharp bettors and [traders looking for edge](/blog/prediction-market-strategies-finding-edge-as-a-retail-trader) are increasingly drawn to prediction markets.

## The Hidden Comparison: Expected Value

Here is the math that matters most.

A typical DraftKings bettor needs to win **52.4%** of -110 bets just to break even. That means the vig costs you 2.4% on every standard bet.

A Polymarket trader needs to win **50%** of fair-priced bets to break even (ignoring the bid-ask spread, which is typically 1-2%).

Over 1,000 bets at $100 each:
- DraftKings at 52% win rate: **-$1,200** (below the 52.4% breakeven)
- Polymarket at 52% win rate: **+$2,000** (above the 50% breakeven)

Same win rate, opposite outcomes. The vig is not a minor detail — it is the difference between winning and losing over any meaningful sample size. This is why [understanding fees](/blog/kalshi-fees-explained) on any platform is critical before you start trading.
