## Iran Strike Markets: A Study in Time-Decay Dynamics

The most actively traded contract on [Polymarket](https://polymarket.us/1762) today was "US strikes Iran by February 23, 2026?" pulling in $3.5 million in 24-hour volume despite trading at just 0.7¢ — effectively pricing in a less than 1% probability. With February 23rd now in the past as of this writing on the 24th, this market represents pure resolution mechanics at work.

More interesting is the market structure across different expiration dates. "US strikes Iran by February 24, 2026?" trades at 3.9¢ with $1.2 million in daily volume, while the February 28th expiration shows significantly more concern at 20.5¢ with $2.3 million changing hands today. This term structure reveals how traders are pricing in escalation risk — the probability jumps from under 4% for an immediate strike to over 20% within the next four days.

The total all-time volume across these Iran markets exceeds $53 million, indicating sustained institutional and sophisticated retail interest. ## Federal Reserve Policy: The March Meeting Consensus

Two Fed-related markets tell a remarkably consistent story about monetary policy expectations. The "Will the Fed decrease interest rates by 50+ bps after the March 2026 meeting?" contract sits at 0.8¢ across an impressive $67.3 million in total volume, with $2.9 million traded in the past 24 hours. On the flip side, "Will the Fed increase interest rates by 25+ bps after the March 2026 meeting?" trades at 0.7¢ with $2.6 million in daily volume.

This pricing structure — both tail outcomes trading under 1¢ — suggests the market has coalesced around an expectation of either no change or a modest 25 basis point cut. The roughly $5.5 million in combined daily volume indicates active position management as traders await economic data releases and Fed communications in the lead-up to the March decision.

The 50 basis point decrease market's $67.3 million total volume makes it one of the highest-volume contracts currently active on the platform, demonstrating how economic policy decisions can rival political events in terms of trader interest.
